## Bounded synthesis

The exact 1700 target cannot be upgraded safely to a positive slavery claim from the
evidence recovered.

Modern archaeology strongly supports major social, settlement and agricultural change
in Ifugao between roughly 1600 and 1800, including increased status differentiation
and intensified wet-rice production around Old Kiyyangan. It does not identify an
enslaved class or slaveholding institution at 1700.

The strongest direct descriptions of Ifugao slavery/servitude are much later:
Roy F. Barton's 1919 *Ifugao Law* and 1922 *Ifugao Economics* describe practices
remembered or observed before/during the early American colonial period, including:
- children sold by impoverished parents;
- debtors entering bondage;
- captives held/sold for debt;
- people described as **himbut**;
- inheritance and alienability of some enslaved persons;
- slave dealing toward Nueva Vizcaya and Isabela.

Those materials establish that slavery-like and strongly unfree institutions existed
in at least some Ifugao communities in the recent traditional/early-colonial horizon.

They do **not** securely establish those institutions at the exact 1700 anchor.

William Henry Scott's later comparative work further cautions against treating every
Ifugao dependent status as a chattel-slave class, emphasizing propertyless/debt-peon
statuses in Ifugao and sharper purchased/captured chattel-slavery categories elsewhere
in the Cordillera. That creates a genuine category-mapping warning rather than a reason
to erase Barton's evidence.

The bounded conclusion is therefore:

**Ifugao communities at 1700 remain researched-inconclusive for slavery or a specific
servile institution. Later ethnographic evidence supports child sale, debt bondage,
captivity and slaveholding in some Ifugao communities, but its temporal reach back to
1700 and exact category mapping are unresolved.**

No P-level is assigned.

## Frame and chronology

EXP-07 qualified a multi-community Ifugao highland frame with Old Kiyyangan/Tuwali
evidence as one bounded locus.

Acabado and collaborators show:
- large social/economic shifts after c.1600;
- intensified wet-rice agriculture and settlement reorganization;
- increased status differentiation;
- continued lowland/highland interaction.

The first substantial written Spanish description of the ethnolinguistic group is much
later, around 1793, and terrace descriptions appear around 1801.

This makes exact-1700 social-status reconstruction unusually dependent on archaeology
and later retrospective ethnography.

## Barton: later traditional slavery and bondage

Barton's early twentieth-century ethnographies are direct and detailed but temporally
late.

He describes:
- poverty-driven child sale;
- slaves as valuable and transferable;
- some owners possessing strong property rights over enslaved people;
- inherited slave status/property relations in some cases;
- debt servitude;
- women/children seized in feud/debt contexts and potentially sold;
- a named category, **himbut**, in *Ifugao Economics*.

These are strong sources for the existence of serious unfreedom in at least some
Ifugao communities before/during the early American period.

They are not exact-1700 evidence.

Barton's own wording such as “before the American occupation” is too broad to justify
a two-century backward projection.

## Scott and category translation

Scott's comparative analysis of unhispanized Philippine class structures is important
because it does not simply repeat Barton's terminology.

For Ifugao, he emphasizes ranked/property-based classes, poverty/disinheritance and
debt-peon-like conditions, while reserving a clearer purchased/captured chattel-slave
class for some other Cordillera settings.

This does not prove Barton was wrong.

It does show that:
- “slave” in older ethnography may aggregate distinct statuses;
- child sale, debt bondage, household incorporation and chattel ownership must remain
  analytically separable;
- the project should preserve Barton's source-native descriptions and not force all
  dependents into one modern slavery category.

## Archaeology and exact-anchor limits

The Ifugao Archaeological Project provides the strongest exact-period evidence.

Around the seventeenth century:
- settlement patterns changed;
- wet-rice terraces intensified;
- social differentiation increased;
- political consolidation and ritual feasting became more pronounced.

None of those observations establishes slavery by itself.

Hierarchy, wealth concentration, community labor and political mobilization are not
slavery proxies.

The project therefore refuses:
> archaeological social differentiation → slavery at 1700.

## Law / practice

No exact-1700 written Ifugao legal corpus was recovered.

Later customary-law ethnography records coercive status and property relations, but
its date of observation and remembered temporal depth are insufficient to define an
exact-1700 legal regime.

## Territory / community

No single Ifugao polity or uniform community-wide practice is assigned.

Old Kiyyangan/Tuwali evidence is one local anchor within a multi-community highland
region. Later ethnography cannot be generalized automatically to every Ifugao group.

## Evidence locus and inference extent

Evidence loci:
- Old Kiyyangan and surrounding Ifugao archaeological landscape, c.1600-1800;
- Barton's early twentieth-century ethnographic fieldwork, centered largely in Ifugao;
- later comparative Cordillera class-structure scholarship.

Maximum defensible inference:

**Serious forms of unfreedom including child sale, debt bondage, captivity and
slaveholding are documented in later traditional Ifugao society, while exact-1700
presence, distribution and category mapping remain unresolved.**

Required abstention:
- no exact-1700 slavery assertion;
- no projection from early-20th-century ethnography across two centuries without
  independent evidence;
- no hierarchy/wealth → slavery inference;
- no one-category collapse of himbut, debt peon, servant, captive and slave;
- no uniform Ifugao-wide prevalence;
- no practice polygon.

## Geometry state

**regional/community frame valid; exact practice geometry unresolved.**

Old Kiyyangan may remain an archaeological evidence/navigation locus. It does not
authorize a region-wide slavery polygon.

## Source dependency and access audit

- Barton 1919 and Barton 1922 are closely related products of the same ethnographic
  programme and are one major evidence family, not two independent traditions.
- Scott 1979 is a later comparative reinterpretation useful for category control.
- Acabado/IAP archaeology is independent for exact-period social/economic change, but
  it does not provide slave-status evidence.
- No exact-1700 indigenous written record or contemporaneous Spanish slave-status
  description was recovered.
- No exhaustive Ifugao-language oral-history corpus was reconstructed.

## Research disposition

**Researched-inconclusive for exact-1700 slavery/unfree status.**

Preserve as supported later/traditional evidence:
- child sale;
- debt bondage;
- captive/debt seizure;
- slaveholding/alienability in some communities;
- the himbut category.

Do not promote as supported:
- exact-1700 slavery;
- exact-1700 chattel-slave class;
- Ifugao-wide prevalence or uniformity;
- continuity of every later status category back to 1700.

Reopen/deepen if:
- eighteenth-century Spanish or indigenous-source material identifies a bounded
  Ifugao slave/dependent category;
- oral-history/genealogical scholarship securely dates relevant institutions to the
  seventeenth/eighteenth century;
- archaeological evidence independently identifies captive/servile status.

## Adversarial findings

- **SUPPORTED LATER/TRADITIONAL:** child sale, debt bondage and slaveholding in
  Barton's Ifugao ethnography.
- **SUPPORTED EXACT-PERIOD CONTEXT:** major seventeenth/eighteenth-century social and
  agricultural transformation.
- **REJECT:** hierarchy/status differentiation proves slavery at 1700.
- **REJECT:** “before American occupation” proves continuity to 1700.
- **REJECT:** every dependent/servant category is chattel slavery.
- **UNRESOLVED:** exact-1700 presence and distribution of slavery/servitude.
- **UNRESOLVED:** temporal depth and precise juridical meaning of himbut.
- **PORTABLE CONTRACT SURVIVES:** later positive evidence can coexist with an
  exact-anchor inconclusive result.
