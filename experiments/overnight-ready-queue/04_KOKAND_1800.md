## Bounded synthesis

The research supports slavery as a real institution in the nineteenth-century Khanate
of Kokand, but the strongest target-specific evidence recovered is later than the exact
1800 anchor.

Jeff Eden's specialist synthesis of Central Asian slavery treats Kokand as part of the
regional slave system while emphasizing a critical scale distinction: compared with
Khwarazm and Bukhara, slave trading in Kokand was **strikingly small in scale**.

Concrete Kokand evidence becomes clearer in the following decades. W. H. Wathen's
1834 account, based on recent Kokandi informants, reports that:
- the khan sent Chinese slaves as diplomatic gifts to St Petersburg;
- during Kokand's intervention in the Jahangir Khoja conflict in the 1820s, Chinese
  prisoners were made slaves.

Laura Newby's specialist study of bondage on the Qing northwestern frontier uses the
same Wathen episode as evidence of Chinese slaves in early-nineteenth-century Khoqand.

These sources establish a real Kokand-specific slavery/captive-enslavement context in
the early nineteenth century.

They do **not** securely establish the same practice at exactly 1800.

The bounded conclusion is therefore:

**The Khanate of Kokand participated in slavery and captive enslavement in the early
nineteenth century, but the evidence recovered does not securely date a specific
slaveholding, slave-trading or enslavement event to the exact 1800 anchor. Exact-1800
Kokand practice remains researched-inconclusive.**

No P-level is assigned.

## Frame and chronology

EXP-07 established that the Khanate existed at 1800 in an early, Fergana-centered
phase.

Scott Levi and Encyclopaedia Iranica place the start of Alim Khan's expansion phase in
1799, but major conquests and the better-documented expansionary state belong mainly
to the following decade.

This packet therefore excludes:
- the late-1830s territorial maximum;
- later nineteenth-century Kokand institutions as if they were unchanged in 1800;
- evidence from Russian conquest-period Kokand;
- Bukhara/Khiva slave systems as substitutes for Kokand evidence.

## Slavery existed in Kokand, but at smaller scale

Eden's 2018 specialist monograph is the main interpretive control.

Its regional comparison is important:
- Central Asian slavery was widespread and institutionally meaningful in the
  eighteenth and nineteenth centuries;
- forms and scale differed sharply by polity and region;
- the trade thrived in Khwarazm and Bukhara;
- in Kokand it was strikingly smaller in scale.

This supports a Kokand-specific slavery context while directly blocking an inference
from the much denser Bukhara/Khiva archive to Kokand prevalence.

The book's principal detailed empirical geography centers on Khwarazm, Bukhara,
Khurasan and the Russian-Kazakh frontier, so its Kokand statement is used as a
high-level specialist classification/scale constraint, not as an exact-1800 local
attestation.

## Wathen's early-nineteenth-century evidence

Wathen's 1834 *Memoir on the U'sbek State of Kokan* records information obtained from
Kokandi travelers.

He reports two relevant episodes:
1. a recent Kokand embassy to St Petersburg carrying an elephant and Chinese slaves as
   gifts;
2. a Kokandi campaign in Chinese Turkistan during the Jahangir Khoja conflict in which
   Chinese prisoners were made slaves.

These observations are strong for:
- captive-to-slave conversion in Kokand's early nineteenth-century military sphere;
- enslaved people as alienable diplomatic property;
- a Kokand-specific connection to Qing-frontier enslavement.

They are not exact-1800 evidence:
- the Jahangir conflict belongs to the 1820s;
- the St Petersburg gift belongs to the years immediately preceding Wathen's 1834
  report;
- Wathen himself was reporting informants rather than observing the events directly.

## Newby and Qing-frontier bondage

Laura Newby's 2013 specialist article on Qing northwestern bondage independently
situates the Chinese-slave episode within the broader frontier system.

Newby cites the Kokand-to-Russia gift of Chinese slaves as an example of early
nineteenth-century Khoqand bondage.

This strengthens the interpretation of Wathen's terminology and frontier context.

It does not create a second independent event: the specific diplomatic-gift episode
still derives from Wathen and remains one evidence chain.

## Alim Khan and captive-taking boundary

Kokand's expansion under Alim Khan began near the anchor, and later campaign narratives
describe prisoners taken in warfare.

Prisoner-taking is not automatically slavery.

Likewise, later descriptions of Alim Khan's Ghalcha/Pamiri military recruitment are
not treated here as proof of an exact-1800 slave army. Encyclopaedia Iranica describes
the force as Tajik conscripts; other later accounts use stronger coercion/slavery
language. The status, recruitment mechanism and exact date require separate source
reconstruction before any slave-soldier claim is admitted.

This packet therefore refuses:
> captive / conscript / dependent soldier = slave by default.

## Law / practice

Kokand was a Sunni Hanafi polity, but general Hanafi slave law is not used to create an
exact territorial-practice claim.

Eden demonstrates that Hanafi legal norms structured slavery elsewhere in Muslim
Central Asia, but the exact 1800 Kokand legal record was not recovered here.

No exact-anchor Kokand sale deed, manumission deed, court case or slave-market record
was located in this pass.

## Network / territory

The later Kokand evidence includes:
- enslavement of Chinese captives;
- movement of enslaved Chinese persons as diplomatic gifts;
- participation in a broader Central Asian slavery environment.

These are bounded network/political-participation facts.

They do not establish:
- slave trading throughout the Fergana Valley at 1800;
- a large market;
- uniform household slaveholding;
- territorial prevalence.

Bukhara and Khiva evidence is explicitly excluded as territorial substitution.

## Evidence locus and inference extent

Evidence loci:
- early Fergana-centered Khanate frame at 1800;
- early-nineteenth-century Kokand/Qing frontier campaigns;
- Kokand diplomatic exchange with Russia;

Maximum defensible inference:

**Kokand was part of the nineteenth-century Central Asian slavery system and
participated in captive enslavement and transfer of enslaved people, but the verified
target-specific evidence recovered is later than 1800 and the institution was much
smaller in scale than in Bukhara/Khwarazm.**

Required abstention:
- no exact-1800 positive territorial-practice claim;
- no Bukhara/Khiva → Kokand transfer;

## Research disposition

**Researched-inconclusive for exact-1800 Kokand slavery/slave-trade practice.**

Preserve as supported later/near-period evidence:
- Kokand-specific slavery existed in the early nineteenth century;
- captive Chinese were enslaved during the 1820s conflict;
- Chinese enslaved persons could be transferred as diplomatic gifts;
- specialist synthesis characterizes Kokand's slave trade as small relative to
  Bukhara/Khwarazm.

Do not promote as supported:
- exact-1800 slaveholding/trade;
- a large or structurally dominant slave economy;
- slave status for Alim Khan's conscript troops;
- polity-wide prevalence.

Reopen/deepen if:
- late-eighteenth/very-early-nineteenth-century Kokand court, legal, fiscal or narrative
  sources document slave purchase, ownership, manumission or captive enslavement;
- specialist work dates the relevant Kokand slave institution more precisely to the
  1790s/1800 horizon;
- source-native evidence resolves the status of Alim Khan's Pamiri/Ghalcha troops.

## Adversarial findings

- **SUPPORTED BROADER PERIOD:** slavery/captive enslavement in early-nineteenth-century
  Kokand.
- **SUPPORTED SCALE CONTROL:** Kokand slave trading was substantially smaller than in
  Bukhara/Khwarazm.
- **SUPPORTED LATER EVENT:** Chinese prisoners were enslaved in the 1820s Kokand/Qing
  conflict tradition reported by Wathen.
- **REJECT:** regional Central Asian slavery proves exact-1800 Kokand practice.
- **REJECT:** Bukhara/Khiva evidence can substitute for Kokand.
- **REJECT:** prisoner or conscript automatically means slave.
- **UNRESOLVED:** exact-1800 territorial occurrence and legal institution.
- **UNRESOLVED:** exact status of Alim Khan's Pamiri/Ghalcha troops.
- **PORTABLE CONTRACT SURVIVES:** a strong broader-period positive can coexist with an
  exact-anchor inconclusive result.
