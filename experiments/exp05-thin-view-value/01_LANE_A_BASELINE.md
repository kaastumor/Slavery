## T1 — temporal + spatial truth

### Sumerian City-States — 2000 BCE

The strongest positive evidence is **near-anchor**, not an exact 2000 BCE observation.
Late Ur III evidence immediately precedes the anchor, while the political frame crosses
the Ur III → Isin-Larsa transition. The safest representation is therefore a
**polity aggregate / multiple evidence loci**, not one sovereign “Sumerian” state.

Do not fill a single “Sumerian slavery territory.” Ur, Umma, Garshana, Nippur and
Irisagrig are evidence loci whose historical jurisdictions differ.

Useful provenance recovery from the source notes:
- SUM-ADAMS-2010 for late-Ur-III institutional/context synthesis;
- SUM-NEUMANN-2011 for private-household slavery and prevalence limits;
- SUM-CDLI-P136342 / SUM-CDLI-P101616 as bounded sale records;
- transition chronology is contextual rather than an exact-year event.

### Cuzco — 1300 CE

Cuzco at 1300 is an **incipient/local Killke-period polity/node**. The mature Inca
institutions commonly used to discuss yana/yanacona, mita and mitimaes are later, and
the written record is colonial/retrospective.

The safest representation is a Cuzco-area node/incipient-polity frame with the labor
status question still under review. Do not back-project a later Tawantinsuyu/Cuzco
imperial polygon or later imperial dependency system into 1300.

Useful provenance:
- CUZ-BAUER-COVEY-2004 and CUZ-COVEY-2006 for state-formation chronology/frame;
- CUZ-NOACK-NOWACK-2023 for retrospective-source and yana-category limits;
- CUZ-HU-QUAVE-2020 is later imperial comparison only.

### Angkor — 1200 CE

K.273 (Ta Prohm, 1186) and K.908 (Preah Khan, 1191) are strong **near-anchor**
institutional records, but not documents dated exactly to 1200.

The safest representation is an **Angkor node/site plus linked institutional/
provisioning networks**. Do not turn Angkor's rich temple epigraphy into an
empire-wide Khmer slavery polygon.

Useful provenance:
- ANG-K273 and ANG-K908 are distinct near-anchor inscriptions;
- ANG-LUSTIG-LUSTIG-2013 supplies wider category/status interpretation;
- later numerical/spatial studies that reuse those inscriptions do not multiply the
  underlying source events.

## T2 — external/network versus territorial practice

### Indus Valley Civilization — 2000 BCE

There is positive **external/network association**: P453801 concerns people identified
as Meluhhans in Mesopotamia, and Laursen–Steinkeller give a text-specific royal-donated-
slave interpretation.

That does **not** establish local Indus enslavement, territorial slavery, recurrence or
prevalence. Acquisition geography and route into Babylonia remain unknown. The
territorial Indus question therefore remains **under review**.

Key dependency trap:
P453801, CDLI readings, Harappa reproduction and Laursen–Steinkeller discussion are one
artifact/citation chain, not multiple independent attestations.

### Viking trade network — 900 CE

Here the **network itself is the legitimate positive frame**. Near-anchor evidence
supports capture, transport and sale within parts of the Viking/Rūs network. Western
sources independently support extensive captive-taking.

This positive network result does not become one Viking territorial practice polygon.
Nor does “captive” automatically mean “sold slave”; ransom and other outcomes existed.

### Andaman Islands communities — 1800 CE

British colonial **external captivity/removal** of Andamanese people is positively
supported near the anchor.

Indigenous Andamanese internal slavery/servile practice remains **unassessed**.
Colonial kidnapping cannot establish indigenous slavery, while retrospective
ethnographic silence cannot establish absence.

Later penal-colony/Andaman Home systems cannot be backdated into 1800.

## T3 — category + dependency negative control

### Sumer

Category boundary:
- `geme₂` is context-dependent and can denote a free worker in Garshana;
- `un-il₂` is treated in current specialist work as legally free/serflike, not slave.

Dependency/counting trap:
- CDLI artifact + edition/lexical discussion does not create multiple events;
- repeated Ur III sale texts/archive density cannot establish population prevalence or
  slavery as the dominant labor mode.

### Angkor

Category boundary:
- `khñum` cannot be universally normalized to slave;
- specific property/bondage contexts can support slavery while other temple personnel
  require broader dependency/labor labels.

Dependency/counting trap:
- K.273/K.908 personnel totals are not slave totals;
- analyses reusing K.273 or K.908 remain dependent on those inscription chains.

### Ethiopian Empire

Category boundary:
- `gabr/gäbbar`, servant and tributary language is context-sensitive and cannot be
  normalized automatically to slave.

Dependency/counting trap:
- Pankhurst's use of Bruce does not create another independent attestation of Bruce's
  observations;
- traveler, market and export counts do not establish empire-wide prevalence,
  especially under fragmented Zemene Mesafint sovereignty.
