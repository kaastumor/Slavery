# EXP-04 — dependency, category and access audit

State: integrated audit for issue #213. Experimental only; canonical v0.6.1 unchanged.

This audit asks whether the D-089 portable evidence contract preserved the material
historical distinctions encountered across all eight frozen cases. It does **not**
reclassify unresolved historical cases, assign P-levels, or turn internal review into
independent historical review.

## 2. Source-dependency audit

### Indus

CDLI P453801 / Nisaba 15 371, its old/current CDLI readings, the Harappa reproduction
and Laursen–Steinkeller's discussion are one artifact/citation chain. The stronger
“royal donated slaves” interpretation and CDLI's lexical rendering are interpretive
layers on that same record, not independent attestations.

Bartash's broader a-ru-a work is a separate category/status synthesis, not another
attestation of Nanaza or Samar.

### Sumer

The Sumer packet deliberately does not reuse P453801 as another positive event.
Bartash's a-ru-a analysis is shared conceptual/category context across Indus and Sumer.

Garshana records synthesized by Adams, and individual Ur III sale/court texts discussed
by later scholars, are not multiplied when one underlying artifact or archive is cited
by several publications. CDLI entries remain source-native artifact layers; lexical
resources such as ePSD2 provide mapping context, not new historical events.

### Hadhramaut

The AIBL Hadrān presentation and Bron's review concern the same 458-inscription corpus.
Bron is independently useful for genre/dating criticism, but does not create another
inscription set. Frantsouzoff's later Raybūn synthesis overlaps the 2001/2007 corpora.

The 2026 onomastic index is a navigation/reference layer over Hadramitic material, not
a separate historical corpus. Grasso's South Arabian synthesis is independent
scholarship but its non-Hadramitic examples do not become Hadhramaut attestations.

### Cuzco

Bauer/Covey and Covey synthesize overlapping Cuzco archaeological traditions and are
used for frame/chronology rather than counted as independent labor-status evidence.

Noack/Nowack's pre-Inca yana argument is a specialist reconstruction from later
colonial source traditions. Colonial chronicles quoted inside modern scholarship are
not separately counted because they were not independently re-edited in this pass.

Hu/Quave provide a genuinely different archaeological evidence class, but it belongs
to the later imperial period and therefore functions as a comparator rather than an
anchor attestation.

### Viking network

Modern translations and later quotations of Ibn Rustah are one primary-source chain.
Raffield and Jankowiak reuse Arabic sources, western annals and archaeological/
numismatic evidence in different syntheses; those syntheses are not mechanically
counted as independent events.

Western annal traditions can independently support ninth-century captive-taking.
Ibn Fadlan is a separate succeeding-period source but remains downstream of the 900
anchor.

### Andaman communities

Anderson's publications share parts of a colonial archival research programme and are
not treated as two independent 1789/1790 events. The 1790 Calcutta-removal chronology
is retained as a specific recovery lead until its source-native archival chain is
independently verified.

Radcliffe-Brown is a different ethnographic evidence class, but is retrospective and
post-disruption; it constrains what may be inferred about indigenous social structure
rather than providing an absence finding.

### Angkor

K.273 (Ta Prohm) and K.908 (Preah Khan) are distinct near-anchor inscriptions from the
same reign and temple-administrative milieu. Modern numerical, archaeological and
historical analyses that reuse either inscription remain dependent for the underlying
personnel totals.

Lustig/Lustig's broad inscriptional synthesis is independently useful for category
interpretation. The history of translating khñum/personnel lists as “slaves” is itself
part of the evidence problem: later scholarship questions a universal slave rendering,
so translation convention is not counted as an independent status attestation.

### Ethiopian Empire

Bruce's travel account is one near-anchor source chain. Pankhurst's use of Bruce does
not create a second attestation of those same events.

Fernyhough and broader modern syntheses provide independent regional/structural
interpretations but much southern nineteenth-century evidence is spatially or
chronologically outside the 1800 frozen imperial frame.

Fetha Nagast and the Gälawdéwos edict form earlier legal-context chains; they establish
a legal tradition, not near-anchor practice.

## 3. Historical-category audit

The tranche repeatedly demonstrates that a generic “dependent = slave” mapping would
create false inference.

| Case | Material category boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Indus | a-ru-a cannot be universally normalized to slave; text-specific interpretation and broader dependent status both survive |
| Sumer | geme₂ is context-dependent; un-il₂ is legally free/serflike in current specialist analysis; a-ru-a, captives and hired labor remain distinct |
| Hadhramaut | ʿbd, ʾdm and translated “servant” are context-sensitive; regional South Arabian proprietary/captive evidence cannot define every Hadramitic term |
| Cuzco | yana/yanacona is strong asymmetric dependency but not a simple slave equivalent; mita, mitimaes and camayoc are separate institutions/statuses |
| Viking | captive/prisoner is not automatically enslaved/sold; Saqaliba and Rūs are source categories, not precise modern nationalities |
| Andaman | kidnap/confinement, penal convict labor, colonial dependency and indigenous internal status are separate dimensions |
| Angkor | khñum/personnel terminology is heterogeneous; specific property/bondage contexts can support slavery while temple headcounts cannot |
| Ethiopia | barya/gabr/gäbbar/servant/tributary vocabulary needs source-specific mapping; tributary dependency is not automatically chattel slavery |

D-089's historical terms, category mapping status/note, source evidence role and
claim fitness were materially used in every case. They were not decorative fields.

## 4. Temporal and frame audit

The sample stressed several non-equivalent frame/temporal problems:

- **Sumer:** polity aggregate across the Ur III → Isin-Larsa transition;
- **Indus:** civilization-region rather than one sovereign polity;
- **Hadhramaut:** polity with early-corpus dating uncertainty;
- **Cuzco:** incipient/local polity before mature Tawantinsuyu;
- **Viking:** mobile network rather than territory;
- **Andaman:** multiple communities plus external colonial actors;
- **Angkor:** node/site with institutional networks beyond monument boundaries;
- **Ethiopia:** persistent imperial title under fragmented effective sovereignty.

The existing frame, temporal, bounded-proposition, abstention, evidence-locus,
inference-extent, network/territorial and geometry fields preserved these distinctions
without changing the frozen target identities.

## 5. Law / practice audit

No case required law to stand in for practice.

- Sumer/Ethiopia use older legal traditions as context while retaining separate
  transaction/travel/practice evidence.
- Hadhramaut/Cuzco do not manufacture legal regimes from later or regional material.
- Viking is primarily a practice/network case with no single governing law.
- Andaman keeps colonial legal/institutional practice separate from indigenous social
  organization.
- Angkor's foundation inscriptions are administrative/practice evidence, not treated
  as one comprehensive slave code.
- Indus remains legally unassessed.

The law/practice note remains sufficient for the material distinction found here.

## 6. Network / territorial audit

This was the strongest repeated scope pressure.

- Indus: Meluhhan association abroad does not establish Indus territorial practice.
- Hadhramaut: South Arabian regional evidence does not transfer to Hadhramaut.
- Viking: the **network itself** is the legitimate positive frame; no territorial
  recoloring is needed.
- Andaman: colonial external captivity is positive while indigenous internal practice
  remains unassessed.
- Ethiopia: domestic practice and long-distance export routes coexist under fragmented
  sovereignty.
- Angkor: linked provisioning/captive networks do not imply empire-wide status.
- Cuzco: later imperial relocation networks cannot back-project to the 1300 node.

The hardest case is Andaman because one target contains a supported external dimension
and an unassessed internal one simultaneously. The current contract preserves this in
the bounded proposition, inference extent, network/territorial note, required
abstention and compound research outcome. That is sufficient for **preservation** in
EXP-04, though a future requirement for machine filtering by each dimension could
justify a separate representation experiment.

## 7. Access / language audit

### Blocking or reopening-trigger access

- **Indus:** direct Laursen–Steinkeller 2017 pp. 83–84 remains unread; exact lawful
  recovery trigger retained.
- **Hadhramaut:** full/searchable Hadrān and Kafas corpora, *East of Aden*
  social-structure treatment and 2026 Hadramitic index/glossary remain key recovery
  sources.
- **Cuzco:** the main limitation is not merely paywall access; it is the shortage of
  contemporary c. 1300 status evidence. Colonial sources are intrinsically
  retrospective.

### Non-blocking limitations

- Viking: translated Arabic/annal material and specialist syntheses support the bounded
  network conclusion, but Arabic/Old Norse were not independently edited.
- Andaman: specialist colonial history supports the general external-coercion claim;
  the specific 1790 archival event needs source-native verification before canonical
  event promotion.
- Angkor: published editions/syntheses are sufficient for the bounded institutional
  result; Old Khmer/Sanskrit were not independently re-edited.
- Ethiopia: near-anchor traveler evidence and specialist synthesis are sufficient for
  bounded regional presence; Geʿez/Amharic legal manuscripts were not independently
  re-edited.
- Sumer: published edited texts/specialist scholarship support the bounded conclusion;
  no independent Assyriological translation was performed.

Access limitation is therefore not treated uniformly as a blocker. It blocks only the
claims for which unavailable material could materially change the permitted conclusion.

## 8. Coverage and archive-density audit

The tranche spans radically unequal evidence environments: huge Ur III and Angkorian
epigraphic corpora, retrospective Cuzco/Andaman evidence, fragmentary Hadramitic
epigraphy, external Viking narrative sources and mixed Ethiopian travel/legal records.

No classification was derived from:
- number of surviving tablets/inscriptions;
- number of citations;
- market/coin counts;
- temple personnel totals;
- number of archival references;
- absence of a term in a genre-limited corpus.

This is a substantive success condition, not merely a style rule.

## 9. Audit disposition

**No D-089 field extension is required by this audit.**

All material distinctions encountered can be preserved by the existing portable
contract plus the explicitly experimental research-stage serialization already used
by EXP-04.

This is a method/representation finding only. It does not mean:
- every historical target is resolved;
- the packet has independent scholarly validation;
- the fields are optimal for every future query or UI;
- a future scale/mixed-geometry corpus cannot expose another missing distinction.

Reopening condition for the contract: a future real case or task must show a material
distinction that cannot be represented without either strengthening/losing the claim or
burying it in prose so deeply that the portable representation itself creates a false
inference.
