# EXP-04 case 7 — Angkor, 1200 CE

**Target:** R1:N:angkor_1200
**Frame:** node_site
**State:** experimental research packet

**Canonical effect:** none

## Bounded proposition

Angkorian inscriptions close to the 1200 anchor document large, organized groups of temple and land personnel, including people described with khñum terminology and attached to religious foundations, lands and villages.

Specialist scholarship has often translated khñum as “slave” or “bondsman,” but the equivalence is explicitly disputed. Some listed people held specialized religious, administrative, musical or service roles and could receive maintenance or exemptions.

The safe claim is therefore that institutionally dependent temple/estate labour is strongly supported around the late twelfth century, while a universal chattel-slavery mapping for all khñum is not.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- translate every khñum automatically as chattel slave;
- infer the status of all Angkor residents from temple personnel lists;
- infer empire-wide prevalence from inscriptions at religious foundations;
- treat named personnel in temple records as proof of uniformly abject or property status;
- collapse corvée, attached village labour, temple personnel, bondspeople and slavery.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus:** Angkorian/Khmer inscriptions recording temple personnel and attached labour, including late-twelfth-century texts close to the anchor.

**Maximum inference:** dependent personnel and attached labour were institutional features of Angkorian temple/estate organization; exact slavery classification varies by term/context.

## Historical terminology / mapping

Key term: khñum / kñum, including khñum vrah in some inscriptions.

**Mapping status:** disputed / approximate_historically_specific.

## Counter/limiting evidence

The terminology dispute is material, not cosmetic. Scholars have argued that temple personnel recorded as property of a deity/foundation could also hold roles and privileges inconsistent with a simplistic slave/free binary.
