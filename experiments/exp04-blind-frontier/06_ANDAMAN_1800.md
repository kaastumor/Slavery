## Bounded proposition

British/colonial kidnapping and confinement of Andamanese islanders is directly documented from the late eighteenth century onward and therefore overlaps the 1800 anchor.

That evidence concerns coercion imposed by external colonizing actors. It does **not** establish an indigenous Andamanese institution of slavery or servile dependency among the multiple island communities.

The internal territorial-practice question therefore remains researched-inconclusive in this pass.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- turn the enslavement/kidnapping/confinement of Andamanese people by outsiders into a claim that Andamanese communities practiced slavery internally;
- treat “Andamanese” as one homogeneous society;
- infer absence of internal slavery from later ethnographic descriptions that do not report it;
- equate the colonial penal colony, convict labour, indigenous captivity and precolonial community institutions;
- project nineteenth/twentieth-century community amalgamation backward to 1800.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus for positive coercion:** colonial encounters around the Andaman Islands, including British kidnapping and confinement from 1771–1864.

**Maximum inference:** Andamanese people were subjected to externally imposed captivity around the anchor period; internal territorial practice remains unknown.

## Source roles

Role: specialist colonial-contact history. Strong for British kidnapping/confinement; unfit to establish an indigenous institution.
- A. R. Radcliffe-Brown, The Andaman Islanders (1922). Role: later ethnographic reconstruction of social organization. Useful for community distinctions and colonial transformation; chronologically later and not treated as conclusive absence evidence.
- Library of Congress digitized 1922 edition. Role: exact accessible source version for the ethnographic work.

## Counter/limiting evidence

The later ethnography describes small autonomous local communities and major disruption after European occupation. Its failure to present an internal slavery institution cannot safely prove that none existed at 1800.

This is exactly an unknown-versus-absence safeguard.
