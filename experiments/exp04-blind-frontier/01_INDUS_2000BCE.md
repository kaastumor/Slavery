## Bounded synthesis so far

A positive slavery interpretation exists in the literature and must be retained.
The presently recovered materials do not yet justify an atlas designation of
civilization-wide territorial practice at 2000 BCE. This is an unfinished review,
not a completed inconclusive result, an absence claim, or an automatic dispute label.

Kenoyer 2011, Table 1 (printed p. 4), places the Harappan phase at 2600–1900 BCE;
Table 2 (p. 5) places Harappa period 3C around 2200–1900 BCE. These support an
approximate archaeological-period frame, not exact-year slavery applicability.
His discussion of seals (pp. 9–10) interprets elite power, unlike Green's reconstruction.

Habib 2002, pp. 49 and 58, reports enslaved people among Meluhha's exports and
uses that alongside domestic architecture to argue for substantial slavery.
Preserve both the positive interpretation and its evidentiary route. His domestic
passage (p. 40) also allows joint-family accommodation as an alternative to household
servants/slaves. Network export and house layout cannot silently become a uniform
territorial institution or a prevalence estimate in the atlas.

Green 2020/2021 challenges assumptions that Indus urban complexity required a
coercive ruling class. His discussion of Harappa's Mound F questions the granary
identification. This limits an architectural inference; it does not establish the
absence of slavery or directly refute every reported Meluhha transaction.

Rao 2018 proposes a rationing interpretation for Harappan tablets by comparison
with Near Eastern accounting (pp. 518–521). It is a hypothesis, not a deciphered
slave-status record. The publication reproduces archaeological objects, but the
packet has not independently inspected their excavation catalogue/context.

## Recovered textual lead and next check

Harappa.com's scholarly-excerpt page points to Laursen and Steinkeller 2017,
pp. 83–84, and **Nisaba 15 371:1–7**, an Urusagrig tablet described as mentioning
Meluhhans in royal servile status. The page reports a Shu-Suen year-6 date and
explicitly leaves the acquisition route uncertain. This is an edition/record lead,
not an independently inspected tablet or independently read full book.

Follow-up recovered **CDLI P453801**, including revision **2249158**
(2015-09-15) and the current artifact page (accessed 2026-09-24). CDLI supplies
an administrative text, a Meluhha designation, and `a-ru-a lugal`, translated
there as “royal offerings”. The specialist excerpt instead interprets donated
royal slaves. These are different translation/interpretation layers, not two
independent attestations. Current CDLI metadata gives Šū-Suen.06.12.00; no
absolute-year conversion is imposed here. No tablet image is available there.

The current reading `na-na-sa3` differs from the older revision's `na-na-za`.
Preserve both versions rather than silently replacing the older spelling or
inferring identity from either name. Next read the full specialist discussion of
the status and acquisition route, and compare the edition. The current evidence
locates the record in Mesopotamia, not an Indus enslavement event. Neither a
Meluhha designation nor the date establishes exact 2000 BCE territorial applicability.
