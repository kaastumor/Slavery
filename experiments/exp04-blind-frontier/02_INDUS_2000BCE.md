## Bounded proposition

Current reviewed evidence in this pass does **not** support a defensible slavery classification for the Indus Civilization at 2000 BCE.

This is not evidence of absence.

Specialist archaeology identifies social differentiation and unequal vulnerability in some contexts, while other work stresses the unusual lack of clear evidence for a ruling class. None of those findings by themselves establishes slavery. The script remains undeciphered, sharply limiting direct institutional/status evidence.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- turn “no current evidence of slavery” into “slavery was absent”;
- infer slavery from social stratification, violence, craft specialization or labour organization alone;
- infer freedom/equality from the lack of palaces or elite burials;
- use speculative readings of the undeciphered script as direct status evidence;
- compare archive density mechanically with Mesopotamia.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus:** Harappan/Indus urban archaeology, especially Harappa and Mohenjo-daro.

**Maximum inference:** social differentiation and organized labour existed; a specific slavery/servile status cannot currently be established from the reviewed evidence.

## Source roles

Role: specialist debate on inequality; explicitly warns that a ruling class comparable to other early states has not been demonstrated.
- Gwen Robbins Schug, “A Hierarchy of Values…,” in Bones of Complexity (2017), DOI 10.5744/florida/9780813062235.003.0010. Role: bioarchaeological evidence for vertical differentiation and unequal vulnerability; not slavery evidence.
- Jonathan Mark Kenoyer and Gwen Robbins Schug, Harappa expert Q&A “Is there any indication of slavery or ritual human sacrifice in the IVC?” Role: explicit research-limit statement: no evidence currently establishes slavery, but the evidence also cannot determine that slaves did not exist.
- Coningham & Manuel, “Priest-Kings or Puritans? Childe and Willing Subordination in the Indus,” European Journal of Archaeology 12 (2009), DOI 10.1177/1461957109339691. Role: historiographic control showing older interpretations imported assumptions about slavery/subordination.

## Counter/limiting evidence

The strongest result is methodological: inequality, coercion and slavery are not interchangeable concepts. Archaeological traces can support the first without establishing the last.
