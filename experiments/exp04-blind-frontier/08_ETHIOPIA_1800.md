# EXP-04 case 8 — Ethiopian Empire, 1800 CE

**Target:** R1:P:1800:C:r1
**Frame:** imperial_title_fragmented_sovereignty
**State:** experimental research packet
**Research outcome:** regional_slavery_supported; imperial_uniformity_not_established
**Canonical effect:** none

## Bounded proposition

Specialist scholarship supports slavery, slave raiding, slaveholding and long-distance slave trade as persistent features of societies and polities in the Ethiopian–Eritrean region into the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries.

At the 1800 anchor, however, the Zemene Mesafint was characterized by fragmented sovereignty and competing regional powers. The label “Ethiopian Empire” therefore cannot be treated as one uniformly governed territory with one practice intensity.

The safe result is regional/institutional positive evidence with explicit non-uniformity at the imperial-title frame.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- treat the emperor’s title as evidence of effective uniform control;
- extrapolate Gondar/highland, southwestern raiding, Red Sea export or Shoan practices to every territory under nominal imperial identity;
- use later nineteenth-century export counts as prevalence at 1800;
- infer one legal/practice regime across Christian, Muslim and other polities/communities;
- recolor the later Ethiopian state boundary as the 1800 historical jurisdiction.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus:** northern/central highlands, Gondar and regional slave-raiding/trade systems; wider Ethiopian–Eritrean region in specialist synthesis.

**Maximum inference:** slavery and slave trade were established regional institutions around the temporal neighborhood of 1800; spatial intensity/uniformity across the fragmented imperial frame is not established.

## Historical terminology / mapping

Multiple Ethiopian legal, social and ethnolinguistic traditions are involved; this packet does not normalize them into one local status term.

**Mapping status:** mixed / requires regionalization.

## Source roles

- Giulia Bonacci & Alexander Meckelburg, “Slavery and the Slave Trade in Ethiopia and Eritrea,” Oxford Research Encyclopedia of African History (2023), DOI 10.1093/acrefore/9780190277734.013.836. Role: specialist regional synthesis; strong for persistence and regional diversity, not uniform 1800 polity coverage.
- Richard J. Reid, “States of Violence, to c.1870,” in Frontiers of Violence in North-East Africa (2011), DOI 10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199211883.003.0004. Role: political-context source for Zemene Mesafint fragmentation, c.1770–mid-19th century.
- Richard Pankhurst, A Social History of Ethiopia (1992), chapter on slaves. Role: historical synthesis using Gondarine/eighteenth-century evidence for slave raiding and slave troops.
- Timothy Fernyhough, “Slavery and the Slave Trade in Southern Ethiopia in the 19th Century.” Role: regional history; notes reduced large-scale central-state involvement during Zemene Mesafint while slaving relations remained part of regional political economies.

## Counter/limiting evidence

Political fragmentation is itself a limiting condition. A positive regional institution does not imply uniform practice in every nominal imperial territory, and changing state involvement cannot be read as disappearance of slavery.
