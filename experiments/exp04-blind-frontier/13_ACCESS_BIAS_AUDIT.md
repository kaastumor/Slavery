# EXP-04 — Source Access and Archive-Density Bias Audit

## Question

Are the outcomes driven by historical evidence, or by what is easiest to search and digitize?

## Result

The tranche contains several distinct evidence ceilings and archive biases. They materially affect coverage confidence but do not justify changing the historical outcome mechanically.

## Observed access regimes

### Dense administrative archive — Ur III
Thousands of surviving administrative records create exceptional visibility.
Control: do not treat document abundance as prevalence. Specialist work itself shows many labour statuses and limits on the enslaved share.

### Archaeology with undeciphered text — Indus
Material evidence is substantial while direct textual status evidence is unavailable.
Control: researched-inconclusive, not absent and not egalitarian-therefore-no-slavery.

### Specialist epigraphic corpus not exhaustively queried — Hadhramaut
A target-specific corpus exists, but this run did not independently search/re-edit all inscriptions.
Control: target-specific RI plus an explicit recovery opportunity; do not substitute regional positives.

### Archaeology + later ethnohistory — Cuzco
The temporal mismatch is structural, not merely search failure.
Control: later categories remain context until an anchor-period bridge exists.

### Multi-lingual distributed evidence — Viking network
Evidence is spread across Arabic, Latin/annalistic, Old Norse and archaeological traditions.
Control: network claim only; no source-volume-driven territorial coloring.

### Colonial archive dominance — Andaman
Outsiders documented kidnapping and observation of islanders far more than indigenous internal institutions.
Control: external victimization is positive; internal practice remains unknown. Colonial silence cannot prove absence.

### Epigraphic elite/institutional archive — Angkor
Temple inscriptions disproportionately expose institutional personnel.
Control: strong temple/estate dependency claim, no automatic whole-population prevalence.

### Regional syntheses under fragmented sovereignty — Ethiopia
Sources vary strongly by place and political actor.
Control: preserve regional positive evidence while withholding one imperial-wide state.

## New risk identified

researched_inconclusive can conceal two very different situations:

1. **evidence ceiling** — the surviving/source system currently cannot resolve the question well, as in Indus;
2. **recoverable research gap** — relevant source material likely exists but was not exhaustively queried in this tranche, as in Hadhramaut.

This distinction matters for research scheduling because it changes expected information gain.

It is not yet shown to be necessary for public historical interpretation if the access limitation is preserved.

## Disposition

**No D-089 portable-core extension yet.**

Open the distinction as the next research hypothesis rather than immediately creating another canonical field.
