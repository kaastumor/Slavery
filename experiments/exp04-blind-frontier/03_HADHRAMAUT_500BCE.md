# EXP-04 case 3 — Hadhramaut, 500 BCE

**Target:** R1:L:COV2:-500:C
**Frame:** polity
**State:** experimental research packet
**Research outcome:** researched_inconclusive_target_specific; regional_context_positive
**Canonical effect:** none

## Bounded proposition

Ancient South Arabian epigraphy demonstrates enslaved people, captives, proprietary relations and multiple subordinate-status terms during the first millennium BCE.

However, this bounded pass did **not** recover sufficiently target-specific Ḥaḍramitic evidence at or near 500 BCE to transfer that regional result directly to Hadhramaut.

Therefore Hadhramaut c. 500 BCE remains researched-inconclusive for territorial practice in this experiment, despite a positive wider South-Arabian context.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- infer Hadhramaut practice from Sabaean/Qatabanian/Minaean evidence merely because they belong to South Arabia;
- treat every occurrence of ʿbd as an enslaved person: the term could denote religious or human subordination;
- use later Ḥaḍramitic evidence from the Common Era to fill a 500 BCE gap;
- infer absence from the lack of a target-specific hit in this bounded pass.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Regional evidence locus:** ancient South Arabian epigraphic corpus.

**Target-specific corpus:** Raybūn in inland Hadramawt preserves hundreds of Ḥaḍramitic inscriptions spanning roughly the fifth to first centuries BCE, but the sources retrieved in this pass did not yield a sufficiently explicit c. 500 BCE enslaved-status proposition.

**Maximum inference:** slavery/captivity is securely attested in the wider cultural region; territorial practice in Hadhramaut at the anchor is not yet established.

## Historical terminology / mapping

South Arabian terms include ʿbd, ʾmt, s¹bym, ḫmrtm, hgn, ḥr/ʾḥrr.

**Mapping status:** term-specific and context-dependent.

## Source roles

- Valentina Grasso, “Slavery in First Millennium Arabia: Epigraphy and the Qurʾān,” Millennium (2023), DOI 10.1515/mill-2023-0005. Role: specialist comparative epigraphic synthesis. Strong for South-Arabian slavery/captivity and semantic ambiguity; not by itself a Hadhramaut-500-BCE attestation.
- S. Frantsouzoff, Raybun. Hadran, temple de la déesse Atharum/Astarum, Inventaire des inscriptions sudarabiques V (2001). Role: target-specific corpus identity; 458 Ḥaḍramitic inscriptions dated fifth–first centuries BCE. This establishes researchable source coverage, not a slavery result.
- British Museum RES 4700 from Shabwa. Role: target-specific Ḥaḍramitic/Sabaean “servant” relation, but dating/semantics are insufficient for the anchor classification.

## Counter/limiting evidence

Grasso explicitly shows that ʿbd can denote a servant/subordinate rather than an enslaved person in some contexts. Regional vocabulary cannot be normalized without the exact inscriptional relation.

## Geometry

Polity identity is usable; a c. 500 BCE polygon is not resolved by this research packet.

## Access / language

This pass relied on translations and modern epigraphic scholarship rather than a full query/re-edition of the Raybūn corpus. That limitation is material and is why the result remains inconclusive rather than negative.
