# EXP-04 case 1 — Sumerian City-States, 2000 BCE

**Target:** R1:P:-2000:D:r1
**Frame:** polity_aggregate
**State:** experimental research packet
**Research outcome:** bounded_supported_subtargets; aggregate_uniformity_not_established
**Canonical effect:** none

## Bounded proposition

Ur III evidence from southern Mesopotamian administrative and legal contexts close to 2000 BCE supports the presence of legally enslaved people and coercive labour alongside other dependent and hired statuses.

The evidence is strong enough to establish slavery/coercive labour in specific Ur III institutional and city contexts around the anchor. It is **not** sufficient to assign one uniform practice state to every polity represented by the aggregate label “Sumerian City-States.”

## Required abstention

Do not:
- convert Ur III evidence into one uniform territorial classification for all Sumerian city-states;
- treat every geme2 worker as enslaved: the same term could be used for free and enslaved women depending on context;
- collapse slaves, prisoners of war, state dependents, hired workers and other coerced workers;
- infer high prevalence from the enormous surviving administrative archive;
- treat 2000 BCE as an exact synchronization point for every city/polity in the aggregate.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus:** Ur III administrative/legal contexts including Garshana and Umma, within southern Mesopotamia.

**Maximum inference:** slavery and coercive labour are directly supported in parts of the Ur III system close to 2000 BCE. The aggregate target needs either subtarget-level classification or explicit non-uniformity.

## Historical terminology / mapping

Material terms include arad/arad2, geme2, prisoners/human booty, and other dependent labour categories.

**Mapping status:** mixed / context-dependent.

Specialist work warns that labour/status vocabulary does not map one-to-one onto modern free/slave binaries.

## Source roles

- Robert McC. Adams, “Slavery and Freedom in the Third Dynasty of Ur: Implications of the Garshana Archives,” CDLJ 2010:2. Role: structural and archive-specific synthesis. It documents slaves and hired workers together at Garshana and explicitly notes status diversity.
- J. Nicholas Reid, “Punishment for the Coercion of Labour during the Ur III Period,” International Review of Social History 68/S31 (2023), DOI 10.1017/S0020859022000864. Role: specialist synthesis of slaves/dependents/coercive labour; strong for distinction among statuses and coercion, not prevalence.
- Veenker & Johnson, “The appellate process in a legal record di til-la from Ur III Umma,” Altorientalische Forschungen 36(2), DOI 10.1524/aofo.2009.0022. Role: bounded legal attestation to a slave sale in Ur III Umma.

## Counter/limiting evidence

Reid emphasizes that slaves appear to have been only a limited part of the labour force and that many other dependent workers were coerced without necessarily being slaves. Adams similarly shows that free hired labour could outnumber slaves in specific Garshana records.

This limits prevalence and category generalization but does not negate the bounded slavery evidence.
