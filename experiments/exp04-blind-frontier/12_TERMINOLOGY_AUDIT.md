# EXP-04 — Historical Terminology and Category-Mapping Audit

## Result

D-089's explicit historical-term/category-mapping fields **survive the blind tranche**.

Observed mapping states:
- direct_or_well_established;
- approximate_historically_specific;
- disputed;
- not_mapped.

All four are occupied.

## Cases

### Sumer / Ur III
Terms such as arad/arad2 and geme2 occur inside a wider status/labour system. Geme2 cannot mechanically be translated to slave in every context.
Disposition: **approximate_historically_specific**.

### Indus
The script is undeciphered and no secure indigenous slave-status term can be used.
Disposition: **not_mapped**.

### Hadhramaut / South Arabia
Terms including ʿbd can express different forms of subordination. Inscriptional context is decisive.
Disposition: **approximate_historically_specific**.

### Cuzco
Later Inca terms such as yanakuna and mit'a are historically meaningful, but their applicability at the 1300 anchor is not established.
Disposition: **not_mapped at anchor**.

### Viking network
Thraldom/slavery is a defensible comparative mapping for the relevant network evidence, though local conditions varied.
Disposition: **direct_or_well_established**.

### Andaman
No secure indigenous internal slavery-status term was recovered.
Disposition: **not_mapped**.

### Angkor
Khñum/kñum has repeatedly been translated as slave/bondsman but the equivalence is contested.
Disposition: **disputed**.

### Ethiopia
Multiple regional traditions are involved; one local term cannot stand for the full fragmented imperial frame.
Disposition: **approximate_historically_specific / regionalized**.

## Adversarial check

The field must not become a hidden normalization engine.

A mapping state constrains display and inference. It does not decide whether two institutions are substantively equivalent.

## Decision

No new controlled taxonomy is justified.

Keep the deliberately small contract: historical_terms, category_mapping_status and category_mapping_note.
