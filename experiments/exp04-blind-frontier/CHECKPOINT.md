## Reconciliation result

CDLI P453801 / Nisaba 15 371 is one Mesopotamian administrative artifact. The pinned
2015 revision reads a-ru-a lugal and translates it as “Royal offerings”. A
Harappa.com reproduction attributed to Laursen–Steinkeller 2017 pp. 83–84 preserves
their stronger, text-specific interpretation of Nanaza and Samar as royal donated
slaves. It also preserves their explicit uncertainty about how the two reached
Babylonia; acquisition “somewhere in the Gulf” is a hypothesis, not provenance.

Therefore:
- CDLI translation and Laursen–Steinkeller interpretation are layers on one artifact,
  not independent attestations;
- the tablet provenance is Irisagrig (modern location uncertain), while acquisition
  geography and any Indus enslavement location remain unknown;
- the date is safely retained as Šū-Suen year 6 month 12 / Ur III context rather than
  manufacturing an exact absolute BCE year;
- broader specialist work on a-ru-a supports keeping donated/dependent status and
  modern slavery mapping distinct rather than making a-ru-a a universal slave label;
- this strengthens the bounded external-status evidence but does not establish Indus
  territorial slavery, institutionalization, recurrence, prevalence or exact 2000 BCE state.
