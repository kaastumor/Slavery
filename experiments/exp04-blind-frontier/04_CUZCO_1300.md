## Bounded proposition

Archaeology supports substantial political centralization and state formation in the Cuzco Valley during the Killke period (c. 1000–1400), with Cuzco growing as a regional center around 1300.

The strong evidence for yanakuna, resettled dependent labour and other Inca imperial labour categories belongs primarily to the later imperial system. This experiment does not back-project those mature institutions to Cuzco in 1300 without target-period evidence.

Accordingly, the slavery/servile-dependency classification at the 1300 anchor remains researched-inconclusive in this pass.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- project fifteenth-/sixteenth-century Inca imperial labour institutions backward to 1300;
- equate state formation, hierarchy, tribute or agricultural intensification with slavery;
- treat later Spanish ethnohistorical terminology as exact evidence for the social system at the anchor;
- map the later Tawantinsuyu extent onto Cuzco 1300.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus:** Cuzco Valley archaeology for state formation; later imperial/colonial evidence for yanakuna and other labour categories.

**Maximum inference:** an incipient/centralizing Inca polity existed around 1300; the later imperial dependency categories cannot yet be demonstrated at the anchor from the reviewed evidence.

## Historical terminology / mapping

Later relevant terms include yana/yanakuna, mitmaqkuna, mit'a/mitayos, acllacuna.

**Mapping status at 1300:** not_mapped / temporally unproven.

## Source roles

- Brian S. Bauer & R. Alan Covey, “Processes of State Formation in the Inca Heartland,” American Anthropologist 104(3) (2002), DOI 10.1525/aa.2002.104.3.846. Role: archaeological/state-formation context for 1000–1400; not slavery evidence.
- Brian S. Bauer, Ancient Cuzco: Heartland of the Inca (2004), chapter “The Development of the Inca State (AD 1000–1400).” Role: regional chronology; late-1300s/early-1400s outward expansion boundary.
- “The Question of Slavery in the Inca State,” in the Palgrave Handbook of Global Slavery (2023). Role: specialist analysis of later Inca dependency categories, especially yanacona; deliberately treated as later comparative context, not direct 1300 evidence.

## Counter/limiting evidence

Later Inca evidence shows that even yanacona covered a spectrum of dependency, service and elite roles. Therefore even a temporal back-projection would not justify a simple slavery label.
