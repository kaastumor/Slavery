# EXP-11 internal adversarial replay

**Target:** Temple of Amun at Karnak — 1000 BCE  
**Frozen provisional outcome attacked:** `RESEARCHED_INCONCLUSIVE`  
**Review status:** internal critique only; not independent review

## Attack 1 — Is the packet ignoring strong target-specific Amun slave labour?

**Finding:** no.

New Kingdom Amun/Karnak evidence is strong: prisoners and enslaved/captive people were
assigned to the temple and its productive workshops.

But the relevant evidence predates the selected anchor and belongs to a different
political/economic phase.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES** as earlier positive context only.

## Attack 2 — Does the New Kingdom end only shortly before 1000 BCE, making continuity reasonable?

**Finding:** continuity is plausible as a hypothesis but not demonstrated.

The New Kingdom–Third Intermediate transition materially changed centralized military
acquisition and political organization. Karev specifically warns that Third
Intermediate slavery documentation is sparse and that social/economic conditions
changed.

No source recovered here traces a named Amun slave/captive labour institution
continuously across that transition.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** continuity-by-proximity.

## Attack 3 — Does Menkheperre's `bꜣk.w` establish slave status?

**Finding:** no.

The Banishment Stela's context is political conflict, exile and amnesty. Specialist
treatments render the group as quarrelsome servants and in some discussion as
servants/priests of Karnak.

Loprieno and Karev both show why Egyptian servant/slave terms cannot be normalized
without context.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** `bꜣk.w = slaves` here.

## Attack 4 — Does the Asiatic domestic population inside Karnak establish captive/slave labour?

**Finding:** no.

Bennett's archaeological synthesis gives a valuable near-anchor population within the
Great Amun enclosure, but supplies no slave/property/unfree status.

Foreign identity is not status.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** ethnic/status conversion.

## Attack 5 — Do Amun-priestly property decrees prove Temple-of-Amun unfree labour?

**Finding:** they establish something narrower.

Muhs documents Amun-linked elite/property transactions and perpetual service
alienation near the anchor. This proves that durable alienable personal service
relations existed in the Amun-priestly legal/economic environment.

The clearest labour-service purchase concerns workers linked to the Temple of Osiris
at Abydos, and the property holders are members of the high-priestly family.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES AS CONTEXT; REJECT DIRECT TARGET TRANSFER**.

Institutional owner, transaction locus and labour locus remain separate.

## Attack 6 — Is the packet too strict by requiring modern chattel slavery?

**Finding:** no.

The protocol allows bounded coerced/unfree statuses that do not map to modern chattel
slavery. The problem is not category purity; it is target-specific status evidence.

Near-anchor evidence shows subordination, banishment, population and perpetual
services, but not enough to identify the relevant people as a Temple-of-Amun labour
category.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES**.

## Attack 7 — Does broader Third Intermediate slave sale evidence fill the target gap?

**Finding:** no.

Karev records slavery and slave sales in the period, but broad Egyptian occurrence
does not establish a Karnak/Amun-temple practice.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** regional-to-target substitution.

## Attack 8 — Could shabti sales/service language create a positive?

**Finding:** no.

Near-anchor Amun-family funerary decrees use service/sale metaphors for shabti
figurines. They are funerary substitutes, not human labourers.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** object/person category error.

## Final adversarial disposition

**RESEARCHED_INCONCLUSIVE — SURVIVES.**

The evidence package is unusually informative despite the inconclusive selected-anchor
status:

- **earlier target positive:** New Kingdom Amun/Karnak captive/enslaved labour;
- **near-anchor institutional continuity:** strong;
- **near-anchor subordinate/service/property context:** strong;
- **near-anchor Temple-of-Amun human slave/unfree labour classification:** unresolved.

A `BOUNDED_SUPPORTED` outcome would require crossing at least one unsupported bridge:
temporal continuity, terminology normalization, foreign-identity status, or
high-priestly property → temple workforce.

The strongest overturning evidence would be a Twenty-first-Dynasty Karnak/Amun
personnel, sale, dedication, property or labour record that specialist scholarship
securely classifies as human slave/unfree status tied to the Temple of Amun itself.
