## Per-target constraints

### Temple of Amun at Karnak — 1000 BCE

- **Outcome:** `QUALIFIED_C1_READY`
- **Frozen frame:** `institution_estate`
- **Temporal precision:** `anchor_supported_institutional_continuity`
- **Spatial precision:** Temple/Precinct of Amun-Ra institutional locus; estate holdings and full Karnak-complex extent unresolved.
- **Rationale:** Digital Karnak describes the Temple of Amun-Ra as a long-lived institutional complex. Near the exact anchor, a Metropolitan Museum object dated ca. 1000–797 BCE identifies service to Amun-Re in his Karnak temple within the household of High Priest Pinedjem II.
- **Mandatory limitation:** Keep the Amun institution distinct from the whole Karnak complex, Thebes and any inferred estate territory. Modern archaeological-site geometry is not an ancient estate boundary.

### Tōdai-ji — 800 CE

- **Outcome:** `QUALIFIED_C1_READY`
- **Frozen frame:** `institution_estate`
- **Temporal precision:** `anchor_supported_post_nara_foundation`
- **Spatial precision:** temple/institution locus at Nara; historical estate/property extent unresolved.
- **Rationale:** Tōdai-ji's official history places the predecessor in 728, state-monastery elevation in 741, the Great Buddha and hall in 749–752 and wider complex construction thereafter. The same history records continuity to the 855 earthquake. Nabunken independently describes Tōdai-ji as an eighth-century state-sponsored temple with Nara-period monumental construction.
- **Mandatory limitation:** Do not equate the institution with Nara, all later Tōdai-ji estates, later shōen holdings or modern World Heritage property boundaries.

### Trans-Saharan trade network — 1300 CE

- **Outcome:** `QUALIFIED_C1_READY`
- **Frozen frame:** `mobile_network`
- **Temporal precision:** `anchor_supported_multi_route_network`
- **Spatial precision:** changing multi-route caravan network; no single route or territorial polygon.
- **Rationale:** Guérin places strengthened trans-Saharan links under Mali in the thirteenth century and explicitly discusses connected exchange in the years around 1300. British Museum synthesis documents established trans-Saharan routes from about 800 and distinguishes multiple route/network configurations.
- **Mandatory limitation:** No single empire, city, route or modern desert polygon stands for the network. Later subject evidence must remain route/node/locus specific.

### Sápmi / Sámi communities — 1600 CE

- **Outcome:** `QUALIFIED_C1_READY`
- **Frozen frame:** `region_community`
- **Temporal precision:** `anchor_supported_retrospective_multi_community_frame`
- **Spatial precision:** multiple communities/siida under overlapping and changing state claims; no single polity or fixed 1600 Sápmi boundary.
- **Rationale:** Hansen's specialist study directly analyzes Sámi communities, mobility and tax records from 1551–1600 while noting the absence of fixed northern borders and overlapping taxation/trade claims. A historian-authored Store norske leksikon synthesis independently describes Sápmi around 1500–1600 as not yet cleanly divided by state borders.
- **Mandatory limitation:** “Sápmi / Sámi communities” is a retrospective regional research container. Do not infer one society, jurisdiction, community system or modern Sápmi polygon at 1600.
