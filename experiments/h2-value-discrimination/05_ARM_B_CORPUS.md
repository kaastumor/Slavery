**Issue:** #146

The format below is intentionally human-readable rather than a schema proposal. It applies the separations already accepted in D-058.

## Q1 — Mexica / Nahua `tlacotli`

### MEX-TERM-01 — colonial translation is evidence about terminology, not category equivalence

- **Assertion form:** bounded source/terminology assertion
- **Claim:** Sixteenth-century Nahuatl/Spanish lexical and chronicle traditions used `esclavo/esclava` to translate or describe `tlacotli`.
- **Evidence date:** primarily post-conquest sixteenth-century documentation
- **Historical applicability:** supports a historical translation practice; does not by itself prove that European and Nahua legal/social categories were equivalent
- **Temporal precision:** source items are datable; the pre-Hispanic referent is retrospective
- **Evidence locus:** central Mexican/Nahua documentary tradition
- **Inference extent:** terminology only
- **Attestation pattern:** repeated in multiple lexical/documentary witnesses
- **Interpretive basis:** source-language evidence + specialist philological interpretation
- **Sources:**
  - E4 Nahuatl Dictionary / Molina / Florentine attestations — **supports**
  - E1 Salazar 2025 — **qualifies** by showing `esclavo` could substitute for multiple indigenous categories
- **Must not infer:** “`tlacotli` = one timeless universal slavery type.”

### MEX-STATUS-02 — severe dependent/unfree labor with retained rights in the general category

- **Assertion form:** enduring-status characterization
- **Claim:** Specialist syntheses support a `tlacotli` status involving another person's control over labor, while also describing non-hereditary status and retained capacities such as marriage/property in the general category.
- **Approximate applicability:** Late Postclassic central Mexico, bounded to the pre-conquest Mexica/Nahua context under review; exact universal start is not established here.
- **Temporal precision:** broad period
- **Evidence locus:** central Mexican/Nahua societies represented in the consulted scholarship
- **Inference extent:** category-level, not every individual or every polity using Nahuatl
- **Practice facets supported:**
  - labor control: supported
  - debt/punitive entry: supported
  - hereditary transmission: generally **not supported** for the general `tlacotli` category in Smith/Salazar
  - personal property/family capacity: supported as retained in those syntheses
  - alienability: **qualified/variable**
- **Historical characterization:**
  - occurrence: supported
  - institutionalization: supported as a recognized status
  - prevalence scope: unassessed
  - structural significance: unassessed
- **Sources:**
  - E3 Smith 2012 — **supports**
  - E1 Salazar 2025 — **supports/qualifies**
- **Must not infer:** prevalence or chattel-property equivalence.

### MEX-QUAL-03 — commodification/alienability is contested or subtype-dependent

- **Assertion form:** interpretive qualification
- **Claim:** Scholarship does not support treating alienability/property-in-person as uniform across all `tlacotli`; von Mentz stresses market/reification features while Salazar's general-category reconstruction emphasizes pledged labor and retained personhood, with later work distinguishing more severe subcategories.
- **Classification outcome:** **qualified / historiographically non-uniform**
- **Research stage:** reviewed for this experiment only
- **Evidence locus:** same documentary tradition; different scholarly syntheses
- **Sources:**
  - E2 von Mentz 2007 — **supports** commodifying features
  - E1 Salazar 2025 — **challenges/qualifies** uniform alienability
- **Must not infer:** either “not slavery at all” or “all `tlacotin` were chattel slaves.”

### Q1 corpus answer

Strongest defensible synthesis: **recognized severe dependent/unfree labor conventionally translated as slavery, but property, heredity, alienability and exit must be faceted rather than assumed from the English label.**

Strongest required abstention: **no universal modern slavery subtype and no prevalence/structural-majority claim from this packet.**

## Q2 — East India Company territories around Act V of 1843

### IND-LAW-01 — exact legal breakpoint

- **Layer:** legal/state regime
- **Assertion form:** bounded legal event
- **Claim:** On 7 April 1843, Act V changed the enforceability/recognition of slavery in East India Company territories by barring specified official/court enforcement of slave-property claims and equalizing penal protection in the Act's terms.
- **Evidence date:** 7 April 1843
- **Positive applicability:** exact from enactment for the legal rule, subject to jurisdiction/application
- **Temporal precision:** exact day for enactment
- **Evidence locus:** legal jurisdiction described as East India Company territories
- **Inference extent:** legal layer only
- **Source:** E1 Act V — **directly supports**
- **Must not infer:** actual disappearance of enslaved/dependent practice.

### IND-PRACTICE-02 — the legal event does not create a practice stop date

- **Layer:** territorial/social practice
- **Assertion form:** interpretive boundary + persistence qualification
- **Claim:** Specialist scholarship does not support using 7 April 1843 as a universal practice-absence date; the measure is described as delegalisation/non-recognition rather than active universal emancipation, with coercive/slavery relations persisting or being reclassified.
- **Evidence date/range:** scholarship addresses pre/post-1843 transition; no single exact practice end date is established
- **Positive applicability:** **not represented as one continuous all-territory interval**
- **Temporal precision:** broad/transition
- **Evidence loci:**
  - Bengal for Karak's detailed case
  - wider British India/South Asia in Major/handbook synthesis
- **Inference extent:** only as far as each source supports; Bengal evidence is not silently generalized
- **Attestation pattern:** multiple specialist syntheses
- **Interpretive basis:** legal/social history
- **Sources:**
  - E2 Major 2012 — **supports**
  - E3 Karak 2026 — **supports** for Bengal and the delegalisation interpretation
  - E4 South Asia synthesis 2023 — **supports**
- **Must not infer:** a single post-1843 positive slavery condition everywhere or a single extinction date.

### IND-SPACE-03 — jurisdiction is historical, not modern-national

- **Layer:** geography / legal scope
- **Claim:** The Act's text applies to “Territories of the East India Company”; a modern India boundary is not the evidentiary jurisdiction.
- **Evidence locus:** statutory language
- **Inference extent:** historical Company jurisdiction requires time-aware resolution
- **Source:** E1 Act V — **supports**
- **Must not infer:** identical legal status in princely states or later/nonsynchronous territories.

### Q2 corpus answer

Strongest defensible synthesis: **legal recognition/enforcement changes on 7 April 1843; practice does not receive the same automatic breakpoint.**

Strongest required abstention: **no “slavery absent after 1843” territorial claim and no modern-India geometry substitution.**

## Q3 — Genoese Black Sea slave-trade network

### GEN-NET-01 — Genoese participation is a network claim

- **Layer:** external/network participation
- **Assertion form:** enduring network/process assertion
- **Claim:** Genoese merchants and governing/commercial structures participated materially in Black Sea slave trading during the late thirteenth through fifteenth centuries.
- **Applicability:** broad c. 1260s–15th century for the network, with node-specific bounds handled separately
- **Temporal precision:** century/decades depending subclaim
- **Evidence loci:** Black Sea ports, Mediterranean routes, Genoese commercial institutions
- **Inference extent:** participation network
- **Historical characterization:** no territorial prevalence field is populated from this claim
- **Sources:**
  - E1 Barker 2019 — **supports**
  - E2 Miner 2021 — **corroborates/qualifies**
  - E5 Fossati Raiteri 2008 — **corroborates**
- **Must not infer:** practice intensity in Genoa or all Genoese territories.

### GEN-CAFFA-02 — Caffa is a bounded trade-node claim

- **Layer:** network + site practice
- **Claim:** Caffa functioned as a major Genoese-administered Black Sea slave-market/export node.
- **Applicability:** Genoese administration must end by Ottoman conquest in 1475; do not extend “Genoese Caffa” to the book's 1500 endpoint.
- **Evidence locus:** Caffa
- **Inference extent:** Caffa/site and network role
- **Sources:** E1 Barker; E2 Miner
- **Must not infer:** continuous Genoese control to 1500.

### GEN-EVENT-03 — bounded movement event

- **Layer:** network participation
- **Assertion form:** bounded event
- **Claim:** The Black Sea synthesis records specific enslaved people shipped from Caffa toward Genoa, including a 1395 transport/escape episode.
- **Evidence date:** 1395 for the example
- **Evidence locus:** Caffa → maritime route → southern Black Sea transit → intended Genoa destination
- **Inference extent:** event/route only
- **Source:** E4 Black Sea synthesis 2023 — **supports**
- **Must not infer:** route count = territorial prevalence.

### GEN-PRACTICE-04 — Genoa city practice requires independent evidence

- **Layer:** territorial/local practice
- **Assertion form:** enduring practice assertion
- **Claim:** Independent western-Mediterranean scholarship supports slavery in Genoa city/urban households and markets around the fourteenth–fifteenth centuries.
- **Evidence locus:** Genoa city
- **Inference extent:** Genoa urban practice; broader Ligurian/Genoese territory unassessed
- **Attestation pattern:** aggregate documentary scholarship, not inferred from voyages
- **Interpretive basis:** notarial/tax/household scholarship
- **Historical characterization:**
  - occurrence: supported
  - institutionalization: supported
  - prevalence scope: urban estimates exist in scholarship but this pilot does not normalize them into an Atlas comparative scale
  - structural significance: Barker's framing “society with slaves,” not “slave society,” is retained as specialist interpretation
- **Source:** E3 Schiel 2023 — **supports**
- **Cross-source qualifier:** E1/E2 Barker/Miner — **context/qualify**
- **Must not infer:** network participation itself proves this claim.

### Q3 corpus answer

Strongest defensible synthesis: **Genoese network participation and Caffa's trade-node role are strong; Genoa city slavery is also supported, but by independent urban/household evidence.**

Strongest required abstention: **no voyage/market-volume-derived territorial prevalence and no Genoese-Caffa control after 1475.**

## Cross-case query enabled by Arm B

The three cases are not placed on one intensity ladder. They can nevertheless be compared by **failure mode** and evidence structure:

| Case | Decisive layer | Main inference boundary | Unsupported dimension kept empty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mexica `tlacotli` | practice/status + terminology | translation ≠ one property bundle | prevalence / structural significance |
| Company India 1843 | legal event vs practice | law date ≠ practice end | universal post-1843 practice state |
| Genoese Black Sea | participation/network vs local practice | route/port participation ≠ territorial prevalence | broader territorial prevalence |

This is the main candidate corpus-level advantage: the comparison is made through orthogonal claim properties rather than by ranking the historical institutions themselves.
