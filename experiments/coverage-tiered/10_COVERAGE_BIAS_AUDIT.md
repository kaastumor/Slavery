# COV-003 — Coverage / Bias Audit

**Issue:** #155  
**Purpose:** test whether systematic target selection retains cases that an easy-evidence proxy would omit.  
**Not tested:** actual public demand, grant demand, or user interest.

## Easy-evidence proxy

Preregistered proxy:

> Direct coverage of the exact target/period in Cambridge or Palgrave, where that judgment is already known from C1/shared-source research.

No C0-only target was researched merely to fill this proxy.

### C1 results

Across the 12 C1 targets:

- direct Cambridge coverage: **0/12**
- direct Palgrave coverage: **0/12**
- at least one direct Cambridge/Palgrave target: **0/12**
- bounded-supported outcomes discovered anyway: **4/12**
- researched-inconclusive outcomes: **8/12**

Therefore an inclusion rule requiring direct major-handbook coverage would have selected **none** of the 12 systematically chosen C1 targets whose historical research was actually performed.

This is not evidence that Cambridge or Palgrave are inadequate. Their units are often regional/periodic rather than the exact randomly sampled polity-year cell.

## What systematic selection retained

The systematic sample retained:
- four cells where bounded support was recoverable from specialist/legal/inscriptional evidence despite no direct handbook treatment;
- eight cells where the correct result was a researched abstention rather than a positive classification;
- historically awkward targets that an evidence-richness workflow would have little incentive to choose.

That is a real methodological function:

> systematic selection can preserve difficult and inconclusive targets instead of allowing obvious source-rich cases to define the corpus.

## C0 identity-validation function

Six targets were preregistered for identity/time validation before subject-matter interpretation:

- Later Zhou, -500 — **mismatch**
- Xu, -500 — **ambiguous**
- Myinsaing, 1300 — **valid at anchor**
- Khalji, 1300 — **valid at anchor**
- Pañcāla, -500 — **valid at anchor**
- Vajji, -500 — **valid at anchor**

Observed mismatch/ambiguity: **2/6**.

This is a tiny audit sample and is **not a population defect-rate estimate**.

It does establish that a cheap C0 layer can surface target-frame problems without first producing a slavery conclusion.

## Limits

1. “Direct Cambridge/Palgrave” is deliberately crude. A source-rich target may still lack a dedicated chapter.
2. C0-only handbook coverage is unknown and remains unknown.
3. The three COV-003 batches are not a representative world sample.
4. The experiment remains polity-frame biased and does not solve non-polity coverage.
5. This audit demonstrates a bias-resistance function, not that systematic coverage is always the best allocation of historian effort.

## Disposition

**Coverage/bias function: SURVIVES.**

The experiment supports the narrower proposition:

> A systematic target register preserves difficult, inconclusive and potentially misidentified cases that a direct-handbook/easy-evidence selection rule would omit.

This is a methodological reason for C0 breadth independent of positive slavery findings.
