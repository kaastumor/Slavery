# DISC-05 — external adjacency scan for evidence-core leverage

**Issue:** #238  
**Primary gate:** method / representation discovery  
**Status:** external scan executed; no implementation authorized  
**Base commit:** ef3da3ccd06b7f345c7430626e598dd30a477fd4

## Question

What established methods, standards or adjacent research practices could materially
improve the Historical Slavery Atlas's reviewed evidence core, research workflow,
comparative reasoning or portable outputs without reopening already-parked feature
ideas or adding unjustified infrastructure?

## Baseline

Current strongest boring baseline:
- flat target/source tables + Markdown research packets;
- source/version/locator + independence group;
- support/challenge/qualify/context direction and claim-fitness prose;
- explicit proposition + required abstention;
- separate temporal precision/applicability and locus/inference extent;
- immutable manifest/checksum candidate packages;
- existing historical-identity/geometry model;
- manual/bounded horizon selection from the frozen target registry.

Already-parked product ideas are not candidates merely under new names:
- richer timeline;
- generic search/filter;
- graph visualization;
- dedicated comparison UI;
- generic platform expansion.

## Search modes

1. argument/inference provenance;
2. source-first historical assertion models;
3. research-object/data packaging standards;
4. historical place/time interchange standards;
5. evidence-gap and uncertainty/disagreement methods;
6. source-locator/annotation standards;
7. living-review/update-trigger practice.

For each candidate record:
**external observation → project friction → transfer mechanism → strongest current
baseline → transfer risk → cheap falsifier → disposition**.

## Boundaries

No slavery subject research. No production implementation. No schema/ontology migration.
No new graph/vector/search service. No external participant work. No claim of user
demand from desk research.

## Stop rule

Stop the scan when additional external leads repeat a mechanism already represented in
the register and would not change candidate disposition or next-experiment selection.

Select at most one next discriminating experiment.
