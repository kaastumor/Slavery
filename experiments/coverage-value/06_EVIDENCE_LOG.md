## Supported
- **-500:C Sabaeans:** South Arabian/Sabaic epigraphy supports captivity and slave status, including proprietary purchase/sale relations in some first-millennium contexts.
- **-500:D Carthage:** Specialist scholarship supports slavery as an important institution in Punic Carthage, including slave labour in the agricultural hinterland and aristocratic slaveholding.
- **500:D Anglo-Saxon England:** Early Anglo-Saxon England is defensibly characterized as slaveholding in the broad period around 500 CE, though direct legal evidence begins later.
- **1300:D Zayyanid dynasty:** Sources support enslaved people and slave-trade connections in the Zayyanid/Tlemcen context, including Christian slaves linked to the Banū ʿAbd al-Wād.
- **1300:F Chámpa:** Early-thirteenth-century descriptions of Champa report people being bought and made slaves, supporting a bounded slavery/slave-market claim near 1300.
- **1800:A United States of America:** Chattel slavery was legally institutionalized and practiced in substantial parts of the United States around 1800, with important regional legal differences.
- **1800:C Bornu Empire:** Bornu was materially involved in slave raiding and trans-Saharan slave trading across the period leading into 1800.
- **1800:E British Ceylon:** Slavery is directly documented in Jaffna under Dutch and early British rule around 1800; abolition measures developed only afterward.

## Disputed
- **1800:D Kingdom of Great Britain:** At 1800, metropolitan Britain cannot be summarized as simply slave-society-present or slavery-abolished: metropolitan slavery had largely receded while imperial/Atlantic participation remained extensive.
- **1800:F Portuguese Colonies:** Multiple Portuguese colonial settings had slavery/slave-trade institutions, but the aggregate sampled label 'Portuguese Colonies' cannot support one territorial-practice state at 1800.
