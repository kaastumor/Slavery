# COV-001 — Final Result

**Issue:** #148  
**Date:** 2026-09-23  
**Disposition:** **NARROW SURVIVE — GLOBAL COVERAGE CORPUS THESIS SURVIVES; ATLAS PRODUCT THESIS REMAINS STOPPED**

## What was tested

COV-001 deliberately did **not** test another Atlas UI or database design.

It tested whether assembling a systematic global historical evidence/coverage index has value in its own right.

The experiment:
- froze a deterministic 24-stratum polity-year sample before research;
- retained 21 valid targets and 3 sampling gaps;
- added 4 preregistered non-polity challenge contexts;
- researched 25 cells under a bounded source ladder;
- represented the result as plain JSON/CSV/Markdown;
- compared the frozen mini-index with strong global handbooks and a competent ordinary matrix;
- ran specialist narrative and SlaveVoyages negative controls;
- adversarially downgraded the preliminary result before disposition.

## Research outcome

25 researched cells:
- **8 bounded_supported**
- **2 materially_disputed**
- **15 researched_inconclusive**

Plus:
- **3 sampling gaps** in the polity frame.

No inconclusive or sampling-gap state is historical absence.

All four non-polity challenge cells remained researched-inconclusive. This is important negative evidence about how difficult “complete world coverage” actually is.

## Fixed-query result after adversary

Material mini-index value:
- Q2 law vs practice — **yes**
- Q3 network/participation vs territorial inference — **yes**
- Q5 global-handbook coverage — **yes, bounded**
- Q6 unresolved coverage/gaps — **yes**

Parity / not counted:
- Q1 basic overview — **ordinary matrix already sufficient**
- Q4 category/translation — **useful but too easily reproduced by one ordinary matrix column to count as a distinct material win**

**Final: 4/6 material tasks.**

Pre-registered requirement: at least 3/6 across at least two mechanisms.

**Gate: PASS.**

## Negative controls

- N1 deep Champa historiography: specialist narrative **beats the index**.
- N2 Jamaica 1750–1800 voyage/traffic research: SlaveVoyages **beats the index**.

Both controls pass.

The index is therefore not validated as a replacement for specialist scholarship or specialized databases.

## What actually survived

The surviving contribution is not “a better ontology” or “an interactive Atlas.”

It is:

> **a curated, auditable, explicitly incomplete global coverage corpus that records what can currently be said about a place/time target, what cannot be said, why, and where the evidence comes from.**

Useful properties in this pilot:
- direct law/practice/network separation;
- visible researched-inconclusive and disputed states;
- explicit source-scope and temporal/spatial boundaries;
- reusable judgments about whether global syntheses directly cover a target;
- visibility of sampling/source-identity failures;
- preservation of non-polity and access/source limitations.

## What did not survive

COV-001 provides no new evidence for:
- the former full Atlas application;
- production Postgres migration;
- MapLibre/frontend expansion;
- bulk geography publication;
- vector/RAG/graph infrastructure;
- automated mass research;
- a single world slavery-intensity scale;
- literal completeness.

D-063 remains in force.

## Meaning of “complete overview”

The experiment supports pursuing **systematic coverage**, not claiming completeness.

A defensible future artifact would say:
- what research frame has been covered;
- what remains unresearched;
- what is inconclusive;
- where categories are disputed;
- where evidence is only regional/adjacent;
- where source access/language limits the synthesis.

It must never turn blankness into “no slavery.”

## Why this result is different from H2

H2 asked:
> Does bespoke Atlas structure/UI materially outperform strong ordinary case research?

Answer: **not enough; stop Atlas expansion.**

COV-001 asked:
> Does accumulating disciplined place/time coverage create reusable value even if each individual case could be researched conventionally?

Answer: **yes, narrowly.**

The value appears only when asking across many cases:
- Where is law different from practice?
- Where is trade evidence being over-read?
- Where do global handbooks stop being target-specific?
- Where have we researched and still cannot responsibly classify?

That is a corpus/network effect, not a single-case reasoning advantage.

## Remaining uncertainty

This 25-cell pilot does **not** establish that a much larger corpus is worth its maintenance cost.

The main unresolved question is now scale economics:

> Does coverage-corpus value grow faster than the research, review and update burden when the corpus becomes materially larger?

This pilot authorizes at most **one further bounded corpus-scale experiment** aimed at that question.

It does not authorize “research the whole world now.”

## Canonical effect

None.

- COV-001 rows remain experimental/non-canonical.
