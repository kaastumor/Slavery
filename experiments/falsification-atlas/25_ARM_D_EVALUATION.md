# COV-004 Arm D — Atlas Re-entry Evaluation

**Baseline B:** competent conventional map/GIS + compact register  
**Atlas A:** same frozen evidence and display geometry + linked claim-layer semantics  
**Evaluation:** non-independent artifact-behavior comparison

A gain counts only if it removes a substantive reconstruction step or prevents a misleading inference. Styling, toggles and convenience do not count.

## D1 — Kilwa: evidence locus versus wider inference

Question:
> At 1400, what geographic extent can evidence tied to Kilwa itself safely support, and what wider Swahili-coast inference must remain unmade?

### Baseline B
- map shows Kilwa as a site point, not a coastal polygon;
- register/detail explicitly says the evidence is site/network bounded;
- inference warning says not to generalize to the whole Swahili coast.

State: **direct/traceable**.

### Atlas A
- adds linked evidence-locus / inference-extent display and separate claim lanes;
- still shows only the Kilwa locus.

State: **direct**.

**Material Atlas gain: NO.**

The stronger baseline already prevents the key false territorialization.

**ATLAS A RE-ENTRY GATE: FAIL (0/4 material improvements).**

## Critical interpretation

This result does **not** imply that maps or an atlas presentation have no role.

Baseline B is already:
- a world map;
- year/frame-aware enough for the frozen tasks;
- capable of points, routes and fuzzy regions;
- linked to an evidence register;
- explicit about C0/unresearched state.

In ordinary language, that is itself a **thin atlas**.

What fails is the claim that a bespoke project-specific spatial/temporal evidence inspector currently adds enough value over a competent ordinary map + register to justify additional Atlas-specific machinery.

Therefore:

> **The Atlas concept survives in its boring/minimal form; bespoke Atlas semantics remain unearned.**
