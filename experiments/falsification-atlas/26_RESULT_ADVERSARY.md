# COV-004 — Final Result Adversary

**Issue:** #157  
**Inputs:** Arm A masked replay; Arm B non-polity C0/C1/C2; Arm C publication time-split; Arm D baseline/Atlas comparison  
**Disposition before attack:** METHOD SURVIVE

This adversary attacks both the positive tiered-method result and the negative Atlas re-entry result.

---

# 1. Arm A passed exactly at the boundary

Observed:
- 1/6 previously non-escalated rows contained a material false negative;
- 2/3 previously escalated rows still earned deeper work.

The false negative was not trivial. Chandela's overall inconclusive state was safe, but its positive captivity proposition rested on a citation below the project's production-grade source standard.

## Attack

A system that misses 1/6 of supposedly safe compact rows may still be too risky for publication at scale.

The review is also only masked replay by the same model/project context, not an independent historian.

## Disposition

**SURVIVES ONLY AS AN EXPERIMENTAL RESEARCH TIER.**

Mandatory repair:
- source-quality failure becomes an explicit C2/review trigger;
- C1 cannot be publication-ready merely because its overall outcome is cautious;
- independent or multi-reviewer calibration remains necessary before large public C1 expansion.

Do not convert 1/6 into an estimated global error rate.

---

# 2. Arm B may prove schema flexibility more than historical adequacy

Observed:
- 12/12 non-polity targets registered without false territorialization;
- 8/8 researched rows fit the C1 schema;
- no silent polygon proxy.

## Attack A — candidate pool authorship

The candidate pool was manually curated by the same project/model from general historical knowledge before subject research.

Hash selection within that pool prevents evidence-based cherry-picking, but does not make the pool representative.

## Attack B — experimental frame taxonomy

node/site, institution/estate, mobile/network, and region/community are useful challenge classes, not a validated ontology of non-polity history.

## Attack C — source quality is uneven

Several rows are intentionally difficult:
- Cahokia relies on interpretive archaeology;
- Silk Road evidence is concentrated in Turfan and includes research-lecture/conference material;
- Samoa depends heavily on retrospective ethnography;
- Cluny requires careful servus/ancilla interpretation.

## Disposition

**ARM B SURVIVES AS A REPRESENTABILITY TEST, NOT A VALIDATED NON-POLITY ONTOLOGY.**

What it establishes:
> The claim model can represent sites, institutions, networks and fuzzy communities without forcing territorial-polity semantics.

What it does not establish:
> These four frame classes exhaust non-polity historical reality or that all eight experimental C1 conclusions are publication-ready.

---

# 3. Non-polity C2 supports selective depth but remains small

C2 material gains:
- Viking network — yes;
- Cluny — yes;
- Cahokia — no material change;
- Samoa — no material change.

## Attack

2/4 is encouraging but too small to establish a reliable escalation rate.

The strongest positive is qualitative:
- Viking C2 shows why trade-network evidence must not become “Viking society = slave society”;
- Cluny C2 shows why institutional servile terminology needs deeper category analysis.

## Disposition

**SURVIVES as evidence for triggered depth, not for a calibrated global escalation model.**

---

# 4. Arm C is the strongest new evidence in COV-004

Observed:
- six rows were frozen as 2015-as-of snapshots;
- contaminated candidates were explicitly excluded before selection;
- 3/6 received genuine accepted 2016–2026 evidence events.

Events:
- Sabaeans — material evidence-state strengthening plus terminology qualification;
- Champa — corroboration/qualification;
- Cao — broader contextual corroboration without target-state change.

Three rows had no accepted later event.

## Attack A — 2015 snapshots are bounded reconstructions, not exhaustive 2015 historiographies

Correct.

A source missed from 2014 would make the apparent “later update” less novel.

## Attack B — search completeness is not guaranteed

Correct.

The test shows architecture under bounded update discovery, not bibliographic exhaustiveness.

## Disposition

**ARM C SURVIVES.**

Why:
- the cutoff and snapshots were frozen before the accepted later evidence;
- the Sabaean case materially changes the bounded research state;
- unchanged rows remain unchanged;
- old snapshots remain auditable;
- later states can be release-bound without silent rewriting.

This validates **versioned evidence revision mechanics**, not cheap maintenance.

---

# 5. Attack the Atlas failure: the baseline is already an atlas

Formal Arm D result:
- D1–D4: 0 material gains for bespoke Atlas A;
- D5: parity;
- no new misleading Atlas inference.

## Critical attack

Calling this “Atlas failure” is too broad.

Baseline B contains:
- a world map;
- points, routes and fuzzy regions;
- research-state symbols;
- frame/research filters;
- linked target evidence and citations.

That is already a small historical atlas in ordinary language.

Therefore D does **not** compare:
> Atlas versus no Atlas.

It compares:
> a competent boring atlas/map+register versus a bespoke evidence-semantic atlas inspector.

## Disposition

**THE BESPOKE ATLAS SEMANTICS FAIL; THE ATLAS CONCEPT DOES NOT.**

The experiment supports the smaller product form:
> **ordinary map/GIS navigation + tiered evidence register.**

No custom claim-layer interaction has yet earned its maintenance cost.

---

# 6. Could the Atlas A test have been unfairly hard?

Yes, deliberately.

Baseline B was allowed:
- correct geometry types;
- C0 markers;
- warnings;
- filters;
- row evidence;
- citations.

That is required by the project's strongest-boring-baseline principle.

If Atlas A only wins against a crippled map, it has not demonstrated project value.

## Disposition

The 0/4 result is robust for the **extra semantic UI**.

---

# 7. Could Atlas value emerge only at larger scale?

Possibly.

At 12 targets, a map and table are easy to mentally join.

At hundreds or thousands of targets:
- layer toggles;
- selected-year filtering;
- evidence-locus/inference-extent inspection;
- geometry certainty;
- research-coverage maps

could become materially more useful.

COV-004 does not test that scale effect.

But this is an unproven future hypothesis, not a reason to build the platform now.

## Disposition

Leave large-scale Atlas interaction value **open**, not validated.

---

# 8. Historical geometry remains under-tested here

The COV-004 display geometries are explicitly experimental:
- points;
- schematic routes;
- fuzzy boxes.

They do not test the full historical-polygon resolver or production selected-year geometry architecture.

Therefore COV-004 cannot conclude that historical-boundary navigation has no value.

H2 remains the stronger evidence against bespoke thin-view value in the old cases; COV-004 adds evidence that non-polity semantics still do not make custom UI win at small scale.

---

# 9. Does C0 become a giant empty map?

Potentially.

A global C0 atlas could visually overwhelm users with registered-but-unresearched targets.

The architecture avoids false absence only if public visualization clearly separates:
- target existence;
- research status;
- historical claim.

This is a design obligation, not solved merely by storing C0 correctly.

---

# 10. Final adversarial disposition

## Arm A — escalation calibration
**PASS AT BOUNDARY / FRAGILE.**
Keep source-quality escalation trigger; require independent calibration before production scale.

## Arm B — non-polity representability
**PASS.**
The claim architecture is not inherently polity-bound.

## Arm C — real historical updates
**PASS.**
Versioned/as-of evidence revision works under real later scholarship.

## Arm D — bespoke Atlas re-entry
**FAIL.**
No material advantage over the strong conventional map+register baseline.

## Formal preregistered overall outcome
**METHOD SURVIVE.**

But the correct interpretation is:

> **Tiered evidence method survives, and a boring map/register atlas remains a justified presentation/navigation form. Bespoke Atlas-specific semantic machinery is still unearned.**

This preserves the Atlas concept without confusing “Atlas” with a custom application stack.

- comprehensive C1 research;
- global C2 completion;
- a production Atlas application;
- custom frontend/backend infrastructure;
- automated mass research;
- treating the four non-polity frame classes as canonical ontology;
- claiming independent-review reliability;
- claiming maintenance cost savings.

---

# 12. What a next real programme would have to look like

If the sponsor chooses to move from experiments into a real coverage programme, the evidence now supports only a deliberately lean form:

1. broad C0 target/research register;
2. C1 only for a stratified research programme;
3. C2 triggered by ambiguity, disagreement, temporal/spatial risk **or source-quality failure**;
4. versioned releases;
5. source-to-claim dependencies;
6. conventional map/GIS navigation over the register;
7. no bespoke Atlas platform until scale itself demonstrates a need that the conventional map cannot satisfy.

That is a coherent Historical Slavery Atlas — just not the originally imagined application architecture.
