# COV-002 — Result Adversary

**Issue:** #151  
**Input:** frozen 37-row corpus, F/R/M representations, reuse evaluation, information-loss audit, six maintenance shocks, negative controls  
**Disposition:** **RESULT SURVIVES; APPLY STOP GATE**

## Attack 1 — The maintenance test is underpowered because only three searches changed rows

**Disposition: survives with limitation.**

This is true: 3/6 bounded searches yielded no acceptable new specialist source.

But the protocol explicitly allowed no-change updates and fixed the >=4/6 maintenance-savings threshold before searches.

Counting 0/0 rows as compact-register savings would change the meaning of the metric after seeing the result.

The experiment therefore cannot claim the maintenance-economy gate passed.

It may say only:
- static review surface is lower;
- observed changed rows cost the same number of local review atoms in F and R;
- stronger claims about long-run maintenance economics remain unproven.

## Attack 2 — Review atoms are not real labour time

**Disposition: survives with limitation.**

Correct. They are an observable representation-surface proxy.

Therefore:
- do not claim R is 40% faster or cheaper in staff hours;
- do claim R contains about 60% of F's reviewable artifact surface under the preregistered counting rule.

## Attack 3 — M could simply add law/network/coverage columns and become R

**Disposition: survives and shrinks contribution.**

Exactly.

The surviving reusable value is not proprietary project machinery. A competent spreadsheet that adds the demonstrated columns **is effectively the compact register**.

This supports simplification, not a larger system.

## Attack 4 — R omits source role/direction metadata that might matter later

**Disposition: survives with preservation constraint.**

F retains richer evidence metadata and should remain preserved as an audit/research artifact where it already exists.

The fixed tasks do not show that metadata earning routine maintenance cost at corpus scale.

Do not delete F; do not require every future coverage row to reproduce all F metadata absent a demonstrated consumer.

## Attack 5 — Three of twelve expansion targets were invalid/unsafe before slavery classification

**Disposition: survives and counts against scaling.**

New Netherland 1800, medieval Kingdom of Georgia 1800 and the Western Regions protectorate 500 expose upstream historical-geography/identity defects.

This means global scaling incurs a target-validation cost before subject research even starts.

The coverage corpus is useful for exposing such problems, but they make “just add more cells” less economical.

## Attack 6 — Same-agent research/evaluation contaminates productivity conclusions

**Disposition: survives with limitation.**

No human productivity or elapsed-time claim is made.

The strongest results are structural artifact behavior:
- explicit field exists or not;
- row/filter count;
- update fields touched;
- source link retained or not.

Independent user testing remains absent.

## Attack 7 — Handbook direct/adjacent coding is subjective

**Disposition: survives.**

The coding is a reviewed research judgment, not objective bibliographic completeness.

Its reuse value is real but bounded; it must retain source/version context.

## Attack 8 — The maintenance updates were English/web-accessible and may not represent archival revision work

**Disposition: survives with scope limit.**

Correct. The experiment does not establish economics for archival teams, non-digitized corpora or multilingual review.

That uncertainty argues against scaling authorization, not for it.

## Attack 9 — Since reuse value passed, STOP is too harsh

**Disposition: reject.**

The experiment was specifically designed to require both reusable value **and** an economically justified artifact form.

REDIRECT requires the maintenance-economy condition in addition to static compression.

NARROW SUSTAIN requires F to earn its extra complexity.

Neither occurred.

Changing the outcome because some components look promising would violate the preregistration.

## Attack 10 — STOP would imply the compact register has no value

**Disposition: reject that inference.**

STOP means:
- do not automatically scale research further;
- preserve COV-001/COV-002 and the compact register pattern;
- reuse the compact fields when a real research task needs them;
- require a new external reason before comprehensive corpus expansion.

It does not mean the 37-row experiment or methodology is valueless.

## Adversarial conclusion

The mixed result is robust:

- cross-cell reuse value **does compound enough to remain visible**;
- compact representation **is materially smaller at rest**;
- full F does **not** earn its extra routine complexity;
- compact R does **not** demonstrate lower evidence-update cost under the preregistered maintenance shock;
- upstream target validation adds nontrivial scaling burden.

No positive COV-002 outcome meets its complete gate.

**Apply STOP / preservation.**
