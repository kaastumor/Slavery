# COV-002 — Setup Adversary

**Issue:** #151  
**Historical research started:** no  
**Disposition:** **REVISE, then proceed**

## Attack 1 — "more rows" can manufacture a scale advantage

If M must reread all rows while F/R get explicit columns, larger N mechanically favors structured artifacts.

**Disposition: REVISE.**

Repair:
- M is allowed a research-status column and caveat column;
- R must demonstrate value specifically from the four proven cross-cell distinctions, not simply from row count;
- T5 overview is a parity control rather than a likely win.

## Attack 2 — the compact register is designed from COV-001 answers, so of course it may win

**Disposition: SURVIVES WITH PURPOSE.**

This is intentional. COV-002 is partly a compression test: now that COV-001 identified the useful information, can we remove everything else?

A win for R is not evidence of novelty. It is evidence that the project should shrink.

## Attack 3 — review-atom counts are a poor proxy for cost

**Disposition: REVISE.**

Never call them time or labour hours.

Add two qualitative checks:
- does the reviewer need to understand a distinct concept to validate the field?
- does the field duplicate a proposition stored elsewhere?

Report both raw atom counts and concept/duplication observations.

## Attack 4 — choosing rank-2 cells from the same strata may produce correlated geography/history

**Disposition: SURVIVES WITH LIMITATION.**

The purpose is scale economics, not another representativeness test. The COV-001 non-polity challenge cells remain in the combined corpus.

Do not claim the 37-row corpus is a representative world sample.

## Attack 5 — 12 new cells may be too small to reveal scale problems

**Disposition: SURVIVES WITH GATE.**

A 48% increase from 25→37 is enough to test whether reuse costs trend in the expected direction without converting the experiment into bulk research.

A positive result can justify only a *decision about artifact form*, not comprehensive ingestion.

## Attack 6 — maintenance shock can be gamed by picking sources after seeing rows

**Disposition: REVISE.**

Freeze the six update rows before update searches.

For each, use a fixed query recipe:
- exact target + anchor-period label + slavery/slave/unfree/dependency;
- one specialist-source search;
- take the first credible genuinely new specialist source that materially addresses the target;
- if none appears within the bounded search, record no-source-found.

Do not keep searching until a dramatic contradiction appears.

## Attack 7 — R could hide nuance that F preserves but fixed tasks never ask about

**Disposition: REVISE.**

Add an **information-loss audit**:
for each of the 12 new rows, list every F field omitted by R and classify it:
- duplicated;
- unused by demonstrated tasks;
- potentially safety-critical;
- uniquely valuable.

Any safety-critical loss fails the compression unless represented elsewhere in R.

## Attack 8 — maintenance is not just row edits; sources and interpretations go stale

**Disposition: REVISE.**

The update test must record:
- whether source metadata are version/date-specific;
- whether handbook coverage judgments become stale;
- whether a changed source invalidates only one row or a cross-cell query result.

## Attack 9 — same-agent research/evaluation contamination

**Disposition: SURVIVES WITH LIMITATION.**

Prefer isolated evaluators if available. Otherwise classify results as artifact-behavior evidence only.

No human-productivity claim.

## Attack 10 — positive economics can be mistaken for permission to scale indefinitely

**Disposition: REJECT THAT INFERENCE.**

Even REDIRECT or NARROW SUSTAIN ends this experiment with the backlog idle.

Any actual comprehensive expansion requires separate sponsor authorization and a release/review plan.

## Revised gate

Proceed with:
- 12 deterministic rank-2 cells;
- F/R/M comparison;
- information-loss audit;
- six deterministic maintenance shocks;
- the exact quantitative thresholds in the protocol.

No historical research starts until the expansion sample is frozen.
