# Qi — 500 BCE

Target: R1:P:-500:F:r2. Frozen frame: **polity**.  
Research stage: **researched_internal**.  
Research outcome: **researched-inconclusive for Qi territorial slavery/servitude; near-anchor redeemable personal-servitude narrative preserved as context**.  
EXP-08 packet; no R1 reviewed-state change, canonical update, P-level, geometry
promotion or publication.

## Bounded synthesis

The strongest recovered evidence is the transmitted **Yue Shifu (越石父)** story in the
*Yanzi chunqiu* tradition associated with Yan Ying (晏嬰), the Qi statesman who died
around the 500 BCE anchor.

In the received account, Yan Ying encounters Yue Shifu at **Zhongmou** while travelling
to Jin. Yue says that he has served another person as a **臣僕 / 僕** for three years,
having entered that condition to escape hunger and cold. He says that he can be
**redeemed (贖)**, and the story later has him speak of being sold again (**鬻**) if he
is still to be treated as a servant.

This is meaningful positive evidence for a transmitted early-Chinese concept of
**redeemable personal servitude/dependency** set within Yan Ying's lifetime.

It does **not** establish territorial slavery or servitude in Qi at exactly 500 BCE.

The decisive limits are:
- the servitude locus in the received narrative is Zhongmou during a journey to Jin,
  not securely Qi territory;
- the individual episode is not exactly dated inside Yan Ying's lifetime;
- the *Yanzi chunqiu* is a transmitted, composite ancient text rather than a dated
  administrative record of the event;
- parallel versions in the *Lüshi chunqiu* and *Shiji* belong to the same Yue Shifu
  narrative tradition and are not independent attestations;
- the terms 臣僕 / 僕 / 贖 / 鬻 do not by themselves establish hereditary chattel
  slavery, legal ownership, or a polity-wide slave institution.

The bounded conclusion is therefore:

**Around the 500 BCE horizon, a Qi-associated textual tradition preserves a
redeemable personal-servitude relationship involving Yan Ying and Yue Shifu, but the
current evidence does not securely locate that servitude within Qi territory or date it
to the exact anchor. Qi territorial slavery/servitude at 500 BCE remains
researched-inconclusive.**

No P-level is assigned.

## Frame and chronology

EXP-07 established that Qi is a defensible polity frame at 500 BCE while requiring that
later Warring States or Qin/Han boundaries not be projected backward.

The subject evidence does not improve that selected-year territorial geometry.

Yan Ying's death around 500 BCE makes the Yue Shifu tradition relevant to the frozen
historical horizon, but the episode itself carries no exact year in the received text.
The project therefore treats it as **near-anchor/lifetime tradition**, not as an exact
500 BCE cross-section.

Olivia Milburn's specialist study of the *Yanzi chunqiu* supports treating the work as
a genuinely ancient textual tradition with a complicated transmission history. That
textual antiquity is not converted into exact event dating.

## Yue Shifu and redeemable servitude

The received *Yanzi chunqiu* explicitly describes Yue Shifu with the terms
**臣僕 / 僕** and says:
- he had been in that condition for three years;
- hunger and cold were given as the reason he entered it;
- he could be redeemed;
- Yan Ying gave up a carriage horse to redeem him;
- Yue later uses the language of being sold again if treated as a servant.

Those features support a bounded category of **redeemable personal servitude or
bonded dependency**.

They do not by themselves answer:
- whether the status was juridically equivalent to later 奴婢 categories;
- whether the relationship was hereditary;
- whether the person was legally property;
- whether a market in people operated in Qi;
- whether the status was common;
- whether the episode reflects a Qi legal institution.

## Territorial problem: Zhongmou is not a Qi-positive locus

The same received account places the encounter at **Zhongmou** on Yan Ying's journey
to Jin.

That matters methodologically.

A story involving the minister of Qi is not automatically evidence of territorial
practice inside Qi. The project therefore refuses the inference:

> Qi actor / Qi-associated text → Qi territorial slavery.

The *Lüshi chunqiu* parallel preserves the same redemption story with textual
differences, and the *Shiji* later preserves another version. Neither fixes the core
territorial problem.

The episode is retained as **Qi-associated contextual evidence**, not a territorial
Qi attestation.

## Parallel textual witnesses are one dependency family

Three transmitted sources preserve versions of the Yue Shifu story:
- *Yanzi chunqiu*;
- *Lüshi chunqiu*, “Guan shi”;
- *Shiji* 62, “Guan Yan liezhuan”.

They are useful for textual transmission and variation.

They are **not counted as three independent historical events**.

For this packet they form one dependency family:
"yue-shipu-narrative-family".

The *Yanzi chunqiu* received version is the most detailed for the servant/redeemable
status vocabulary. The *Lüshi chunqiu* and *Shiji* parallels are corroborative of the
story's transmission, not independent prevalence evidence.

## Specialist category control

Robin D. S. Yates's specialist work on slavery in early China argues for major changes
in forms of slavery and marketability during the Eastern Zhou while also stressing the
difficulty of forcing varied dependent statuses into a single universal slave category.

Most of his detailed legal evidence is later Qin material.

For Qi 500 BCE this is used only as a **category boundary**:
- bought/redeemed dependency can be historically meaningful without being mapped
  automatically to Atlantic-style chattel slavery;
- later Qin legal categories must not be projected backward into Qi;
- broad Eastern Zhou change does not establish the exact Qi territorial institution.

## Law / practice

No exact-anchor Qi legal text defining the Yue Shifu status or a general slave category
was recovered in this pass.

The evidence is narrative/status vocabulary, not a legal code.

No legal recognition, prohibition, heritability rule, ownership rule or prevalence
claim is assigned.

## Network / territory

No external slave-trade network is assigned.

The journey setting is important only because it prevents a territorial overclaim:
Yan Ying's Qi political identity and later return with Yue Shifu do not relocate the
servitude episode into Qi.

## Evidence locus and inference extent

Evidence loci:
- Zhongmou in the Yue Shifu narrative;
- the *Yanzi chunqiu* textual tradition centered on Yan Ying/Qi;
- later parallel textual witnesses preserving the same story family;
- broad specialist Eastern Zhou slavery/status synthesis.

Maximum defensible inference:

**A near-500-BCE Qi-associated textual tradition preserves a redeemable
personal-servitude relationship, but the episode is not an exact-year or secure
Qi-territorial attestation.**

Required abstention:
- no exact 500 BCE event date;
- no Zhongmou → Qi territorial transfer;
- no 臣僕 / 僕 → hereditary chattel-slave conversion;
- no three-text-parallels → three independent attestations;
- no later Qin/Han legal category back-projection;
- no prevalence or market-scale claim;
- no practice polygon.

## Geometry state

**Qi polity frame valid; practice locus unresolved for Qi.**

No new historical geometry is promoted. The servitude story does not authorize a
Qi-wide practice polygon, and Zhongmou is not silently attached to Qi for this claim.

## Research disposition

**Researched-inconclusive for Qi territorial slavery/servitude at 500 BCE.**

Preserve as supported context:
- a near-anchor Yue Shifu narrative of redeemable personal servitude/dependency;
- terms 臣僕 / 僕, 贖 and 鬻 within that story.

Do not promote as supported:
- slavery/servitude physically occurring in Qi territory at the anchor;
- hereditary/property slavery;
- polity-wide institutionalization or prevalence;
- Qi-specific slave trade.

Reopen/deepen if:
- an independently sourced Spring-and-Autumn-period record securely locates slave or
  servile status inside Qi territory;
- archaeology or excavated texts establish a Qi-specific dependent-status institution
  near the anchor;
- specialist scholarship resolves the Yue Shifu status and territorial relation more
  specifically than the current transmitted narrative allows.

## Adversarial findings

- **SUPPORTED CONTEXT:** the received Yue Shifu story describes three years of
  redeemable personal servitude using 臣僕 / 僕 vocabulary.
- **REJECT:** the story proves territorial Qi slavery at 500 BCE.
- **REJECT:** the *Yanzi chunqiu*, *Lüshi chunqiu* and *Shiji* versions are three
  independent attestations.
- **REJECT:** redeemability or sale-language alone proves hereditary chattel slavery.
- **REJECT:** later Qin legal evidence supplies Qi's 500 BCE legal status.
- **UNRESOLVED:** exact legal classification of Yue Shifu's status.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Qi territorial occurrence, institutionalization, prevalence and
  slave-market/trade scale at the exact anchor.
- **PORTABLE CONTRACT SURVIVES:** near-anchor contextual evidence can remain positive
  while the frozen territorial claim remains researched-inconclusive.
