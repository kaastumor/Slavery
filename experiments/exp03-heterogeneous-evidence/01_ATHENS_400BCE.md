# EXP-03 case 1 — Classical Athens, c. 400 BCE

**State:** experimental research packet
**Target type:** city-state / polity with urban core
**Anchor:** c. 400 BCE
**Research outcome:** bounded_supported
**Canonical effect:** none

## Bounded proposition

Specialist scholarship supports chattel slavery as a legally and socially recognized institution in classical Athens during the period containing the c. 400 BCE anchor. The evidence also shows meaningful variation among enslaved statuses and lived conditions rather than one uniform enslaved experience.

A contemporary/near-contemporary political text independently treats slaves as an identifiable population at Athens and discusses some slaves' economic activity. This is direct evidence for the presence and social visibility of slavery, but its rhetorical genre does not establish prevalence.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- infer a numerical prevalence at c. 400 BCE from the density of surviving Athenian evidence;
- treat all Athenian enslaved people as occupying one uniform social/economic condition;
- project evidence about Athens automatically onto all of Attica or the wider Greek world;
- use a modern Athens polygon as the historical territorial extent.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus:** Athens and Athenian legal/social order.
**Maximum defensible inference in this packet:** classical Athenian city-state context during the broad period 451/0–323 BCE, with the c. 400 BCE anchor inside that interval.

The urban settlement, the civic polity and Attica are related but not interchangeable spatial units.

## Practice / status terminology

- chattel slave;
- privileged chattel slave;
- freed person with conditional obligations;
- broader spectrum of legal/social statuses.

The packet does not compress these into one intensity score.

## Law / practice

Kamen's status analysis supports a legal-status distinction and also emphasizes differences between formal legal position and practical experience. Legal status therefore does not mechanically encode everyday labour conditions.

## Network / territorial

No network-participation claim is needed for the bounded conclusion. This packet is about territorial/social practice in the Athenian context.

## Geometry state

**point_or_named_place_resolved; historical_extent_unreviewed**

Athens can be identified as a historical place, but this experiment has not reviewed a c. 400 BCE urban/polity polygon. No extent is inferred from the place label.

## Contrary / limiting evidence

The main limiting evidence is internal to the scholarship: formal slave status did not produce one single lived condition. The primary political text is also rhetorically interested rather than a neutral social survey.

## Access / language limitation

This pass used English-language specialist scholarship and an English translation of the ancient text. It did not exhaust Greek epigraphy, papyri, archaeology or non-English scholarship.

## Coverage confidence

**High for institutional presence; low for numerical prevalence; medium for exact spatial extent.**

## Portability pressure

This case demonstrates that source density must remain separate from prevalence and that **source role / claim fitness** is material: a rhetorical primary text and specialist status synthesis support different propositions.
