# EXP-03 case 5 — Zanzibar Town / port, c. 1850 CE

**State:** experimental research packet
**Target type:** port/city on Zanzibar island within the Sultan's African dominions
**Anchor:** c. 1850 CE
**Research outcome:** bounded_supported
**Canonical effect:** none

## Bounded proposition

Specialist scholarship supports urban and plantation slavery in nineteenth-century Zanzibar, with the 1840s–1850s falling in a period of intense slave importation and a large enslaved population.

The 1845 British–Muscat agreement, effective 1 January 1847, prohibited export of slaves from the Sultan's African dominions and import into his Asian possessions, while explicitly exempting transport between specified ports within his African dominions, including Zanzibar and Pemba, from British seizure under that agreement.

The c. 1850 case therefore demonstrates a direct law/practice/network split: a legal restriction on external export coexisted with territorial slavery and permitted intra-dominion transport under the treaty framework.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- infer territorial prevalence from trade-volume or population counts;
- treat the 1845 treaty as abolition of slavery within Zanzibar;
- treat a prohibition on export as proof that the trade ceased in practice;
- infer Zanzibar Town practice automatically for all of Zanzibar, Pemba or the mainland;
- treat every vessel movement as evidence of a territorial practice claim at every connected port.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus:** Zanzibar Town/urban slavery and Zanzibar island/system; treaty applies to the Sultan's African dominions and explicitly names a wider coastal zone.
**Maximum defensible inference:** slavery was present in Zanzibar around 1850 and the legal regime restricted some external trade while preserving a defined intra-African-dominion transport exception.

## Practice / status terminology

- urban slavery;
- domestic slavery;
- plantation slavery;
- slave trade / transport.

These are separate relations and should not be merged into one intensity value.

## Law / practice

The Hamerton agreement is a legal/network instrument, not evidence that territorial slavery ended. Specialist historical scholarship independently supports continuing slavery in Zanzibar during the anchor period.

## Network / territorial

This case requires both dimensions simultaneously:
- **territorial practice:** slavery in Zanzibar;
- **network participation:** slave import/transport/trade connections.

Neither derives mechanically from the other.

## Geometry state

A Zanzibar Town point and island land shape do not establish the historical jurisdiction of the Sultanate or the spatial scope of every slavery claim.

## Contrary / limiting evidence

The treaty itself is limiting evidence against any simplistic anti-slave-trade-treaty-equals-abolition narrative because its scope was partial and its Article III exception is explicit.

## Access / language limitation

This pass used English-language specialist scholarship and English diplomatic treaty text. It did not independently review Arabic/Omani administrative sources or the full Zanzibar archival record.
