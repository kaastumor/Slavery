# EXP-03 case 2 — Mamluk Cairo, c. 1300 CE

**State:** experimental research packet
**Target type:** city within a wider sultanate
**Anchor:** c. 1300 CE
**Research outcome:** bounded_supported
**Canonical effect:** none

## Bounded proposition

Specialist scholarship supports slavery as a multi-form institution in Mamluk Egypt around the c. 1300 anchor, including military slavery as well as domestic and labouring slavery. Cairo-specific scholarship documents Frankish captives in the city after the fall of Acre in 1291 and their forced labour on Cairo building sites.

The Cairo packet therefore supports territorial practice in the city, while also requiring a distinction between the elite military-mamluk institution, ordinary enslaved labour/domestic service and war captivity.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- treat “Mamluk” as synonymous with every enslaved person in Cairo;
- infer city-wide prevalence from military recruitment or one captive population;
- infer Egyptian-wide territorial prevalence from Cairo evidence alone;
- collapse slave import networks into evidence of the same practice in every origin or transit location.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus:** Cairo for the Frankish-captive forced-labour evidence; Mamluk Egypt for the broader slavery structure.
**Maximum defensible inference:** multiple forms of slavery were institutionally present in Mamluk Egypt and at least some coerced/captive labour is directly localized to Cairo close to 1300.

## Practice / status terminology

- mamluk / military slave;
- domestic servant/slave;
- labouring slave;
- captive / forced labour.

These categories overlap historically but are not interchangeable.

## Law / practice

This packet does not rely on a single legal rule. It is practice/institution evidence assembled from specialist historical synthesis and Cairo-specific captive history.

## Network / territorial

Import and origin networks are central to the Mamluk system, but they remain separate from the territorial claim. A person's foreign origin does not itself establish practice at the place of origin.

## Geometry state

**city_point_resolved; historical_urban_extent_unreviewed; sultanate_extent_separate**

The Cairo place can be located; neither the c. 1300 urban footprint nor the Mamluk Sultanate polygon is silently inferred in this experiment.

## Contrary / limiting evidence

The term “military slavery” can draw attention to the elite mamluks and thereby obscure lower-status domestic/labouring enslaved people. The packet therefore refuses a single Mamluk-slavery archetype.

## Access / language limitation

This pass relied on English-language scholarship. It did not independently re-read the Arabic chronicles, documentary material or archival editions underlying the specialist studies.

## Coverage confidence

**High for institutional presence; high for Cairo-specific captive/forced-labour presence; low for prevalence; medium for exact city extent.**

## Portability pressure

A portable packet must preserve **practice subtype** and **evidence locus versus inference extent**. A single “slavery present” field would materially overstate what the Cairo-specific source establishes.
