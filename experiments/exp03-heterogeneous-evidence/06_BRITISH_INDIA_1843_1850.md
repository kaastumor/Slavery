# EXP-03 case 6 — British India, 1843–1850 CE

**State:** experimental research packet
**Target type:** large changing colonial jurisdiction
**Anchor interval:** 1843–1850 CE
**Research outcome:** legal_event_supported; continuing_practice_supported_regionally; politywide_uniformity_not_supported
**Canonical effect:** none

## Bounded proposition

Act V of 1843 removed the enforceability in East India Company courts of rights claimed as property in another person and prohibited public officers from selling people or compulsory labour on the ground of slavery. The act did not itself declare all enslaved people free.

Specialist scholarship therefore characterizes the measure more accurately as **delegalization** than immediate universal emancipation and documents the continuation of slavery/coercive relations after 1843 in parts of British India.

The defensible 1843–1850 claim is consequently split:
- a legal-status change is strongly evidenced across East India Company territories covered by the act;
- continued slavery/coercive practice is supported regionally;
- one uniform post-1843 practice state for the whole of “British India” is not established by this packet.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- equate Act V with immediate disappearance of slavery;
- infer uniform practice across the very large and changing colonial jurisdiction;
- relabel all caste dependence, debt bondage or later indenture as slavery;
- use a modern India boundary or a single timeless British-India polygon;
- infer practice from legal text alone.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Legal evidence locus:** territories of the East India Company named by the act.
**Practice evidence locus:** heterogeneous regional contexts synthesized by specialist scholarship.
**Maximum defensible inference:** delegalization occurred in 1843, while coercive/slavery relations continued in at least some regions afterward.

## Practice / status terminology

Indian slavery was heterogeneous and embedded in varied social, agrarian, domestic and caste contexts. The packet does not force those relations into the Atlantic plantation model.

## Law / practice

This is the strongest law/practice stress case in EXP-03.

The legal source establishes what courts/public officers could no longer enforce. It does not establish compliance, immediate emancipation or the absence of continuing coercive relations.

## Network / territorial

Indentured migration and wider imperial labour networks are analytically adjacent but are not automatically treated as slavery or as evidence for territorial slavery in every sending/receiving place.

## Geometry state

**jurisdiction_identity_resolved_at_label_level; historical_extent_unresolved_and_time_sensitive**

“British India” changed territorially and administratively. No modern-state proxy or single polygon is silently adopted for 1843–1850.

## Contrary / limiting evidence

The legal act is itself a counterexample to using legal status as a proxy for social practice. The specialist literature also warns that Indian slavery took regionally varied forms that do not map cleanly onto one familiar Atlantic model.

## Access / language limitation

This pass used English-language legislation/parliamentary material and English-language specialist scholarship. It did not exhaust regional-language records or every Company jurisdiction.

## Coverage confidence

**High for the 1843 legal change; high that practice did not end everywhere immediately; low for a single uniform 1843–1850 practice characterization across all British India; low for a single historical geometry.**
