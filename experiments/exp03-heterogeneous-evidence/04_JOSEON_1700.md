# EXP-03 case 4 — Joseon Korea, c. 1700 CE

**State:** experimental research packet
**Target type:** polity with internally heterogeneous status relations
**Anchor:** c. 1700 CE
**Research outcome:** bounded_supported_with_material_classification_dispute
**Canonical effect:** none

## Bounded proposition

Document-based scholarship supports the existence around the late seventeenth and early eighteenth centuries of privately held *nobi*, purchase/sale of *nobi*, status redemption, and owner-controlled status relations in Joseon Korea.

However, the historiography materially disputes whether the entire *nobi* institution should be classified as “slavery,” “serfdom,” or differentiated into subtypes. The packet therefore supports concrete ownership/status practices without assigning one universal slavery label to every *nobi* relationship.

## Required abstention

Do not:
- label every *nobi* as a chattel slave;
- infer that Joseon as a whole was a “slave society” from population estimates or one historiographical school;
- collapse *ipyeok-nobi*, *napkong-nobi*, private *nobi* and other subtypes;
- infer prevalence from the number of surviving household registers or sale documents;
- project later eighteenth/nineteenth-century self-sale patterns backward to exactly 1700 without temporal qualification.

## Evidence locus / inference extent

**Evidence locus:** Joseon legal/social order and documented private *nobi* transactions/management in specific local/family archives.
**Maximum defensible inference:** coercive, inheritable/transferable status relations and documented sale/purchase practices existed in Joseon around the anchor period; their umbrella classification is contested and subtype-sensitive.

## Practice / status terminology

Historical term: **nobi (奴婢)** with material internal distinctions.

**Mapping status:** disputed at umbrella level.

This packet deliberately separates:
- evidence of ownership/sale/status control;
- the analytical question whether every subtype equals slavery;
- the stronger societal characterization “slavery society.”

## Law / practice

Studies of redemption and government control show legal/status rules; sale documents show practices. Neither dimension alone is treated as a complete description of Joseon social reality.

## Contrary / limiting evidence

The Lee critique is not merely a minor caveat: it contests the scope of “slavery” classification and differentiates *nobi* subtypes. The packet therefore cannot honestly collapse the evidence into one unqualified slavery-present proposition.
