# EXP-13 internal adversarial replay

**Target:** Tōdai-ji — 800 CE  
**Frozen provisional outcome attacked:** `BOUNDED_SUPPORTED`  
**Review status:** internal critique only; not independent review

## Attack 1 — the strongest slave registers are around 751/756, too early for 800

**Finding:** that objection would defeat a continuity-only claim, but the packet has a
near-anchor bridge.

`Nihon Kōki` records a Tōdai-ji petition in **792** to free named `奴` into `良`
status.

That is eight years before the anchor.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES** for near-anchor institutional status presence.

The earlier registers are background, not the sole temporal basis.

## Attack 2 — translating `奴/奴婢` as slave is anachronistic inflation

**Finding:** a literal graph translation alone would be insufficient.

Here, the 792 event records a status transition `従良`. Historical lexical references
define this as releasing `奴婢` / other `賤民` into `良民`.

Jinno's specialist monograph treats ancient Japanese `奴婢`, Tōdai-specific service
and Tōdai slave registers as part of the `良賤` status system.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES WITH CATEGORY LIMIT**.

Use “legally non-free / servile-slave `奴/奴婢` status,” not a claim of perfect
equivalence to every comparative chattel-slavery system.

## Attack 3 — the 792 event proves emancipation, so perhaps no slaves remained by 800

**Finding:** the event proves at least near-anchor presence, not exact-800 headcount.

The packet does not infer that Hiro-mae or the same people remained unfree in 800 after
being emancipated.

It infers that Tōdai retained legally non-free persons as late as 792, making
institutional presence **around the 800 horizon** supportable.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES**, but no exact-year roster or prevalence claim.

## Attack 4 — the 750 donation of about 200 proves a large slave workforce in 800

**Finding:** no.

The eighth-century `奴婢` population was declining, and the 789 reform accelerated
that transition.

No evidence in this packet shows the original 200-person scale persisted to 800.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** scale continuity.

## Attack 5 — Tōdai construction was done by slaves

**Finding:** not demonstrated.

Specialist construction research identifies paid labourers, skilled craftspeople and
corvée workers and explicitly treats slave participation as a research gap.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** construction=slave inference.

The positive claim is independent of the construction workforce.

## Attack 6 — corvée and temple service can be rolled into the same unfree category

**Finding:** no.

Corvée/service obligations and `奴婢` legal status remain separate unless a specific
source connects them.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** status collapse.

## Attack 7 — later Tōdai Yōroku descriptions give exact slave duties

**Finding:** unsafe.

Imperial Household Agency source criticism notes that some later Yōroku duty
descriptions are retrospective inference and do not themselves prove Nara-period slave
guarding.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** later-duty back-projection.

## Attack 8 — Tōdai estate maps show where slave labour occurred

**Finding:** no.

Estate/property geography is not labour-status geography.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** geometry inference.

## Attack 9 — two catalogs of Tōdai slave registers are independent corroboration

**Finding:** no.

The Cultural Heritage Database and Historiographical Institute records are modern
catalogs of related source material.

**Disposition:** one documentary family for independence assessment.

## Attack 10 — this merely repeats Nālandā/Shaolin/Karnak inconclusive institution cases

**Finding:** no.

The methodological discriminator succeeds here because Tōdai has a target-specific,
near-anchor status-changing event. The evidence bridge absent in the earlier cases is
present.

This does not prove that institution cases are generally easier; it shows what kind of
evidence can legitimately cross the target/anchor boundary.

**Disposition:** method signal survives.

## Final adversarial disposition

**BOUNDED_SUPPORTED — NEAR-ANCHOR TŌDAI-SPECIFIC `奴/奴婢` STATUS PRESENCE.**

Supported:
- Tōdai-specific slave-register tradition;
- named Tōdai `奴` in a 792 official chronicle event;
- formal emancipation into `良`;
- specialist institution-specific `奴婢` scholarship.

Not supported:
- exact-800 headcount;
- persistence of the 750 scale;
- all construction/estate labour as slave;
- exact duties of the 792 people;
- later shōen continuity;
- later Yōroku duties as contemporary fact;
- prevalence/intensity;
- practice geometry.

The strongest overturning evidence would be source-critical scholarship showing that
the 792 Tōdai entry has been misattributed, materially mistranslated, or does not
represent the `良賤` status transition currently understood.
