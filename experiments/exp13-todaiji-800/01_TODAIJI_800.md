# Tōdai-ji — 800 CE

Target: `R1:N:todaiji_800`. Frozen frame: **institution_estate**.  
Research stage: **researched_internal**.  
Research outcome: **BOUNDED_SUPPORTED — Tōdai-ji-specific legally non-free `奴/奴婢` status is supported close to the 800 anchor; labour role, scale and estate-wide prevalence remain unresolved**.  
EXP-13 under issue #282; no R1 reviewed-state change, canonical update, P-level,
geometry promotion, cumulative-candidate integration or publication.

## Bounded synthesis

Tōdai-ji differs materially from earlier institution-frame cases such as Nālandā,
Shaolin and Karnak because the reviewed packet contains a direct near-anchor
status-changing event tied to the institution.

The strongest evidence chain is:

1. Tōdai-ji preserves an eighth-century `東大寺奴婢見来帳` / Tōdai-ji slave-register
   document dated 751/756 in Japan's Cultural Heritage Database.
2. A Historiographical Institute record separately identifies a Tōdai-ji `奴婢帳`
   from Tenpyō Shōhō 3 (751).
3. `Nihon Kōki` records that in **792**, Tōdai-ji's three administrators petitioned
   to release named `奴`, including Hiro-mae, into `良` status. The petition recalls
   the earlier imperial donation of `奴婢等` to Kinkōmyōji/Tōdai's predecessor.
4. The historical term `従良` is defined in standard Japanese historical reference
   works as freeing `奴婢` or other `賤民` into `良民`.
5. Jinno Kiyokazu's specialist monograph on ancient Japanese `奴婢` contains dedicated
   chapters on **forms of service of slaves at Tōdai-ji** and the Tōdai-ji slave
   registers.

The maximum defensible conclusion is therefore:

> **Tōdai-ji retained persons in a legally non-free `奴/奴婢` status very close to the
> 800 CE anchor. A 792 official chronicle entry records Tōdai-ji administrators
> successfully petitioning to free named temple `奴` into `良` status. Earlier
> Tōdai-specific slave registers and specialist scholarship establish the institutional
> background. This supports the presence of a slave/unfree status at Tōdai-ji around
> the anchor, but not the proposition that all temple, construction or estate labour
> was enslaved, nor any prevalence, workforce-size or practice-geometry claim.**

No P-level is assigned.

## The 792 status-changing event

The decisive temporal bridge is `Nihon Kōki`, Enryaku 11 (792), twelfth month.

The entry reports that Tōdai-ji's `三綱` petitioned concerning `奴広前等`. It refers
back to the earlier donation of `奴婢等` to Kinkōmyōji and requests that the named
people be `従良`; the request was granted.

A modern source edition from Kogakkan's Historiographical Institute summarizes the
event as releasing Tōdai-ji's Kinkōmyōji slave Hiro-mae and others into `良` status.

This event is only eight years before the selected anchor.

It securely supports:
- Tōdai institutional ownership/control context;
- named persons explicitly classified as `奴`;
- a formal transition from non-free status to `良`;
- persistence of the status category at Tōdai close to 800.

It does **not** establish:
- the exact number of Tōdai `奴婢` in 800;
- the work performed by the named 792 people;
- whether every earlier donated slave remained at the temple;
- estate-wide or workforce-wide prevalence.

## What `従良` establishes

Kotobank's historical dictionaries define `従良` as freeing `奴婢` or other
`賤民` and making them `良民`.

The entry itself cites a 750 Tōdai/Kinkōmyōji-related administrative document as an
early example.

This matters because the 792 event is not classified as slavery simply by translating
the graph `奴` literally. The status change is independently intelligible within the
`良/賤` legal-status system.

The packet therefore maps:
- `奴/奴婢` = legally non-free / servile-slave status in this documented context;
- `従良` = release into `良` status.

It does not claim that ancient Japanese `奴婢` was identical in all respects to every
later or comparative chattel-slavery system.

## Tōdai-specific eighth-century registers

Japan's Cultural Heritage Database lists `東大寺奴婢見来帳` as an Important Cultural
Property owned by Tōdai-ji and dates it to 751/756.

The University of Tokyo Historiographical Institute separately catalogs
`東大寺奴婢帳 天平勝宝三年三月`, DOI
`10.57459/hi.01.0s0_7_46`.

These records establish a target-specific documentary tradition for Tōdai `奴婢`.

They are not independent evidence families merely because two modern catalogs describe
related Tōdai slave-register material.

## Specialist interpretation

Jinno Kiyokazu's *Nihon kodai nuhi no kenkyū* (1993) is a specialist monograph devoted
to ancient Japanese `奴婢` and the `良賤` status system.

Its table of contents includes dedicated chapters:
- `東大寺における奴婢の用役形態` — forms of service of slaves at Tōdai-ji;
- `東大寺奴婢籍の編首奴婢` — slaves in Tōdai-ji slave registers;
- later discussion of `放賤従良`.

This is important claim-fitness evidence: Tōdai's `奴婢` are a recognized
institution-specific subject of specialist historical study, not a category inferred
solely from a catalog title.

The monograph is not fully open-access in the reviewed pass, so no detailed work-role
claim is taken from inaccessible chapter text.

## Scale and decline around the anchor

A Nipponica/Kotobank synthesis states that Tōdai-ji received about 200 people,
principally government `奴婢`, in 750 and that Tōdai/Shōsōin documents illuminate
their composition and labour.

The same synthesis emphasizes that private `奴婢` declined throughout the eighth
century and that the 789 rule making children of mixed `良賤` unions `良民`
accelerated the decline.

Therefore the earlier 200-person donation is **not** used as a workforce count for 800.

The 792 emancipation is evidence of near-anchor presence, not evidence that the
mid-century scale persisted unchanged.

## Construction labour is a separate category

Specialist work summarized by the Sainsbury Institute describes the Tōdai-ji
Construction Office as administering:
- skilled craftspeople;
- paid labourers;
- corvée workers.

The same report explicitly identifies the role of slaves in these construction
projects as a remaining research question.

Therefore:
- Tōdai construction labour ≠ slave labour by default;
- corvée ≠ `奴婢`;
- paid labour ≠ `奴婢`;
- the Great Buddha / temple-building project is not itself evidence for the positive
  status claim.

The positive claim rests on the Tōdai `奴婢` documents and 792 emancipation, not on
monumentality or construction scale.

## Later Tōdai Yōroku duties require source criticism

The later `Tōdai-ji Yōroku` describes the 750 donation of 200 `奴婢` and assigns
specific temple/treasury duties to their descendants.

An Imperial Household Agency Shōsōin study explicitly warns that at least some of these
duty descriptions are retrospective inference by the later compiler and do not by
themselves prove Nara-period slave guarding.

This packet therefore does not promote later Yōroku job descriptions into exact
eighth-century practice.

The earlier donation/status tradition remains relevant, but specific duties require
independent contemporary evidence.

## Estate evidence is not labour-status evidence

Tōdai-ji had eighth-century estates and surviving estate maps.

Those maps establish property/administrative geography only.

They do not establish:
- slave estate labour;
- where named `奴婢` worked;
- the spatial extent of unfree labour;
- a practice polygon.

The frozen `institution_estate` frame therefore remains an institutional frame with
estate extent unresolved for this claim.

## Law / practice

The packet does not rely on a generic ritsuryō category alone.

The strongest evidence is target-specific:
- Tōdai slave-register documents;
- a Tōdai administrative petition concerning named `奴`;
- an actual status transition granted in 792.

Generic `良賤` law is used only to interpret the documented status vocabulary.

That makes the result stronger than a legal-category-only inference.

## Evidence locus and inference extent

Evidence locus:
- Tōdai-ji / Kinkōmyōji institutional slave-register tradition;
- Tōdai administrators and named `奴` in 792;
- broader Nara-period `良賤` legal/status context.

Maximum defensible inference:

**Legally non-free `奴/奴婢` status was institutionally present at Tōdai-ji close to
800 CE.**

Required abstention:
- no exact-800 roster;
- no claim that 200 mid-century donated slaves remained in 800;
- no all-temple-labour slave classification;
- no construction-worker status inflation;
- no estate-resident status inflation;
- no prevalence or workforce share;
- no later shōen back-projection;
- no practice geometry.

## Geometry state

**Institutional locus qualified; slave/unfree practice geometry unresolved.**

Temple precinct, construction sites, estate maps and later shōen are different spatial
objects.

No practice polygon is authorized.

## Source dependency and access audit

- The Cultural Heritage Database and Historiographical Institute catalogs describe
  related Tōdai `奴婢` documentary material and are not counted as independent
  historical attestations merely because they are separate catalogs.
- The 792 `Nihon Kōki` event is one primary textual family. Kogakkan's source edition
  and modern digital transcriptions do not become multiple witnesses.
- Kotobank's `従良` entry is category/terminology control, not an independent Tōdai
  event.
- Jinno 1993 is the principal specialist institution-specific interpretive source;
  detailed chapter text was not openly accessible in this pass, so only verifiable
  bibliographic/TOC-level claims are used.
- The Nipponica `奴婢` synthesis is period/scale context and is not treated as
  independent of the broader Japanese `奴婢` historiography.
- The Sainsbury/Van Goethem construction-labour report is a negative/category control,
  not evidence for Tōdai slave presence.
- The Imperial Household Agency Shōsōin study is a source-critical control on later
  `Tōdai-ji Yōroku` descriptions.
- No exhaustive reading of all Shōsōin/Tōdai documents or Japanese-language specialist
  literature is claimed.

## Research disposition

**BOUNDED_SUPPORTED — near-anchor Tōdai-specific `奴/奴婢` status presence; labour
extent and prevalence unresolved.**

Supported:
- Tōdai-specific eighth-century `奴婢` documentation;
- formal Tōdai petition in 792 concerning named `奴`;
- emancipation into `良` status;
- specialist treatment of Tōdai `奴婢` organization/service as a real historical
  institution.

Not supported:
- exact 800 headcount;
- persistence of the 750 donation at original scale;
- slave status for all construction workers;
- slave status for all tenants/estate residents;
- later Yōroku duty descriptions as contemporary proof without control;
- later shōen continuity;
- practice geometry.

Reopen/deepen if:
- a near-800 Tōdai personnel/estate record identifies the work location or duties of
  specific `奴婢`;
- the full Jinno chapters or another specialist source materially narrows Tōdai
  `奴婢` work roles/scale at 790–810;
- source-critical work revises the dating/status interpretation of the 792 event.
