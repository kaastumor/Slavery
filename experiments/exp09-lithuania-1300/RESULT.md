## Result

Specialist Lithuanian historiography supports an unfree household/slavery category
inside developing grand-ducal court structures during the second half of the thirteenth
and fourteenth centuries, a range that directly spans the 1300 anchor.

The strongest bounded formulation is:

> Around the 1300 horizon, an unfree household/slave population
> (`nelaisvoji šeimyna; vergai; šeimynykščiai`) was present in at least part of the
> grand-ducal court/domain economy. Captives were one source of this unfree population.

Near-anchor raiding chronicles independently support capture/forced movement as a
mechanism, but do not by themselves establish final slave status.

## Limits

Do not infer:
- polity-wide prevalence or scale;
- a uniform legal regime across the Grand Duchy;
- exact 1300 statutory codification;
- that every captive became a slave;
- that ordinary dues/labor obligations were slavery;
- that later serfdom or the 1529 Statute applied unchanged at 1300;
- a territorial practice polygon.
