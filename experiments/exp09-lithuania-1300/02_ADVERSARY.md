# EXP-09 internal adversarial replay

**Target:** Grand Duchy of Lithuania — 1300 CE  
**Frozen outcome attacked:** `BOUNDED_SUPPORTED`  
**Review status:** internal critique only; not independent review

## Attack 1 — Is the positive result actually later serfdom projected backward?

**Finding:** no, if the claim is kept narrow.

Dubonis's positive terminology is explicitly placed in grand-ducal courts established
during the second half of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, directly spanning
1300. The same source distinguishes unfree court people from other labor- and tribute-
obligated populations.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES**, but later 1529 statutory categories and mature serfdom
must remain outside the anchor claim.

## Attack 2 — Is a fifteenth-century reconstruction being presented as an exact 1300 record?

**Finding:** the source says the scheme is supported both by concrete historical sources
and by better-documented fifteenth-century `valsčius` structure plus older analogies.
That is a historical reconstruction, not a dated 1300 roster.

**Disposition:** **REVISE / NARROW** the temporal wording to
**late-thirteenth/fourteenth-century institutional presence spanning the 1300 horizon**.
Do not claim an exact-year administrative event.

## Attack 3 — Does raid captivity prove slavery?

**Finding:** no.

Jeroschin gives a near-anchor 1301–1302 capture episode, but Nikžentaitis shows captive
treatment changed substantially over 1283–1409 and that later economic exploitation
should not be generalized backward.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** captive = slave as a default rule. The raid narrative is
context/mechanism only. The positive territorial claim rests on Dubonis's separately
identified `nelaisvoji šeimyna / vergai`.

## Attack 4 — Are tribute and labor obligations being inflated into slavery?

**Finding:** no in the revised packet.

Dubonis lists dues, transport and collective labor for local `valsčius` people and
separately labels court populations as free, partly free and unfree. The target row
does not convert ordinary obligations into slave status.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES**.

## Attack 5 — Does one court structure justify the whole polity?

**Finding:** no.

Direct grand-ducal control was uneven. The same synthesis explicitly notes regional
variation, including limited/absent direct ducal court structures in some lands.

**Disposition:** **REVISE / NARROW** inference extent to institutional presence in at
least part of the grand-ducal domain/court economy. No polity-wide prevalence.

## Attack 6 — Are historical categories being flattened?

**Finding:** the terms overlap but are not identical.

`nelaisvoji šeimyna`, `vergai`, `šeimynykščiai` and `belaisviai` are preserved
separately. VLE/Jurginis describes a transition from patriarchal slavery into an unfree
household; Dambrauskaitė shows later formalization. Neither warrants a timeless single
category.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES WITH CATEGORY LIMIT**.

## Attack 7 — False source independence

**Finding:**
- Jeroschin substantially follows Peter of Dusburg: one chronicle chain.
- VLE explicitly depends on Jurginis for the patriarchal-slavery category history.
- Other Orbis excerpts from the 2011 Baronas–Dubonis–Petrauskas volume would belong
  to the same synthesis family.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES** because the positive result does not depend on counting
these as multiple independent attestations. Dependency is recorded.

## Final adversarial disposition

**BOUNDED_SUPPORTED — NARROWED TO INSTITUTIONAL PRESENCE.**

The evidence supports a positive bounded claim around 1300 for an unfree
household/slavery category inside developing grand-ducal court structures.

It does **not** support:
- exact-year statutory precision;
- whole-polity prevalence;
- every captive becoming a slave;
- ordinary feudal dues as slavery;
- direct continuity into later serfdom;
- a practice polygon.

The strongest overturning evidence would be specialist scholarship showing that
Dubonis's `nelaisvoji šeimyna / vergai` reconstruction cannot validly reach the
late-thirteenth/early-fourteenth-century ducal court system, or that the terminology
has been materially mistranslated/misclassified here.
