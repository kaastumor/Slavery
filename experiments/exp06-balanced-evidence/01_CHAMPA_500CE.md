# Chámpa — 500 CE

Target: R1:L:500:F. Frozen frame: **polity**.
Research stage: **under_review**.
Research outcome: **unassessed_frame_ambiguous_with_near_anchor_status_evidence**.
Experimental packet, 2026-09-24; no R1 reviewed-state change, canonical update, P-level,
geometry promotion or publication.

## Bounded synthesis

The first EXP-06 case exposes a target-frame problem before it exposes an evidence
shortage.

Specialist work on early central Vietnam does not securely support treating “Chámpa”
at 500 CE as one already-coherent polity equivalent to the later Campā kingdom.
Schweyer reconstructs a mosaic in which Linyi and the Thu Bồn/Xitu sphere were distinct
early kingdoms; Southworth describes multiple emerging central-Vietnamese polities such
as Linyi during the first half of the first millennium. The name **Campā** itself first
appears in local epigraphy only in 658 CE.

This does not mean slavery/dependency evidence is absent. Near the anchor there are
several bounded signals:

- the *Nan Qi Shu* preserves a 484 petition from the Funan ruler Jayavarman saying
  that a slave named Jiu Chouluo fled him, overthrew Linyi and made himself king;
- fifth-century inscription C.41 at Chợ Dinh contains the Sanskrit phrase
  śivo dāso baddhyate. Older editions translated this as a slave bound to a post,
  whereas the current DHARMA edition renders it more cautiously as a
  **servant ... bound**;
- earlier Chinese traditions about Fan Wen describe a person captured/sold into
  slavery who later entered Linyi and became its ruler; modern specialist synthesis
  uses this as evidence for slave trading in the coastal trading world;
- the *Song Shu* records that, before the 446 Chinese campaign, Linyi's ruler offered
  to return Rinan households he had carried off. That is evidence of captive removal,
  not by itself proof of slave status.

These strands support a real **regional/early-polity coercion and status problem**.
They do not yet support a single territorial slavery designation for the frozen
“Chámpa — 500 CE” polity.

No P-level is assigned.

## Frame and chronology

The anchor sits in a historiographically contested transition.

Schweyer's reconstruction distinguishes Linyi, an early polity in the Thu Bồn/Xitu
sphere associated with Bhadravarman, and later epigraphic Campā, whose name appears
explicitly only in the seventh century.

Vickery likewise treats the equation of Linyi and Champa as a major unresolved
historiographical problem. Southworth's current archaeological overview speaks of
**emerging polities** in central Vietnam rather than assuming one seamless early
Champa state.

Therefore the frozen target remains in EXP-06, but its effective frame is
**historically ambiguous at the anchor**. No silent replacement with Linyi or Xitu is
made.

## C.41 at Chợ Dinh — fifth century

DHARMA's current digital edition identifies C.41 as fifth-century CE and gives
śivo dāso baddhyate.

This is especially important because the translation history itself demonstrates the
category problem:
- Bergaigne: a propitiatory dāsa is attached/bound;
- Majumdar: “Śiva, the slave is bound (to the post)”;
- current Griffiths/DHARMA translation: “The servant, who is favorable, is bound.”

The modern edition does **not** authorize a universal equation dāsa = chattel slave.
Nor does the inscription by itself establish a population-wide institution or human
sacrifice. It is a bounded fifth-century status/ritual attestation whose social meaning
requires specialist interpretation.

Its political attribution is also not allowed to repair the frozen frame automatically:
a Campā epigraphic corpus repository is not proof that every fifth-century find belonged
to one polity called Champa.

## 484 Funan petition about Linyi

The *Nan Qi Shu* preserves Jayavarman's petition delivered by Nāgasena in Yongming
year 2 (484).

The petition says Jayavarman had a **slave** named Jiu Chouluo who fled, joined
rebellion, conquered Linyi and proclaimed himself king. Momoki's specialist discussion
retains the same report when analyzing Linyi's relations with Funan and the Chinese
court.

This is unusually close to the 500 anchor, but it is still bounded:
- it is a claim made by a hostile neighboring ruler asking China for military help;
- it attests the petitioner's characterization of Jiu Chouluo's prior status;
- it does not prove the prevalence or legal structure of slavery inside Linyi;
- repeating the story in later scholarship does not create independent attestations.

## Earlier Fan Wen chain

The *Shuijing zhu* and related Chinese textual traditions describe Fan Wen as having
been captured/sold into slavery and eventually entering Linyi before later becoming its
king.

Li Tana argues that this episode is evidence for a long-running maritime slave-trade
environment involving Jiaozhou and merchants linked to Linyi.

The event belongs to the fourth century, not 500 CE. It is therefore predecessor/network
context, not an anchor-period territorial observation.

No modern nationality is inferred for Fan Wen or the merchants from the historical
labels used in later texts.

## Captive removal in 446

The *Song Shu* account of the 446 war says the Linyi ruler offered to restore
**Rinan households he had carried off**.

This supports captive population removal by Linyi near the fifth-century context.
It does not establish that every removed household member entered slave status, nor
does it quantify a slave population.

Captivity and slavery remain separate claims.

## Category controls

Relevant terms/claims:
- Sanskrit **dāsa** in C.41 — currently translated “servant” by DHARMA; older
  scholarship rendered “slave”;
- Chinese **奴** in the 484 Funan petition — explicitly slave in the petitioner's
  status claim, but politically interested testimony;
- households carried off from Rinan — captive/removal evidence, status afterward
  unknown;
- later Champa inscriptional “slave”/temple-personnel evidence — chronologically
  downstream and not back-projected to 500.

The packet therefore rejects a single normalized slave label for all four.

## Law / practice

No near-anchor comprehensive legal regime for slavery was recovered.

C.41 is ritual/status epigraphy, the 484 petition is diplomatic narrative, and the 446
record concerns military captive removal. Together they show bounded practices/status
claims but not one codified law uniformly applied across a “Champa” territory.

## Network / territory

The Fan Wen tradition is partly a **maritime slave-trade/network** story.
The 484 petition is a cross-polity status/political story.
The 446 Rinan episode is a captive-removal story.

None may automatically become a uniform territorial practice claim for all central
Vietnamese polities around 500.

## Evidence locus and inference extent

Evidence loci include:
- Linyi in Chinese historical traditions;
- Chợ Dinh/Phú Yên for C.41;
- Jiaozhou–Linyi maritime connections in the Fan Wen tradition;
- Rinan–Linyi conflict zone in 446.

Maximum defensible inference:

**Around the fifth-century central Vietnamese political landscape there is bounded
evidence for slave-status claims, captive removal, and an earlier maritime slave-trade
connection, but the frozen “Chámpa — 500 CE” polity frame is too historically
ambiguous to convert those strands into one territorial classification.**

## Geometry state

**unresolved; frozen polity geometry not defensible at this stage.**

Do not back-project later Champa boundaries, merge Linyi/Xitu/other coastal polities,
or use the geographic spread of Campā inscriptions as one 500 CE practice polygon.

## Source dependency and access audit

- *Nan Qi Shu* 484 petition is one primary textual chain; Momoki and later historians
  discussing the escaped-slave claim are interpretation layers on that chain.
- C.41 is a separate epigraphic source chain. DHARMA's current edition supersedes
  older translation certainty but preserves earlier readings in its apparatus.
- Fan Wen's slavery story appears through later Chinese compilations and modern
  synthesis; repeated retellings are not independent events.
- *Song Shu* 446 captive-household account is a separate source tradition for removal,
  not slave status.
- Schweyer, Southworth and Vickery overlap the early-central-Vietnam evidence base but
  provide materially different frame interpretations.

Chinese source text and modern English/French scholarship/editions were consulted.
No independent Classical Chinese, Sanskrit or Old Cham philological edition was
performed.

## Reopening / resolution condition

Do not “fix” the case by renaming it inside EXP-06.

A later target-identity review may decide whether the frozen registry should distinguish
Linyi, Xitu/Thu Bồn and later Campā at this anchor. That would be a separate identity/
method decision and, if canonical, must follow the Decisions Log process.

Historical classification can be revisited if specialist work can securely tie
C.41 or another near-anchor slavery/status attestation to the same defensible political
entity intended by the frozen target.

## Adversarial findings

- **REJECT:** Linyi = Champa = one polity at 500 as an automatic identity equation.
- **REJECT:** C.41 proves a slave sacrifice; current edition deliberately uses the
  more cautious “servant ... bound.”
- **REJECT:** a former slave ruling Linyi in 484 proves systemic Linyi slavery.
- **REJECT:** carried-off Rinan households are automatically slaves.
- **REJECT:** later Champa slave-trade evidence can be backdated to 500.
- **FRAME DEFECT / UNDER REVIEW:** the frozen polity label is not sufficiently secure
  for a territorial classification.
- **PORTABLE CONTRACT SURVIVES:** frame ambiguity, competing status translation,
  captive/slave separation and network/territorial limits remain representable without
  schema extension.
