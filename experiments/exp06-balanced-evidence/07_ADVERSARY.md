# EXP-06 adversarial review

Review type: internal tranche-level red team after all six frozen cases.
Canonical v0.6.1 remains unchanged.

## Attack 1 — Chámpa should have stopped the tranche

The protocol says revise/stop if a frozen frame is historically invalid at the anchor.
Chámpa at 500 exposed serious Linyi/Xitu/later-Campā identity ambiguity.

**Response:** the current evidence establishes a **frame ambiguity**, not enough to
declare the frozen row definitively invalid. The case therefore remains under review
with no territorial classification, and the sample is not silently repaired. If later
identity review establishes that the row is invalid rather than ambiguous, that becomes
a target-registry issue outside EXP-06.

Disposition: **SURVIVES, with explicit unresolved target identity.**

## Attack 2 — Magadha launders later Buddhist texts into 500 BCE

Early Buddhist traditions about Bimbisāra/Ajātasattu are transmitted later, while the
absolute chronology of the Buddha and those kings is disputed.

**Response:** the packet does not label those narratives exact 500 BCE events. It
supports a mid-first-millennium Magadhan slavery/status context and keeps the exact
Haryanka-at-500 placement under review.

Disposition: **SURVIVES.**

## Attack 3 — Mayapán is being used as all Later Maya city-states

The strongest slavery/captive-sale interpretation is Mayapán-specific and depends in
part on retrospective ethnohistory.

**Response:** the portable row explicitly marks Mayapán as a member-locus and rejects
aggregate-wide uniformity. Archaeology independently strengthens warfare/captive
violence, but not slave legal status.

Disposition: **SURVIVES.**

## Attack 4 — the Yam is just ordinary taxation, not coerced labour

Station households sometimes received fiscal substitutions/privileges, so calling the
system coerced labour could overstate it.

**Response:** the evidence is not based merely on taxation. Designated households had
compulsory transport/service/resource duties; Tibetan evidence records severe burden
and household flight, and Ghazan's exact-anchor reform addresses abusive relay
exactions. The category is coerced public/service labour, not slavery.

Disposition: **SURVIVES, category boundary essential.**

## Attack 5 — Yaghan inconclusive is absence by another name

Later ethnography describes decentralized/local social organization and does not
foreground slavery.

**Response:** the case explicitly refuses that inference. Internal practice is
researched-inconclusive because the anchor predates dense documentary observation;
external/intergroup coercion remains positive but directional.

Disposition: **SURVIVES.**

## Attack 6 — Khmer 1300 just reuses Angkor 1200

The case was deliberately chosen to catch node→polity and 1200→1300 leakage.

**Response:** Zhou Daguan's 1296–1297 eyewitness account independently supports
household slavery at Angkor immediately before the anchor. K.273/K.908 are used only
as predecessor context and are not counted as new 1300 attestations.

Disposition: **SURVIVES strongly.**

## Attack 7 — Zhou's vivid numbers tempt an empire-wide high-intensity result

Zhou reports very large slaveholding counts and says only the poorest households had
none.

**Response:** those figures remain one observer's prevalence claims centered on the
capital. They are preserved as source claims but not converted into a population share,
P-level, or empire-wide practice intensity.

Disposition: **SURVIVES.**

## Attack 8 — source density is uneven and could bias positive cases

Khmer, Mongol and Mayapán have richer surviving evidence than Yaghan and early Champa.

**Response:** EXP-06 success was six honest research states, not equal source volume
or six positive classifications. Source count never determines intensity. Yaghan stays
inconclusive and Chámpa stays under review rather than being pushed toward a result.

Disposition: **SURVIVES.**

## Attack 9 — 'globally balanced' is overstated

Six cases cannot make the Atlas globally balanced, and the tranche excludes many
regions/periods.

**Response:** correct. 'Balanced' describes the **selection direction** relative to
Atlantic/documentation-density bias, not achieved global representativeness.

Disposition: **REVISE LANGUAGE — say balanced evidence-growth tranche, not globally
representative tranche.**

## Attack 10 — portable contract may be surviving only because fields are verbose

The contract can store these cases, but long prose fields may hide future analytical
difficulty.

**Response:** that is a scaling/ergonomics issue, not a lost historical distinction.
EXP-06 found no repeated distinction that requires a schema/ontology extension.

Disposition: **SURVIVES; no schema change.**

## Tranche disposition

**SURVIVES.**
