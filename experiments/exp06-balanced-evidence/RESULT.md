## What changed belief

### 1. Target identity can be the limiting evidence problem

Chámpa shows that positive slavery/status evidence is not enough when the political
object itself is unstable at the anchor. The correct output can be 'evidence exists,
territorial classification withheld.'

### 2. Exact chronology and institutional presence can separate

Magadha shows the same pattern temporally: the social institution is better supported
than the exact dynastic cross-section.

### 3. One positive member-locus does not make an aggregate

Mayapán supports a strong Postclassic locus while preserving non-uniformity across
Later Maya city-states.

### 4. Coerced public/service labour must remain distinct from slavery

The Mongol Yam is a positive coerced-labour network without requiring slave status.
This materially validates the project's multidimensional approach.

### 5. Inconclusive internal practice can coexist with positive external coercion

Yaghan preserves the same directional distinction previously tested in Andaman without
turning missing internal evidence into absence.

### 6. The Khmer scope-transfer attack produced an independent anchor result

The Khmer 1300 case does not depend on projecting K.273/K.908 forward. Zhou Daguan's
1296–1297 eyewitness account independently supports household slavery at the capital,
while polity-wide extent remains withheld.
