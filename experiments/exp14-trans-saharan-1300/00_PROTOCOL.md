# EXP-14 protocol — Trans-Saharan trade network, 1300 CE

**Issue:** #290  
**Mode:** execution  
**Target:** `R1:N:trans_saharan_1300`  
**Anchor:** 1300 CE  
**Frozen frame:** `mobile_network`  
**Qualification state:** `QUALIFIED_C1_READY` via EXP-10  
**Qualified spatial object:** changing multi-route caravan network linking North Africa / Mediterranean with regions south of the Sahara; no single route or territorial polygon  
**Prior subject research:** none

## Question

What bounded slavery/coercion interpretation, if any, is supportable for the
Trans-Saharan trade network around 1300 CE?

## Existing qualification evidence

EXP-10 established only that a multi-route trans-Saharan exchange network is a valid
historical object around 1300. It did not establish slave trading, captive movement,
territorial slavery or trade volume.

## Strongest rival explanations / failure modes

A positive-looking result may be false if it depends on:
1. slavery in Mali, the Sahel or North Africa plus generic caravan trade;
2. one slave market or one caravan route standing for the network;
3. enslaved people at a court being treated as traded across the Sahara;
4. war captives being assumed to enter commerce;
5. later fifteenth–nineteenth-century slave-trade scale being projected backward;
6. gold/salt routes being assumed to carry enslaved people;
7. Arabic ethnonyms or status labels being normalized without context;
8. network participation being converted into territorial practice at every node;
9. modern route maps being treated as exact 1300 geometry;
10. source counts being converted into prevalence or volume.

## Evidence plan

Prioritize:
- specialist scholarship specifically on medieval trans-Saharan slave trade / movement
  around the thirteenth–fourteenth centuries;
- near-anchor Arabic travel/geographical/administrative texts that explicitly describe
  sale, transport, caravan movement, export/import or origin/destination of enslaved
  people;
- independent route/node evidence where it constrains rather than homogenizes the
  network.

Use broader slavery in Mali/North Africa only as context unless movement across the
network is explicit.

## Discriminator

### BOUNDED_SUPPORTED
Requires evidence that enslaved people were actually moved/traded through at least a
bounded part of the trans-Saharan network around the anchor, with route/node and time
limits explicit.

### RESEARCHED_INCONCLUSIVE
Use when near-anchor slavery and network connectivity are both supported but movement
of enslaved people through the network is not securely demonstrated.

### HOLD_EVIDENCE_ACCESS_OR_SCOPE
Use when decisive Arabic/source access or route/status ambiguity blocks responsible
synthesis.

## Mandatory separations

- territorial slavery vs network participation;
- capture vs trade/transfer;
- gift/retainer vs commercial movement;
- node evidence vs network inference;
- one route vs multi-route network;
- anchor-period evidence vs later trade scale;
- evidence locus vs route/inference extent.

## Qualification inheritance

EXP-10 limitation:
Do not collapse the network into Mali, Kanem, one caravan trail, one city chain or a timeless Sahara-wide route. Any later subject claim must identify the relevant route/node/evidence locus; map geometry is schematic unless separately evidenced.
