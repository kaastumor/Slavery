# EXP-14 internal adversarial replay

**Target:** Trans-Saharan trade network — 1300 CE  
**Provisional outcome attacked:** `BOUNDED_SUPPORTED`  
**Review status:** internal critique only; not independent review

## Attack 1 — Is this just “slavery in Mali + trans-Saharan trade = slave trade”?

**Finding:** no.

The decisive event is a caravan physically carrying 600 female slaves north from the
central Sudan toward Morocco in 1353. Haour and Prange independently frame a medieval /
fourteenth-century trans-Saharan slave-trading system.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES**.

## Attack 2 — 1353 is too late for a 1300 anchor

**Finding:** exact-year 1300 movement is not directly recovered.

The bridge is twofold:
- Haour's specialist system-level period spans ca. 750–1500;
- direct event evidence is mid-fourteenth century.

The correct claim is therefore a **medieval system spanning the anchor with direct
near-anchor fourteenth-century route evidence**, not an exact 1300 count/event.

**Disposition:** **NARROW AND SURVIVE**.

## Attack 3 — Does one 600-person caravan define the whole network?

**Finding:** no.

The event proves at least one northbound corridor participated. It does not establish
equal traffic on western, central or eastern routes.

**Disposition:** **REJECT network-wide uniformity**.

## Attack 4 — Can 600 be turned into an annual estimate?

**Finding:** no.

Some later scholarship speculates about regularity. The packet adopts no annualization.

**Disposition:** **REJECT count inflation**.

## Attack 5 — Is Taghaza slave labour being used as transport evidence?

**Finding:** no.

Taghaza establishes node-level slave labour in the same network environment but does
not establish origin, route movement or commercial transfer of those workers.

**Disposition:** **context only**.

## Attack 6 — Mansa Musa's famous slave entourage proves 1324 commercial traffic

**Finding:** the popular numbers and terminology are unstable across retellings and the
event is a royal pilgrimage, not a clean commercial caravan source.

**Disposition:** **REJECT as decisive proof**.

## Attack 7 — Later slave-trade organization is projected backward

**Finding:** Prange extends to the nineteenth century, Wright much later still.

Only their medieval/fourteenth-century claims are used; later scale, routes and
organization are not imported.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES WITH TEMPORAL FENCE**.

## Attack 8 — Network participation becomes territorial slavery everywhere

**Finding:** no.

Movement through Takedda/Tuat/Sijilmasa and slave labour at Taghaza do not establish
local slavery prevalence or law in every connected polity/node.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES**.

## Attack 9 — False source independence

**Finding:**
- Wright, El Hamel and modern retellings of the 600-person caravan ultimately depend on
  Ibn Battuta's one textual chain.
- Haour and Prange are independent specialist syntheses at a different evidentiary
  level.
- Guérin supports network connectivity, not slave movement.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES** because the packet does not count derivative versions of
Ibn Battuta as multiple independent events.

## Final adversarial disposition

**BOUNDED_SUPPORTED — NARROWED TO MEDIEVAL/FOURTEENTH-CENTURY NETWORK PARTICIPATION.**

Supported:
- an established medieval trans-Saharan slave-trading system spanning the selected
  anchor in specialist scholarship;
- direct northbound caravan movement of enslaved people in 1353;
- fourteenth-century slave-trade organization through Sudanic/Saharan/Maghrib
  entrepôts.

Not supported:
- exact-year 1300 volume;
- every route carrying slaves;
- annualization of 600;
- territorial slavery at every node;
- later trade scale;
- a fixed route polygon.

The strongest overturning evidence would be source-critical scholarship showing that
the 1353 caravan reading is materially wrong or that the medieval system-level
continuity used to span the anchor is not supportable.
