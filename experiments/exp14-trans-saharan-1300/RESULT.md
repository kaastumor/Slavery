## Result

The evidence supports actual medieval trans-Saharan movement/trade of enslaved people,
not merely slavery at connected nodes.

The strongest direct event is Ibn Battuta's 1353 northbound caravan from the central
Sudan toward Morocco carrying 600 female slaves.

Specialist work independently places:
- a trans-Saharan slave trade across approximately AD 750–1500;
- organized slave-trade routes from Sudanic Africa through Saharan/Sahelian entrepôts
  to the Maghrib from the fourteenth century.

Therefore the bounded target state is:

> **The medieval trans-Saharan network spanning the 1300 horizon participated in the
> movement/trade of enslaved people. Direct route evidence is strongest in the
> mid-fourteenth century; exact 1300 volume and all-route participation remain
> unresolved.**
