## Result

Around the 1600 horizon, Sámi communities in different northern jurisdictions were
subject to overlapping state fiscal and sovereignty claims.

The reviewed evidence supports:
- individual/community taxation under Swedish administration;
- Danish-Norwegian fiscal incorporation of Sea Sámi;
- overlapping Swedish/Russian and other state claims;
- Swedish punishment-backed enforcement of tax allegiance and controlled trade near
  1605;
- bounded capture/displacement of Sámi families in 1589 eastern-borderland conflict.

This is a **bounded coercive-state relation**, not a Sápmi-wide slavery classification.

The reviewed evidence does **not** establish:
- internal Sámi slavery/slaveholding at 1600;
- slave/property status for taxpayers;
- forced labour merely because Sámi worked for the Crown;
- that captured people became enslaved;
- later seventeenth-century mining corvée at the anchor.

## Why the compound result matters

A binary slavery field would mishandle this case.

The evidence package contains serious coercion:
- fiscal extraction;
- punishment-backed sovereignty;
- trade control;
- capture/displacement.

But those relations are not automatically slave status.

The project’s existing separation of actor direction, territorial/community scope,
status mapping, temporal applicability and inference extent is sufficient to express
the result without inventing a new ontology category.
