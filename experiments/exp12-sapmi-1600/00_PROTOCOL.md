# EXP-12 protocol — Sápmi / Sámi communities, 1600 CE

**Issue:** #285  
**Mode:** execution  
**Target:** `R1:N:sapmi_1600`  
**Anchor:** 1600 CE  
**Frozen frame:** `region_community`  
**Qualification state:** `QUALIFIED_C1_READY` via EXP-10  
**Prior subject research:** none

## Qualified historical object

EXP-10 established that Sámi communities are securely valid at the anchor using
1551–1600 tax/community evidence and historian-authored regional synthesis.

Frozen spatial meaning:
- multiple Sámi communities/siida;
- northern Fennoscandia and adjacent regions;
- overlapping/changing state taxation and claims;
- no single polity, jurisdiction or exact regional boundary.

This qualification is not evidence of slavery/coercion.

## Question

What bounded slavery/coercion interpretation, if any, is supportable for Sámi
communities in the Sápmi regional frame around 1600 CE?

## Strongest rival explanations / failure modes

A positive-looking result may be false if it depends on:
1. calling taxation, tribute, transport, mining, church or military obligations
   "slavery" without status evidence;
2. treating Sámi people enslaved/captured by outsiders as proof of internal Sámi
   slaveholding;
3. generalizing one siida/community or coastal/inland group to all Sámi communities;
4. merging Swedish, Danish-Norwegian and Russian jurisdictional evidence;
5. treating state colonial extraction as an indigenous internal institution;
6. back-projecting eighteenth-/nineteenth-century colonial policies to 1600;
7. turning retrospective ethnonyms or modern Sápmi geography into one historical
   jurisdiction;
8. using archive density/source count as prevalence;
9. treating debt, clientage, service or labor obligations as slave/property status
   without specialist interpretation.

## Evidence plan

Prioritize:
- specialist scholarship on Sámi social organization, state taxation/extraction,
  labor/service obligations, captivity and status around the late sixteenth/early
  seventeenth century;
- source-native or translated tax, legal, administrative and missionary evidence for
  bounded events/status/obligations;
- separate scholarship for Swedish, Danish-Norwegian and Russian contexts.

Primary/state sources may establish bounded obligations, captures, transfers,
terminology and jurisdiction. Specialist secondary scholarship must carry the
classification and continuity interpretation.

## Discriminator

### BOUNDED_SUPPORTED
Requires target-relevant evidence near 1600 for a clearly coercive/unfree/slavery
relationship with scope and actor direction explicit. This may be state-imposed,
external/intergroup or internal, but the dimension must be named.

### RESEARCHED_INCONCLUSIVE
Use when coercive extraction/captivity context is supported but slave/unfree status,
internal practice or anchor applicability is not securely established.

### HOLD_EVIDENCE_ACCESS_OR_SCOPE
Use when decisive linguistic/source access or unresolved actor/jurisdiction ambiguity
blocks a responsible synthesis.

## Mandatory separations

- internal Sámi practice vs state-imposed coercion;
- Swedish vs Danish-Norwegian vs Russian jurisdiction;
- tax/tribute vs compelled service/labor vs slave/property status;
- captivity vs final enslaved status;
- one community/siida vs regional frame;
- historical regional object vs modern Sápmi polygon;
- evidence locus vs inference extent.

## Qualification inheritance

EXP-10 limitation:
Use 'Sápmi / Sámi communities' as a retrospective regional research container, not a single society, polity, jurisdiction or exact historical polygon. Preserve community/siida and state-claim variation; modern Sápmi boundaries are not 1600 jurisdictional boundaries.
