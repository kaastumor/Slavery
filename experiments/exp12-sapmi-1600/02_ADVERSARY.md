# EXP-12 internal adversarial replay

**Target:** Sápmi / Sámi communities — 1600 CE  
**Frozen provisional outcome attacked:** `BOUNDED_SUPPORTED` for state-imposed coercive extraction; internal slavery unresolved  
**Review status:** internal critique only; not independent review

## Attack 1 — taxation is being relabelled slavery

**Finding:** that would be invalid.

The packet does not classify tax payment itself as slavery or slave status.

The bounded positive is **state-imposed coercive fiscal/jurisdictional extraction**,
supported especially where sovereignty/tax allegiance was backed by punishment threats
and controlled trade.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES WITH DIMENSION LABEL**.

No slavery label is applied to taxpayers.

## Attack 2 — ordinary Crown employment is being inflated into forced labour

**Finding:** no.

Wheelersburg records Sámi employed as Crown reindeer herders in 1605. The packet
preserves this as employment/service and uses it as a counterexample to status
inflation.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES**.

## Attack 3 — the word `leidang` proves military servitude

**Finding:** no.

Evjen & Hansen explicitly explain that the medieval military obligation had become an
annual tax. Its extension to Sea Sámi in the early seventeenth century is fiscal
incorporation, not evidence of compelled military service at the anchor.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** lexical inflation.

## Attack 4 — 1589 capture proves enslavement

**Finding:** no.

The Kitka study reports 33 Sámi families captured by Swedes and surviving families
fleeing violence.

The reviewed evidence does not establish sale, ownership, hereditary status or final
enslaved condition.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** capture = slavery. Preserve capture/displacement only.

## Attack 5 — later mining corvée proves forced labour at 1600

**Finding:** no.

Nasafjäll and comparable mining corvée are later seventeenth-century developments.
They show that explicit forced labour emerged under stronger Swedish state extraction,
but cannot be projected backward.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** temporal back-projection.

## Attack 6 — Swedish evidence is being generalized to all Sápmi

**Finding:** no after scope control.

The packet separates:
- Ume/Lule/Kemi Swedish contexts;
- Sea Sámi / Danish-Norwegian fiscal context;
- eastern borderland capture context;
- broader Swedish elite household service.

The regional target is a container for these bounded loci, not a claim of uniformity.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES**.

## Attack 7 — double/triple taxation means one especially severe Sápmi-wide regime

**Finding:** no.

Overlapping taxation primarily demonstrates competing sovereignty and jurisdiction.
Burden intensity varied by community, period and state.

No prevalence or intensity score is derived.

**Disposition:** **REJECT** homogenization.

## Attack 8 — absence of internal slave evidence proves egalitarian absence

**Finding:** no.

The packet found no secure internal Sámi slavery/slaveholding evidence at the anchor.
Modern scholarship on siida organization helps prevent invention of a hierarchy, but it
does not prove historical absence.

**Disposition:** **RESEARCHED-INCONCLUSIVE internal dimension**, not absence.

## Attack 9 — coerced domestic service in Swedish households proves internal slavery

**Finding:** no.

Nordin & Ojala’s evidence concerns Sámi people incorporated into elite Swedish
households, with chronologically scattered examples. Actor/locus direction is external.

**Disposition:** **external/broader-period context only**.

## Attack 10 — this is just colonial history, not coerced labour/slavery research

**Finding:** partially correct and therefore important.

The near-anchor positive is not a slave-status finding. It is a bounded **coercive
state-extraction/jurisdiction** finding. The project ontology explicitly separates
different coercive dimensions rather than forcing a binary slavery field.

The result is useful precisely because it prevents coercive taxation, sovereignty and
capture from being normalized into slavery.

**Disposition:** **SURVIVES** as a dimensionally bounded coercion result.

## Final adversarial disposition

**BOUNDED_SUPPORTED — STATE-IMPOSED COERCIVE EXTRACTION; INTERNAL SLAVERY UNRESOLVED.**

Supported near the 1600 horizon:
- overlapping state taxation;
- punishment-backed tax-allegiance enforcement;
- state-controlled trade/fiscal incorporation;
- bounded capture/displacement in eastern borderlands.

Not promoted:
- slave/property status for taxpayers or Crown workers;
- internal Sámi slaveholding;
- Sápmi-wide uniformity;
- later mining corvée;
- captured people becoming slaves;
- practice geometry.

The strongest overturning evidence would be a near-anchor community-specific source
or specialist study that securely identifies either:
1. internal Sámi slave/property status; or
2. compelled personal service by a state beyond fiscal obligation with clear locus and
   status.
