**Issue:** #198  
**Parent:** #183

**Stage:** pre-registered after red-team correction  
**Production implementation authorized:** no

## Why this reset exists

The prior wide-angle review was directionally strong: it already compared against boring baselines, challenged the full-platform identity and separated some project identities.

However, parts of its reasoning risked over-weighting competitor/standards overlap. The current post-MVP queue then became feature-first (#185–#187) before the project had reconstructed who has which job and what realistic alternatives they already use.

This reset corrects that ordering.

# Red team of the initial rerun plan

## Initial plan

The naive plan was:

1. rerun the entire old wide-angle review;
2. scan competitors and adjacent products;
3. classify every capability by novelty;
4. score opportunities;
5. prioritize feature experiments.

## Strongest attacks on that plan

### Attack 1 — framework theatre

A literal pass through every strategy/innovation framework for every feature would generate a large document without proportionate information gain.

**Correction:** use the frameworks as decision logic, not mandatory sections for every trivial capability.

### Attack 2 — false “customer” precision

The Atlas has internal methodological/release evidence, but no demonstrated external-user adoption.

Treating historians, educators, institutions or the public as proven customers would manufacture demand.

**Correction:** explicitly distinguish observed project users/workflows from hypothesized external segments. Mark missing demand evidence UNKNOWN.

### Attack 3 — commercial-model distortion

Pricing, TAM and business-model analysis may be useful eventually, but forcing them now would distort a research/public-history project whose operating model is not yet chosen.

**Correction:** economic/business-model analysis is conditional on a credible segment/value hypothesis.

### Attack 4 — starting from features reproduces the bias

If the reassessment begins with timeline/search/graph/comparison, it can only discover value around ideas already imagined.

**Correction:** reconstruct jobs and alternative workflows before deciding whether #185–#187 deserve to exist.

### Attack 5 — redoing already-settled technical work

The old review and R1 produced substantial evidence about provenance, temporal/spatial safeguards, non-absence semantics and release discipline.

Repeating those experiments would waste effort and risk treating mature internal evidence as uncertain merely because the strategy frame changed.

**Correction:** preserve internally demonstrated core capabilities; reassess their **external relative value**, not whether they technically work.

### Attack 6 — novelty can become a new vanity metric

The corrective brief attacks novelty bias, but a large novelty taxonomy could still make “what is unusual?” the organizing question.

**Correction:** novelty is a secondary descriptor. User/job, alternative and behavioural consequence come first.

### Attack 7 — competitor documents are not user evidence

Vendor documentation/marketing can establish feature presence, workflow design and positioning, but not actual satisfaction or switching behavior.

**Correction:** mark competitor claims accordingly and seek behavioural/community/usage evidence where it materially matters.

### Attack 8 — one generic “historian” segment is too broad

A curator building a dataset, a scholar comparing cases, an expert reviewer auditing evidence and a public explorer have materially different jobs and switching costs.

**Correction:** segment by job/circumstance, not by one broad profession label.

# Reassessment — desk/repository pass

## 1. Executive reassessment

The previous wide-angle review **did not simply reject ideas because competitors exist**.

Several of its largest conclusions remain strongly supported by independent project evidence:
- H2 found corpus/method material improvement over the strongest baseline in only 1/3 difficult historical tasks, while the thin view improved the corpus/method in 0/3;
- COV-004 found the bespoke Atlas evidence inspector did not materially outperform a competent conventional map + register on 4 frozen map tasks;
- the full-platform identity carried substantial maintenance/operations cost relative to demonstrated use;
- there was no demonstrated cross-domain need for a generic DH platform.

Those are value/baseline/cost findings, not novelty claims.

However, the old review sometimes used statements such as “nodegoat already provides X” or “standards already supply Y” as if overlap itself weakened the opportunity. That part is corrected.

**New interpretation:**

- Generic DH capability overlap shows that modelling, maps, timelines, networks, APIs and publication are **available alternatives/commodities**.
- It does **not** show that a domain-specific evidence system is valueless.
- The strategic question is whether the Atlas creates enough relative advantage for a specific job to change user behaviour despite the availability of those alternatives.
- Current project evidence says the strongest candidate advantage lies in the **evidence/review/coverage system**, not in bespoke visualization.

The old “continue + simplify” direction therefore survives, but for a cleaner reason.

## 2. Proven core — with evidence calibration

### Internally observed core

**OBSERVED — project evidence**

The following capabilities repeatedly did correctness/research work inside the project:

1. **Bounded proposition + required abstention**
   - R1 adversarial replay materially corrected temporal projection, target/context joins, external/territorial leakage and status overstatement.

2. **Unknown / inconclusive / unresearched / held remain distinct from absence**
   - This survived COV/R1 and is now explicit in the MVP.

3. **Law / practice / external participation / research coverage / geometry are separate**
   - Multiple experiments found that collapsing these dimensions produced unsafe inferences.

4. **Temporal applicability and spatial inference guards**
   - H2/COV/R1 repeatedly found exact-year and territorial overreach failure modes.

5. **Source-version provenance + source-family dependency**
   - R1 replay corrected dependency/provenance groupings even where the historical conclusion did not change.

6. **Tiered C0/C1/selective-C2 research architecture**
   - COV-003 returned TIERED SURVIVE; COV-004 returned METHOD SURVIVE.

7. **Immutable/as-of release discipline**
   - COV-004 time-split and R1 release work showed value in preserving historical evidence state rather than silently mutating it.

8. **Simple evidence register / ordinary map can carry the core**
   - COV-004 and R1.7 both support a small surface rather than a bespoke platform.

### What is not proven

**UNKNOWN / HYPOTHESIS**

The project does **not** currently know:
- whether external historians would adopt this workflow;
- whether expert reviewers complete real work faster or more accurately with it;
- whether researchers will switch from notes/spreadsheets/Zotero/nodegoat/Heurist/QGIS;
- whether institutions would maintain/contribute such structured evidence;
- whether public users want deep-time global exploration in this form;
- whether anybody would pay for it;
- whether a thin Atlas increases use of the corpus;
- whether the project has an efficient distribution/channel.

Therefore “proven core” must be read as **internally validated research/release core**, not product-market validation.

## 6. Relative value analysis

### A. Evidence audit / review

**Relative-advantage hypothesis**

> For a historian/editor reviewing a difficult slavery/coercion claim, when evidence varies in date, locus, terminology and source dependence, the Atlas packet may be chosen over ordinary notes/source lists because proposition + abstention + locus/extent + temporal guard + dependency family makes overreach easier to detect, despite extra structuring effort.

**Difference:** **OBSERVED internally.** R1 replay found material errors and dependency issues using these controls.

**Importance:** **SUPPORTED INFERENCE**, not direct external evidence. DH provenance/uncertainty research suggests transparency and uncertainty matter to historical interpretation, but this is adjacent evidence.

Relevant literature:
- L. Clarke, M. Orr, U. Hinrichs, “Provenance visualization: Tracing people, processes, and practices through a data-driven approach to provenance,” *Digital Scholarship in the Humanities* 38(3), 2023.
- G. Panagiotidou et al., “Communicating Uncertainty in Digital Humanities Visualization Research,” *IEEE TVCG* 29(1), 2023.

**Behavioural consequence:** **UNKNOWN.** No external reviewer has chosen this workflow over their own.

**Current state:** **DISCOVERY CANDIDATE.**

### B. Systematic coverage planning

**Relative-advantage hypothesis**

> For a research programme trying to build broad historical coverage without converting missing research into absence, the Atlas tiered register may be chosen over an ordinary spreadsheet/matrix because it keeps research state, target identity, evidence status and escalation separate while supporting bias-resistant sampling.

**Difference:** **OBSERVED internally.** COV-001/COV-003/COV-004 and R1 show this architecture working.

**Importance:** **HYPOTHESIS externally.**

**Behavioural consequence:** **UNKNOWN.**

**Current state:** **DISCOVERY CANDIDATE.**

### C. Cross-case historical comparison

**Relative-advantage hypothesis**

> For a comparative historian examining several unlike cases, the Atlas may be chosen over notes/table/GIS because it preserves temporal/spatial/claim non-equivalence while keeping exact evidence inspectable.

**Difference:** **PARTLY OBSERVED.** The corpus/method sometimes preserved distinctions better.

**Importance:** **SUPPORTED INFERENCE.** This is a real historical reasoning problem.

**Behavioural consequence:** **weak current evidence.**
- H2: corpus/method beat baseline materially in only 1/3 tasks.
- H2 thin view beat corpus/method in 0/3.
- COV-004 custom inspector beat conventional map/register in 0/4.
- DISC-01 found cross-anchor comparison friction, but a table could plausibly solve it.

**Current state:** **PARK as product feature; retain as experiment task.**

### D. Public exploration

**Relative-advantage hypothesis**

> For a public/educational user exploring slavery across deep time, the Atlas may offer a broader and more uncertainty-aware view than specialized databases.

**Difference:** plausible.

**Importance:** unknown.

**Behavioural consequence:** unknown.

**Current state:** **PARK.**

The project has no direct evidence that this is the user/job to optimize for.

### E. Generic DH platform

**Relative-advantage hypothesis**

There is currently no credible one.

nodegoat and Heurist already offer broad modelling/visualization/publication. More importantly, the Atlas has **no observed cross-domain user problem** that requires it to generalize.

**Current state:** **REJECT NOW.**

## 7. System-level opportunities

The corrected review exposes opportunities that are **not features**.

### Opportunity S1 — Audit-ready historical evidence package

**Concept:** make the project’s strongest unit the reusable evidence packet/protocol rather than a bespoke application.

Potential system:
- bounded proposition;
- abstention;
- evidence locus / inference extent;
- temporal precision;
- source-version + dependency family;
- review state;
- access/language limitation;
- immutable release identifier.

The package could live in JSON/CSV/Markdown and be consumed through ordinary tools.

**Why this matters:** generic platforms can provide storage/UI while the Atlas contributes domain-specific reasoning discipline.

**Novelty:** uncommon combination, not novel technology.

**Current state:** **DISCOVERY CANDIDATE.**

### Opportunity S2 — Bias-resistant systematic coverage protocol

**Concept:** C0/C1/C2 + frozen sampling + non-absence research state as a reusable way to build broad historical evidence corpora without allowing archive/demand density to dictate coverage.

**Why this matters:** this may be more valuable than any public map.

**Novelty:** uncommon workflow/system.

**Current state:** **DISCOVERY CANDIDATE.**

### Opportunity S3 — Benchmark/evaluation corpus for historical AI/DH systems

**Concept:** use adversarial cases to test whether tools hallucinate equivalence, false annual precision, false territorial inference, false source independence or false absence.

**Evidence for:** the project has many real corrected failure cases.

**Evidence against:** no external evaluator/user demand has been demonstrated.

**Current state:** **PARK / discovery candidate only if a concrete evaluator/research need appears.**

### Opportunity S4 — Interoperate instead of compete

Use WHG/QGIS/nodegoat/Heurist/Zotero where they already solve generic jobs, while preserving the Atlas evidence package as a portable layer.

**Strategic logic:** complementarity may matter more than standalone platform differentiation.

**Current state:** **SUPPORTED INFERENCE** as architecture direction; user value still needs testing.

## 8. Adoption / switching risks

### For historians/researchers

Likely friction:
- existing notes/Zotero/spreadsheet workflows are familiar;
- structured entry adds procedural overhead;
- learning domain fields may exceed perceived payoff;
- scholars may prefer narrative nuance to formalized fields;
- moving existing notes/data creates migration cost.

**Evidence status:** mostly **SUPPORTED INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS**; no user study yet.

### For DH/database users

nodegoat/Heurist already provide mature modelling and publication.

Switching to a custom Atlas platform would require a very large relative advantage.

This strengthens the case for portable evidence packages rather than platform replacement.

### For institutions

Trust, governance, maintenance ownership and long-term preservation may dominate feature quality.

The Atlas currently lacks independent historical review and an institutional operating model.

### For public users

The current R1 candidate has only 19 reviewed C1 rows and all 77 historical target geometries unresolved.

That is a weak adoption trigger for a public exploration product regardless of methodological quality.

## 9. Red-team results

### Attack S1 — “ordinary structured notes can do this”

Strong case:
a careful scholar can write proposition/abstention/provenance/uncertainty into a table or research note. No custom Atlas is required.

**Survival:** yes, but narrowed.

The opportunity is not “build an app”. The question is whether the **standardized packet + review protocol** measurably reduces missed caveats or review reconstruction effort.

That is testable.

### Attack S2 — “systematic coverage is expensive and may answer nobody’s question”

Strong case:
broad deep-time research can consume enormous effort; specialized resources will always be deeper; COV-002 already exposed maintenance burden.

**Survival:** partial.

The tiered architecture survived internally, but external value is unproven.

Do not scale coverage. Test whether a real curator/research programme values the coverage-state protocol.

### Attack cross-case view — “a table is enough”

Strong case:
H2, COV-004 and DISC-01 all point toward ordinary tables/registers reaching parity with bespoke views.

**Survival:** as a job, yes; as a dedicated feature, no current evidence.

Keep comparison as a test task, not a product commitment.

### Attack source-dependency visualization — “the graph is feature attraction”

Strong case:
the real value is detecting shared source families, which a grouped list or matrix may expose more clearly than a graph.

**Survival:** the **audit problem** survives; the visualization form does not.

#186 must not test “is a graph cool/useful?” It would need to test whether any representation improves reviewer performance over grouped provenance.

### Attack search/filter — “there is no current pain”

At 77 frozen targets and discrete anchor filtering, browser find/ordinary controls are credible.

No evidence shows users repeatedly fail retrieval tasks.

**Survival:** none as a priority experiment.

Search remains useful commodity functionality at larger scale, but that is not current evidence.

### EXP-1 — External expert audit crossover

**Hypothesis**

For expert historical reviewers assessing difficult slavery/coercion claims, the Atlas evidence packet reduces safety-critical missed caveats / false inferences and/or provenance reconstruction effort versus a competent ordinary source-note/table baseline.

**Why this matters**

If this fails, the strongest claimed value of the method may be mostly internal discipline rather than transferable user value.

If it succeeds, it validates the core without requiring a bespoke platform.

**Current evidence**

- internal adversarial replay caught material errors;
- dependency fields repeatedly corrected source-independence assumptions;
- external DH research supports provenance/uncertainty disclosure as potentially valuable.

**Falsifier**

Reject transferable audit advantage if a competent baseline reaches practical parity on:
- missed material caveats;
- false temporal/spatial/territorial inference;
- provenance recovery;
- reviewer effort;
and participants show no meaningful preference or reuse intent.

**Target sample**

Small purposive sample:
- historians working with slavery/coercion/dependency, or
- experienced DH/data historians accustomed to source criticism.

Aim for enough independent reviewers to reveal repeated task behavior; do not claim population estimates from a tiny sample.

**Method**

Crossover task on 2–3 frozen historical packets:
- Arm A: strongest ordinary source-note/table presentation;
- Arm B: Atlas structured evidence packet;
- counterbalance order;
- same underlying evidence;
- hidden preregistered inference traps;
- no bespoke UI required.

**Observe**

- material caveats detected;
- overclaims made;
- provenance/source-dependency correctly reconstructed;
- task completion time where measurable;
- questions reviewers ask;
- whether they would reuse the format in a real workflow;
- what fields they ignore or find burdensome.

**Encouraging evidence**

Repeated improvement on safety-critical reasoning or meaningful reduction in reconstruction effort across independent reviewers, plus concrete reuse intent.

**Disconfirming evidence**

Parity, slower/burdensome structured review with no correctness gain, or experts preferring ordinary notes while preserving the same safeguards.

**Decision**

- strong repeated advantage → PROMOTION CANDIDATE for the **method/package**, not automatically a product feature;
- mixed/narrow advantage → revise packet and/or segment;
- parity → shrink toward benchmark/method artifacts and stop feature discovery.

### Deferred experiments

Coverage-planning and portability experiments are **not** queued yet.

Run them only if EXP-1 survives or if a concrete external curator/institutional need appears.

## 12. Changes to prior conclusions

### Conclusions that remain valid

- **Full bespoke platform is not the default direction.**
  - survives because of baseline parity, weak interface value and maintenance burden, not competitor existence.

- **Generic DH platform identity is rejected.**
  - survives because there is no evidenced cross-domain job and it is far from the core; nodegoat/Heurist are strong alternatives, not the sole reason.

- **Map is subordinate to evidence.**
  - survives.

- **Method/corpus contains the strongest demonstrated contribution.**
  - survives and is strengthened.

- **Timeline feature rejection.**
  - survives because of task value / false-precision logic.

### Conclusions weakened

- **“Thin atlas” as the preferred product identity.**
  - weakened further.
  - It is a useful inspection/publication surface, but H2/COV-004 show little evidence that custom visual interaction adds strategic value.

- **Competitor overlap as evidence against project value.**
  - materially weakened.
  - overlap now informs alternative strength and switching costs.

- **“Uniqueness” as a decision criterion.**
  - downgraded. Relative advantage matters more.

### Conclusions reframed rather than reversed

- **nodegoat/Heurist overlap**
  - old reading: weakens distinctiveness.
  - corrected reading: makes generic platform capability commodity and raises the relative-advantage threshold, while increasing the attractiveness of interoperability.

- **Seshat/global historical database precedent**
  - not evidence that another global corpus is useless;
  - evidence that systematic historical data can be organized/released at scale, while Atlas must justify its domain-specific contribution.

### Newly exposed assumptions

1. external historians care enough about structured abstention/provenance to change workflow;
2. the method’s internal error prevention transfers to independent reviewers;
3. a portable evidence package can capture value without a custom platform;
4. broad systematic coverage has external planning/reference value;
5. the project can reach/recruit the relevant expert segment;
6. trust from independent review may be more important than feature work.

## 13. Final wide-lens view

### WHAT WE KNOW

- The research/release method catches real errors inside this project.
- Generic maps, timelines, networks, search, APIs and humanities data platforms already exist.
- Specialized slavery resources are strong within their own units/domains.
- Bespoke Atlas UI has repeatedly failed to show material advantage over strong ordinary map/register/table baselines.
- The technical MVP can expose the evidence core without rebuilding the platform.
- External user adoption is not proven.

### WHAT WE THINK

- The most plausible transferable value is **audit-ready evidence discipline** and **bias-resistant systematic coverage**, not UI novelty.
- Interoperability with existing tools is strategically more coherent than competing with mature generic DH platforms.
- The current map/register should remain boring until a user task proves otherwise.

### WHAT WE DO NOT KNOW

- whether external experts actually review better with the Atlas packet;
- whether the extra structure is worth its procedural cost;
- whether research programmes value the coverage protocol enough to adopt it;
- whether public exploration is a meaningful target segment;
- whether independent review changes trust/adoption.

### WHAT WOULD MOST CHANGE OUR MIND

Direct behavioural evidence from independent historians/reviewers showing either:
- repeated practical advantage from the structured packet, or
- parity/burden versus ordinary research notes.

That evidence matters more than another competitor scan or another UI prototype.

### WHAT WE SHOULD TEST NEXT

**One external expert audit crossover experiment.**

Do not start search, graph, comparison-view or other production-oriented discovery until that higher-level value uncertainty is reduced.
