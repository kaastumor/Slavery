# R1 — Mandatory Midpoint Stop Review

**Issue:** #168
**Parent programme:** #159
**Review date:** 2026-09-24
**Evidence boundary:** R1.4 tranche 01 + R1.5 tranche 02; 16 researched C1 rows total
**Decision:** **CONTINUE ONCE — smallest balance-closing tranche, then stop subject expansion and enter release QC unless the tranche itself creates a new stop condition**

## 1. Are bounded claims surviving adversarial review?

**Yes, but only with the review controls active.**

Tranche 01 was intentionally stress-heavy:
- 10/10 replayed;
- four material release-safety corrections;
- two C2 packets.

Tranche 02 used more ordinary validated frames:
- 6/6 replayed;
- no post-synthesis semantic release correction;
- one C2 packet;
- two source-dependency/provenance normalizations.

This is enough to say Core Contract v1 is operationally useful. It is not enough to weaken replay.

Core Contract §19 remains active at 100% C1 replay because tranche 01 crossed its material-error trigger.

## 2. Are language/access gaps dominating results?

**No, but they are a material release limitation.**

Across the first 16 rows:
- 2 rows have high coverage confidence;
- 2 moderate-high;
- 7 moderate;
- 4 limited/moderate variants;
- 1 limited.

The strongest access/language constraints occur in contexts such as Inuit, Cai, Shaolin, al-Azhar and early Mali.

Several other rows rely on specialist translations/interpretations of Egyptian, Arabic, Chinese, Indic, Punic, Greek or epigraphic source material rather than independent retranslation by this project.

That is acceptable for a bounded candidate research release if:
- it remains explicit;
- inconclusive never becomes absence;
- publication/review labels do not imply exhaustive multilingual review.

It argues **against** expanding toward the 36-row ceiling merely for coverage volume.

## 3. Is source recovery/reconstruction working?

**Yes.**

All 16 final rows pass the Core Contract source-recovery guard:
- recoverable source/version references;
- decisive-source identification;
- claim-fitness state;
- locator/role notes where material.

Replay has also demonstrated that dependency tracking is doing real work:
- tranche 01 caught source-version/source-fitness issues;
- tranche 02 normalized derivative source families for Lazica and Nālandā;
- Mali C2 prevented multiple modern publications from masquerading as independent attestations when they share Ibn Khaldun/later oral source bases.

Remaining limitation: exact-source recovery can still be costly and uneven across deep-history/non-English cases. That is a reason to stop smaller, not to lower the source bar.

## 4. Are we finding genuinely useful cross-history structure?

**Yes.**

The same controls have repeatedly prevented different error classes across unrelated histories:
- selected-year truth vs nearby evidence;
- target evidence vs generic context;
- external/network participation vs territorial practice;
- event/captivity/sacrifice vs slave status;
- evidence locus vs inference extent;
- source publication count vs evidentiary independence;
- aggregate frame vs constituent territorial condition.

These are recurring release-safety structures, not one-region conventions.

## 5. Is the research/review burden proportionate?

**For one small closing tranche, yes. For automatic expansion toward 36, no evidence supports it.**

Observed:
- 16 C1 rows researched;
- 3 C2 packets completed;
- all 16 replayed because the §19 trigger is active.

Tranche 02 shows ordinary rows can be handled without repeated semantic rework, but replay and source recovery remain substantive labour.

R1 should therefore buy only the smallest remaining research needed to satisfy its declared release frame.

Do not aim for the 36-row ceiling.

The minimum closure therefore needs exactly three additional polity rows.
