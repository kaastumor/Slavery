## Pandya Empire — 1300 CE

### Attack

- Is Thanjavur evidence being absorbed into a political frame without synchronizing individual records to jurisdiction/date?
- Are devotional or service terms being treated as literal slavery?

### Result

**C2 completed; final outcome remains inconclusive.**

The deeper check confirms:

The legacy positive is therefore not grandfathered.

## Chimú Empire — 1300 CE

### Attack

- Is labor service being relabelled as slavery?
- Does archaeological state-control language prove coercion or only administration?
- Is an Inka-style mita analogy doing the evidentiary work?
- Does evidence from one province/site support every Chimú community?
- Does later household archaeology undermine the older forced-labor model?
- Is an indexed source summary being treated as inspected full text?

### Result

**Survives as classified / bounded-supported for state labor service/corvée only.**

The positive claim is narrower than slavery:
- Keatinge & Conrad describe a Chimú political economy with state control over labor resources and an extractive flow of tribute and labor service;
- Mackey places a major expansion phase about 1300 and identifies administrative oversight of corvée labor;
- Billman et al. later reject the strongest forced-labor-colony reading at Cerro la Virgen and show household autonomy, which blocks uniform empire-wide coercion.

Replay therefore:
- keeps the corvée/labor-service classification;
- rejects chattel-slavery/permanent-status inference;
- rejects universal household/province application;
- downgrades the eHRAF-rendered Mackey chapter to `usable_with_limitation` because the full chapter itself was not directly inspected in this run.
