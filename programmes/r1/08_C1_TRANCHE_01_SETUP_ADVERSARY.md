# R1.4 — Tranche 01 Setup Adversary

**Issue:** #163  
**Disposition:** **PROCEED**

## Attack 1 — stress selection can manufacture a high failure rate

Yes.

The tranche deliberately over-samples frame-limited and non-polity cases.

**Control:** no failure/success percentage from tranche 01 may be generalized to the full R1 queue. The tranche tests contract robustness, not prevalence or expected release yield.

## Attack 2 — legacy rows can leak old conclusions into “fresh” review

**Control:** fresh Core Contract synthesis is drafted first. Legacy conclusion comparison happens only afterwards. Old sources may be reused but their claim-fitness is reassessed.

## Attack 3 — source abundance can make famous targets look stronger

**Control:** source count is never a historical strength metric. The record captures independence and proposition fitness. One strong specialist synthesis may safely support a bounded claim; ten derivative citations do not create prevalence.

## Attack 4 — English/web-accessible research can create fake negatives

**Control:** every row records language/access limitation and research coverage confidence. Inconclusive means the bounded reviewed packet did not establish the proposition, not that the historical literature has been exhausted.

## Attack 5 — “Portuguese Colonies” is an aggregate that invites false territorial synthesis

**Control:** the R1.3 frame limitation is binding. No single territorial-practice claim may attach to the aggregate. C1 may only describe explicitly bounded constituent/jurisdictional evidence or conclude that the aggregate frame is unsafe and trigger C2/hold.

## Attack 6 — Inuit Arctic communities invite false cultural uniformity

**Control:** community evidence must remain localized/people-specific. No circumpolar institution may be inferred from one community or ethnographic source. If the frame cannot safely support a compact proposition, escalate/hold.

## Attack 7 — Incense Route can collapse movement into territory

**Control:** this is a network frame. Evidence may support movement/trade of enslaved people through particular nodes/corridors; it never colors all traversed territory as territorial practice.

## Attack 8 — Teotihuacan and Cahokia can turn coercion/sacrifice into slave status

**Control:** violence, hierarchy, captivity or sacrifice do not mechanically establish slavery. Category interpretation requires specialist support; unresolved status becomes disputed/inconclusive or C2.

## Attack 9 — al-Azhar can inherit Cairo/Fatimid slavery by proximity

**Control:** urban/imperial context is not institutional practice. Institution-specific claims require institution-specific evidence.

## Attack 10 — Shaolin can inherit generic monastery/estate dependency

**Control:** broader Chinese Buddhist institutional labor is contextual unless evidence specifically supports Shaolin around the target period.

## Attack 11 — Middle Kingdom legal/household terminology can be normalized too aggressively

**Control:** preserve Egyptian terms/source categories and use Egyptological specialist interpretation. Do not map every dependent worker or captive to slave status.

## Attack 12 — Carthage can look easy because the legacy row was positive

**Control:** re-review from scratch under Core v1. Agricultural labor, household slavery, war captives and legal status remain proposition-specific; no population share or universal labor regime is inferred.

## Attack 13 — “strongest bounded proposition” can become rhetorical optimism

**Control:** the required abstention is equally mandatory and source-fit is checked against the exact wording. If the proposition cannot be made safe without caveat stacking, downgrade or C2.

## Attack 14 — tranche can drift into app work

**Control:** no UI/backend/schema changes are in scope. Any workflow friction is logged only as a future friction ticket.

## Verdict

**PROCEED.**

The tranche is intentionally hard enough to expose defects, but its role is calibration only. No subject research began before this protocol/selection/adversary freeze.
