## Pandya

The fresh packet confirms strong thirteenth-century slavery/dependency evidence in medieval Tamil South India.

The problem is target fit.

Orr's synthesis explicitly notes that almost all surviving twelfth–thirteenth-century inscriptions mentioning slaves are concentrated in present-day Thanjavur district. The fresh packet has not yet synchronized those decisive slave records to the frozen Pandya polity at exactly 1300.

Therefore the old positive outcome cannot be grandfathered.

Because this is a material legacy-positive reversal caused by **spatial/generalization risk**, Pandya earns C2 under Core Contract v1.

## Chimú

The fresh result does not attempt to revive a generic slavery claim.

It identifies a different and narrower project category:
- state labor service / corvée;
- state control over labor resources;
- tribute/labor extraction.

Keatinge & Conrad directly describe the Chimú political-economic model in those terms, while Mackey places provincial corvée administration across a period containing the 1300 anchor.

The result is deliberately bounded:
- no chattel-slavery label;
- no permanent personal-status inference;
- no claim that every household or province experienced identical coercion;
- later household archaeology at Cerro la Virgen is retained as counterevidence to the strongest forced-labor-colony model.

The legacy inconclusive outcome can therefore change under Core v1 because the fresh claim is narrower, explicitly typed, and better source-fitted.

## Method result

Legacy direction is not sticky in either direction.

A prior positive can fall when target fit fails; a prior inconclusive can become bounded-supported when a narrower coercion category is directly evidenced.
