# R1 — Setup Adversary

**Issue:** #159  
**Historical R1 target research started:** no  
**Disposition:** **PROCEED AFTER REPAIRS BELOW**

This review attacks the programme design before Core Contract canonization or release research.

---

# Attack 1 — “Core hardening” can become endless ontology polishing

**Severity: critical.**

The phrase “rock hard core” can rationalize indefinite refinement.

### Repair

Core Contract v1 may include only:
- rules directly supported by a documented project failure/success;
- fields required by R1 release/QC;
- minimum invariants needed to reconstruct claims/releases.

Every proposed rule must cite its originating fixture/decision.

No “future-proof” abstraction.

R1.1 gets a hard exit:
> if a proposed field/rule has no concrete fixture or consumer, defer it.

**Disposition: repaired / proceed.**

---

# Attack 2 — the 120/36/12 ceilings may become quotas psychologically

**Severity: high.**

Teams fill declared capacities.

### Repair

- ceilings must be displayed as `MAX`, never targets;
- no progress dashboard based on percentage of ceiling;
- release may end early;
- no minimum C0 count;
- balance constraints apply only to whatever scope is actually claimed.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 3 — “systematic” can still mean systematic within a biased frame

**Severity: critical.**

Cliopatria/polity frames privilege documented political entities.

Non-polity candidate pools can also be curator-biased.

### Repair

R1 must publish its **target-frame coverage statement**:
- what candidate universes were used;
- what target classes are excluded;
- which languages/reference infrastructures bias discoverability;
- what “systematic” means operationally.

The release title/scope must not say “all known history” unless the target frame actually supports that.

**Disposition: proceed with disclosure requirement.**

---

# Attack 4 — geographic balance can become false equivalence

**Severity: critical.**

Equal sample slots do not imply equal historical prevalence or equal evidentiary confidence.

### Repair

Sampling-balance metadata must never enter slavery/intensity interpretation.

Geographic/time balance exists only to resist research-selection bias.

Map legends and release docs must state that sampling density is not practice intensity.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 5 — first real release might simply recycle COV rows and declare success

**Severity: high.**

That would test packaging rather than research programme operation.

### Repair

Existing COV rows may be promoted only after Core Contract re-review.

R1 C1 must contain both:
- re-reviewed legacy experimental material; and
- genuinely new frozen R1 research.

At least **40% of final C1** must be newly researched under R1.

At least **25%** may come from legacy material to test migration/review mechanics.

If final C1 is too small to satisfy both meaningfully, disclose that and do not claim programme-scale validation.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 6 — 25% adversarial replay is too weak after a 1/6 false negative

**Severity: critical.**

A 16.7% observed failure in a tiny sample does not justify light review.

### Repair

For R1 candidate release:
- **50% of C1** receives blinded/adversarial replay;
- **100% of C2**;
- **100% of single-decisive-source positive claims**;
- every source-quality `usable_with_limitation` positive claim.

If the material-error rate in the first half of replayed C1 exceeds **10%**, expand replay to **100% of C1** before release.

Any repeated error class becomes a release-wide QC scan.

**Disposition: repaired / proceed.**

---

# Attack 7 — same-model adversarial replay is still not independence

**Severity: high.**

True.

### Repair

Keep two labels:
- `internally_adversarially_reviewed`;
- `independently_reviewed`.

Never conflate them.

R1 can create a candidate research release without human independence.

Public/canonical promotion must explicitly state whether independent review occurred.

**Disposition: proceed with honest status.**

---

# Attack 8 — source-quality labels can give fake certainty

**Severity: high.**

A source can be specialist and still wrong or derivative.

### Repair

Source-quality state applies to **fitness for the claim**, not prestige of venue.

It must be separate from:
- source independence;
- evidentiary direction;
- access completeness.

A `production_grade` source relation means:
> suitable as evidence for this bounded claim under current review,
not:
> historically true beyond dispute.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 9 — language/access bias remains structurally dangerous

**Severity: critical.**

English/digitized scholarship can create false researched-inconclusive states.

### Repair

Every C1 includes:
- languages actually searched/used where known;
- access limitation;
- whether target-language/regional scholarship was inaccessible or unsearched;
- a `coverage_confidence` distinct from slavery outcome.

`researched_inconclusive` must not imply literature exhaustion.

If a target has material known language/access gaps, public wording must say so.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 10 — R1 may become a software project again

**Severity: critical.**

A “release surface” can pull the team toward web/database work.

### Repair

Atlas implementation budget is fixed:
- reuse existing static/dependency-light patterns first;
- no new backend;
- no DB migration;
- no new package stack;
- no frontend framework change.

Any exception requires a separately decision-logged friction ticket.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 11 — feature tickets can be gamed by counting similar annoyances

**Severity: medium-high.**

### Repair

Three recurrences must be:
- across at least two different historical research tasks or release stages;
- materially similar;
- and independently costly/error-prone.

Cosmetic preference does not count.

Serious one-off correctness failure can qualify immediately.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 12 — independent-review gate could stall the project indefinitely

**Severity: high.**

### Repair

Separate:
- **candidate research release**;
- **public/canonical release**.

R1 is allowed to complete at candidate status if all internal gates pass.

Lack of independent review is then an unresolved publication blocker, not a reason to keep researching endlessly.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 13 — release balancing rules are currently underspecified

**Severity: critical.**

“Five time bands, six regions” without a frozen sampling design is manipulable.

### Repair

R1.2 must preregister:
- exact temporal bands;
- exact neutral geographic sampling frame;
- source/version used to enumerate polity candidates;
- neutral source/version used for non-polity candidate pools;
- deterministic selectors;
- legacy/new allocation;
- C1 selection before slavery research.

No target substitution after subject evidence is inspected.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 14 — contemporary history creates a different legal/ethical problem

**Severity: high.**

A 3000 BCE→present claim can drag modern persons/organizations into sensitive data.

### Repair

R1 target frame is limited to **pre-1900 historical targets** unless a later target is institution/system-level and separately reviewed.

This is an R1 scope limit, not a permanent project exclusion.

Do not mix modern person-level forced-labour data into R1.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 15 — C0 could become a giant useless registry

**Severity: high.**

### Repair

C0 exists only where it supports:
- a declared sampling frame;
- identity/time QA;
- research-state visibility;
- future routing.

No free-form “add interesting places” C0 growth.

C0 outside the frozen release frame is out of scope.

**Disposition: proceed.**

No graph database.

A future graph feature must be earned by repeated failure of the flat relation.

**Disposition: proceed.**

# Attack 17 — the candidate release may still overstate “global”

**Severity: critical.**

### Repair

Release naming is conditional.

Use “global/deep-time sample release” only if balance gates pass.

Otherwise name the narrower scope honestly.

No marketing language outruns frame coverage.

**Disposition: proceed.**

# Attack 18 — Atlas usefulness could be under-tested if kept too boring

**Severity: medium.**

The sponsor wants space for slow feature discovery.

### Repair

Keep the boring Atlas as default, but instrument the research/release process with friction tickets.

Do not prohibit new features forever.

Require them to emerge from:
- scale;
- repeated navigation/review pain;
- provenance failure;
- comparison failure;
- public-comprehension failure.

Then test the smallest intervention adversarially.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 19 — the programme could become self-justifying

**Severity: critical.**

Once real work begins, sunk-cost pressure grows.

### Repair

R1 has two explicit stop reviews:

## Midpoint stop review
After roughly half the planned C1 capacity or earlier if research burden becomes disproportionate.

Ask:
- are bounded claims surviving QC?
- are language/access gaps dominating?
- is new knowledge/reuse emerging?
- are friction tickets meaningful or manufactured?
- should the release stop smaller?

## Final release review
No continuation by default.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Attack 20 — “rock hard” is impossible

**Severity: important conceptual correction.**

Historical interpretation remains revisable.

### Repair

Define “rock hard” operationally:

> not immutable truth, but a core whose uncertainty, provenance, dependencies, revision history and failure states are explicit enough that later correction does not corrupt the record.

This is the programme's durability target.

**Disposition: proceed.**

---

# Setup-adversary verdict

**PROCEED.**

But R1 is narrower after attack:

- Core Contract is empirical, not aspirational;
- pre-1900 by default;
- source/language limitations are first-class;
- 120/36/12 are ceilings;
- 50% C1 internal adversarial replay minimum;
- candidate release is distinct from independently/publicly reviewed release;
- no new backend/platform work;
- C0 only inside a frozen systematic frame;
- exact release sample must be preregistered before subject research;
- midpoint stop review is mandatory;
- Atlas features are discovered from real friction, not roadmap imagination.

**Historical target research remains blocked until Core Contract v1 is hardened and the R1.2 target frame is frozen.**
